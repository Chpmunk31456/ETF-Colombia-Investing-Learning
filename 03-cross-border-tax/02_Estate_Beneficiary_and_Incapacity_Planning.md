# Lesson 20 — Estate, Beneficiary, and Incapacity Planning for Cross-Border Assets

## Learning Objective

Understand the documentation and planning questions that arise when a person owns bank accounts, brokerage accounts, ETFs, CDTs, pensions, and real estate across the United States and Colombia.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

It does not create:

- A will
- A trust
- A power of attorney
- A beneficiary designation
- A succession plan
- A tax opinion
- A legal conclusion

Cross-border estate planning should be reviewed by qualified professionals familiar with the relevant jurisdictions.

## Why Estate Planning Is Part of Investing

An investment plan is incomplete when no one knows:

- What assets exist
- Where accounts are held
- Who should inherit them
- Who can act during incapacity
- How beneficiaries will access funds
- Which documents control each asset
- What taxes or legal proceedings may apply
- Where original documents are stored

Good investment management includes an orderly transfer plan.

## Separate Three Different Events

Planning should distinguish among:

### Temporary incapacity

The investor is alive but temporarily unable to manage finances.

### Permanent incapacity

The investor is alive but may no longer be able to make financial or legal decisions.

### Death

Ownership, beneficiary, succession, probate, tax, and account-access rules may apply.

A document designed for one event may not solve the others.

For each account, record:

- Institution

Do not store full passwords in the asset inventory.

## Ownership Form

Ownership structure may affect what happens after incapacity or death.

Possible forms include:

- Sole ownership
- Joint ownership
- Ownership with survivorship rights
- Marital ownership
- Company ownership
- Trust ownership
- Beneficiary-designated account
- Pension beneficiary arrangement

The name shown on an account is not always enough to determine succession treatment.

## Beneficiary Designations

Some financial products allow a named beneficiary.

Review:

- Primary beneficiary
- Contingent beneficiary
- Percentage allocation
- Identification information
- Contact information
- Relationship
- Last update date
- Required supporting documents

A beneficiary designation may operate differently from a will.

Professional review should identify which document controls each asset.

## Primary and Contingent Beneficiaries

### Primary beneficiary

The first person or entity designated to receive the asset.

### Contingent beneficiary

The person or entity designated if the primary beneficiary cannot receive it.

Do not leave contingent-beneficiary fields incomplete without considering the consequences.

## Minor Beneficiaries

A minor may be unable to receive or control financial assets directly.

Questions for legal counsel include:

- Who will manage the assets?
- Is a guardian required?
- Is a trust or similar arrangement appropriate?
- At what age should control transfer?
- Who can authorize medical, educational, or living expenses?
- What happens if the designated adult cannot serve?

Do not assume that naming a minor directly creates an efficient transfer.

## Incapacity Planning

An incapacity plan may need to address authority over:

- Bank accounts
- Brokerage accounts
- Property
- Insurance
- Taxes
- Healthcare expenses
- Business interests
- Debt payments
- Legal proceedings
- Digital records

Ask legal counsel which documents are recognized in each country.

## Financial Power of Attorney

A financial power of attorney may authorize another person to act for the investor.

Questions include:

- In which country was it created?
- Is it valid where the asset is located?
- Does the institution accept it?
- Is translation required?
- Is notarization required?
- Is apostille or legalization required?
- Does it remain effective during incapacity?
- Can the agent manage investments?
- Can the agent sell property?
- Can the agent access tax records?
- Can the agent make gifts?
- When does authority begin?
- When does authority end?

Do not assume one document will be accepted by every institution.

## Healthcare and Personal Decisions

Financial planning should be coordinated with:

- Healthcare decision documents
- Emergency contacts
- Medical information
- Insurance information
- Preferred healthcare facilities
- Family notification instructions

Do not place sensitive medical details in a public repository.

## Wills

A will may address:

- Distribution of property
- Executor or personal representative
- Guardianship preferences
- Specific gifts
- Residual estate
- Funeral preferences
- Administrative powers

Cross-border questions include:

- Which country's law applies?
- Is more than one will appropriate?
- Could one will revoke another?
- Where is the original stored?
- Is the will recognized in both countries?
- Are translations required?
- Are formalities valid?
- How are jointly owned assets treated?

Separate legal advice should be obtained before creating multiple wills.

## Colombian Real Estate

For Colombian property, identify:

- Registered owner
- Matrícula inmobiliaria
- Acquisition date
- Purchase cost
- Mortgage
- Administration obligations
- Property-tax status
- Occupancy
- Lease
- Beneficiary or succession plan
- Original deed location
- Current Certificate of Tradition and Freedom
- Property manager or local contact

## Distressed Property

For distressed or auction property, also document:

- Court or authority
- Case number
- Registration status
- Possession status
- Outstanding legal proceedings
- Attorney
- Amount committed
- Repair obligations
- Unpaid taxes or administration
- Exit plan

A property under litigation or without possession may be substantially more difficult to transfer or administer.

## US Brokerage Accounts

For each US brokerage account, record:

- Broker
- Legal entity
- Account ownership
- Beneficiary designation
- Transfer-on-death status, if applicable
- Joint owner
- Trusted contact
- Account jurisdiction
- Holdings
- Cost-basis records
- Tax documents
- Customer-service contact
- Procedures after incapacity or death

Institutional rules should be verified directly.

## CDTs

For each CDT, record:

- Issuing institution
- Principal
- Opening date
- Maturity date
- Interest rate
- Renewal instructions
- Beneficiary or succession treatment
- Certificate location
- Account receiving maturity proceeds
- Contact person
- Tax withholding records

A maturity instruction should not remain dependent on one person remembering the date.

## Pension and Social Security Income

Record:

- Paying institution
- Beneficiary or survivor rules
- Payment account
- Contact information
- Identification numbers
- Required notification procedure
- Survivor documentation
- Tax documents

Do not assume that all recurring payments continue automatically after death.

## Digital Access

Prepare a secure digital-access plan covering:

- Email
- Password manager
- Bank portals
- Brokerage portals
- Cloud storage
- Tax records
- Government portals
- Property records
- Mobile phone
- MFA recovery methods
- Cryptocurrency or digital wallets
- Domain names
- Business systems

## Password Security

Do not place passwords, private keys, or recovery phrases in:

- Public repositories
- Unencrypted spreadsheets
- Email drafts
- Printed documents stored openly
- Shared notes without access controls

Use:

- Password manager
- Encrypted emergency-access process
- Sealed physical instructions
- Documented recovery procedure
- Trusted-person access protocol

## Trusted Contact

A trusted contact is not necessarily authorized to transact.

The trusted contact may help an institution respond to:

- Suspected fraud
- Cognitive decline
- Inability to reach the account holder
- Emergency circumstances

Record separately:

- Trusted contact
- Joint owner
- Beneficiary
- Power-of-attorney agent
- Executor
- Property manager

These roles are not interchangeable.

## Executor or Personal Representative

Document:

- Primary person
- Alternate person
- Country of residence
- Contact details
- Ability to travel
- Language ability
- Familiarity with Colombia
- Familiarity with US institutions
- Willingness to serve
- Access to professional advisers

A person living far from the assets may face practical difficulties.

## Annual Beneficiary Review

- After divorce

- After moving countries

- After purchasing property
- After major family changes
- After creating or changing a will

## Warning Signs

Planning is incomplete when:

- Accounts are unknown to family
- No one can access essential records
- Beneficiary designations are outdated
- A deceased person remains named
- Minor beneficiaries are named without management planning
- Property records are missing
- Passwords exist only in one person's memory
- Original wills cannot be located
- US and Colombian documents conflict
- No one can pay bills during incapacity
- An executor cannot realistically act in both countries
- Cross-border tax consequences have not been reviewed

## Beginner Rules

1. Inventory every asset and debt.
2. Separate beneficiary, ownership, and will questions.
3. Plan for incapacity as well as death.
4. Do not store passwords in the investment repository.
5. Review beneficiary designations annually.
6. Identify primary and alternate decision-makers.
7. Organize property and tax records.
8. Confirm whether documents are accepted in each country.
9. Coordinate legal and tax advice.
10. Ensure trusted people know where instructions are stored.
