# Lesson 30 — Finding and Screening Colombian Distressed Property Opportunities

## Learning Objective

Learn how to locate, record, verify, prioritize, and reject Colombian judicial-auction, DIAN-auction, bank-owned, adjudicated, and other distressed-property opportunities before committing substantial research time or money.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

Auction rules, filing requirements, deposit amounts, deadlines, and legal procedures can change and must be verified through the current official notice and qualified Colombian legal counsel.

## Core Principle

Finding a discounted advertisement is not the same as finding an investable property.

A viable opportunity requires:

- Verifiable official source
- Identifiable property
- Clear legal process
- Current title information
- Understandable occupancy status
- Realistic market value
- Sufficient margin of safety
- Available funding
- Acceptable worst-case loss

## Opportunity Categories

Classify every lead as one of the following:

- Judicial auction
- DIAN auction
- Bank-adjudicated property
- Property received in payment
- Insolvency-related sale
- Estate or succession sale
- Occupied distressed sale
- Unfinished construction
- Assignment of contractual rights
- Assignment of litigated rights
- Ordinary discounted sale
- Unverified advertisement

Do not group all discounted properties under one label.

## Official Versus Promotional Source

### Official source

Examples may include:

- Court notice
- Official judicial platform
- DIAN auction publication
- Bank-owned-property portal
- Registered financial institution
- Notarial or official case documentation

### Promotional source

Examples may include:

- Real-estate portal
- Social-media post
- WhatsApp message
- Broker advertisement
- Auction aggregator
- Investment promoter

A promotional source may identify a lead, but the official process must be verified independently.

## Lead Intake Rule

No lead should enter detailed analysis until the following minimum information is available:

- Property location
- Sale type
- Official authority or legal seller
- Case number or official reference
- Matrícula inmobiliaria where available
- Appraised or asking value
- Minimum bid or requested price
- Auction or closing date
- Occupancy status
- Official source
- Contact information

## Initial Rejection Rules

Reject immediately when:

- No official authority can be identified
- Case number cannot be verified
- Matrícula is unavailable without explanation
- Payment is requested before documents are reviewed
- Payment is requested to an unrelated individual
- Guaranteed profit is promised
- Property address is intentionally concealed
- Seller discourages attorney review
- Title or possession questions are dismissed
- Advertisement presents litigated rights as full ownership
- Required deposit must be paid outside the official process
- Documentation appears altered or inconsistent

## Geographic Screening

Define approved geographic areas before searching.

Possible factors include:

- Distance from home
- Travel time
- Public transportation
- Security
- Rental demand
- Resale liquidity
- Medical access
- Schools
- Employment centers
- Flood or landslide exposure
- Administration fees
- Neighborhood development
- Ability to inspect and manage the property

## Geographic Search Zones

Create three groups:

### Primary zone

Areas where the investor has strong local familiarity and can manage the property easily.

### Secondary zone

Areas with acceptable access but requiring more research.

### Excluded zone

Areas rejected because of:

- Security risk
- Distance
- Weak resale demand
- High vacancy
- Environmental exposure
- Poor access
- Limited professional support
- Management burden

## Property-Type Screening

Define acceptable property types.

Possible categories include:

- Apartment
- House
- Townhouse
- Lot
- Commercial space
- Office
- Warehouse
- Parking space
- Storage unit
- Rural property
- Unfinished property

A beginner may wish to exclude complex categories such as:

- Rural title
- Informal subdivision
- Commercial tenant disputes
- Unfinished developments
- Mixed-use title
- Multiple undivided owners
- Litigated rights
- Properties without physical access

## Intended Use

Every lead should have a proposed use:

- Personal residence
- Long-term rental
- Renovate and resell
- Hold for appreciation
- Family use
- Land development
- Commercial operation

Reject leads that do not fit a defined objective.

## Price Screening

Record:

- Official appraisal
- Minimum bid
- Asking price
- Estimated market value
- Estimated occupied value
- Estimated vacant value
- Estimated repaired value
- Estimated quick-sale value

Do not use the highest possible valuation.

## Discount Calculation

Apparent discount equals:

Estimated market value
minus proposed purchase price

Apparent discount percentage equals:

Apparent discount
divided by estimated market value

This is not the true margin because it ignores:

- Legal costs
- Possession
- Repairs
- Debts
- Registration
- Financing
- Carrying costs
- Sale costs
- Taxes

## Adjusted Discount

Adjusted discount should be calculated after estimated costs.

Adjusted purchase basis equals:

Proposed purchase price
plus legal costs
plus possession costs
plus outstanding obligations
plus repairs
plus registration and taxes
plus carrying costs

Adjusted discount equals:

Conservative market value
minus adjusted purchase basis

## Minimum Margin Rule

Establish a minimum required margin before detailed review.

The margin should reflect:

- Legal uncertainty
- Occupancy
- Inspection limitations
- Repair uncertainty
- Financing risk
- Market liquidity
- Holding period
- Selling costs

A thin apparent discount may disappear after one unexpected problem.

## Occupancy Categories

Classify occupancy as:

- Vacant and verified
- Owner occupied
- Tenant occupied
- Family occupied
- Unauthorized occupant
- Occupancy disputed
- Unknown
- Access denied

Unknown occupancy should receive a high-risk classification.

## Inspection Categories

Classify inspection access as:

- Full interior inspection
- Limited interior inspection
- Exterior only
- Photographs only
- Prior appraisal only
- No inspection
- Occupied and inaccessible

The less inspection access available, the larger the contingency should be.

## Title Complexity Categories

### Low complexity

- One clear registered owner
- No unresolved liens
- Standard property description
- No occupancy dispute

### Moderate complexity

- Mortgage payoff
- Multiple owners
- Tenant occupancy
- Minor registry discrepancy
- Standard judicial cancellation process

### High complexity

- Succession incomplete
- Litigated ownership
- Undivided rights
- Family-property restrictions
- Unclear possession
- Multiple embargoes
- Assignment of rights
- Registry and cadastral conflict

## Funding Screening

Before detailed legal review, identify:

- Required deposit
- Deposit deadline
- Remaining payment deadline
- Available cash
- Financing availability
- Currency conversion
- Transfer time
- Required reserves
- Maximum total capital available

Reject opportunities that cannot be funded within the official deadline.

## Maximum Capital Exposure

Define:

- Maximum deposit
- Maximum winning bid
- Maximum total project cost
- Maximum renovation budget
- Maximum legal budget
- Maximum percentage of investable assets
- Minimum emergency reserve after purchase

## Preliminary Rental Screening

For rental use, estimate:

- Conservative monthly rent
- Administration
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Vacancy
- Maintenance
- Management
- Financing
- Net operating income

Reject leads that require unrealistic rent to produce acceptable cash flow.

## Preliminary Resale Screening

For resale, estimate:

- Conservative completed value
- Renovation duration
- Possession delay
- Broker commission
- Taxes
- Legal costs
- Carrying costs
- Minimum acceptable profit
- Expected buyer financing availability

## Opportunity Scoring Model

Use a 1-to-5 score:

- 1 = Unacceptable
- 2 = Weak
- 3 = Acceptable
- 4 = Strong
- 5 = Excellent

Suggested criteria:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Official-source quality | 15% |
| Title clarity | 15% |
| Possession clarity | 15% |
| Market-value evidence | 10% |
| Adjusted discount | 15% |
| Property condition | 10% |
| Funding feasibility | 10% |
| Exit liquidity | 5% |
| Management practicality | 5% |

A high score does not replace attorney review.

## Risk Overrides

The following should override a high numerical score:

- Unverified legal authority
- Unclear ownership
- Unknown payment recipient
- Litigated rights presented as title
- Unaffordable worst-case scenario
- Emergency reserves required
- No viable exit
- Unresolved possession
- Evidence of fraud
- Missing official documents

## Opportunity Status

Use one status:

- New lead
- Initial verification
- Rejected
- Watchlist
- Waiting for documents
- Preliminary analysis
- Attorney review required
- Property inspection required
- Funding review required
- Approved for detailed analysis
- Bid preparation
- No bid
- Closed
- Purchased

## Research Time Control

Do not spend equal time on every lead.

Suggested process:

### Five-minute review

Check:

- Source
- Location
- Price
- Sale type
- Obvious red flags

### Thirty-minute review

Check:

- Authority
- Case
- basic title information
- Occupancy
- Market comparables
- Preliminary total cost

### Detailed review

Only after the lead survives initial screening:

- Attorney review
- Full title analysis
- Inspection
- Appraisal
- Financing
- Maximum bid
- Exit scenarios

## Opportunity Pipeline

Maintain stages:

1. Lead discovered
2. Source verified
3. Basic documents obtained
4. Preliminary economics completed
5. Risk score completed
6. Attorney questions prepared
7. Inspection or access pursued
8. Funding confirmed
9. Maximum bid approved
10. Bid or purchase decision
11. Outcome recorded

## Auction Calendar

Track:

- Publication date
- Registration deadline
- Deposit deadline
- Offer deadline
- Auction date
- Payment deadline
- Court or authority
- Case number
- Reminder dates
- Current status

Missing one deadline can invalidate participation.

## Comparable-Property Review

Use at least several relevant comparables.

Record:

- Address or area
- Property type
- Size
- Condition
- Parking
- Storage
- Floor
- Administration
- Asking or completed price
- Price per square meter
- Source
- Date
- Adjustment required

Do not compare renovated vacant property with occupied damaged property without adjustment.

## Price-Per-Square-Meter Control

Calculate:

Price per square meter equals:

Property value
divided by verified area

Confirm whether the area used is:

- Private area
- Constructed area
- Lot area
- Commercial area
- Registry area
- Cadastral area

Inconsistent area definitions can distort comparisons.

## Property Visit Record

When a visit is possible, record:

- Date
- Exterior condition
- Building condition
- Street activity
- Noise
- Security
- Access
- Occupancy signs
- Nearby services
- Comparable properties
- Photographs
- Concerns
- Follow-up questions

Do not enter occupied property without permission.

## Watchlist Rules

A property may remain on the watchlist when:

- Documentation is incomplete
- Price is currently too high
- Auction date is not scheduled
- Occupancy is unresolved
- Market value needs confirmation
- Funding is not yet available

A watchlist entry is not an approved purchase.

## Rejection Documentation

Record rejected properties and reasons.

Possible reasons:

- Insufficient discount
- Title complexity
- Occupancy risk
- No inspection
- Repair uncertainty
- Weak rental demand
- Poor resale liquidity
- Excessive administration
- Funding deadline
- Geographic exclusion
- Fraud concern

Documented rejection prevents repeated research of the same unsuitable property.

## Weekly Review

During active searching:

1. Add new leads.
2. Verify official sources.
3. Remove duplicates.
4. Reject obvious failures.
5. Update deadlines.
6. Review watchlist.
7. Identify documents needed.
8. Select no more than a small number for detailed review.
9. Update professional questions.
10. Record decisions.

## Beginner Rules

1. Start with official sources.
2. Verify every case and seller independently.
3. Reject missing or inconsistent documentation.
4. Use conservative market values.
5. Include all acquisition and possession costs.
6. Define geographic and property-type limits.
7. Confirm funding deadlines before bidding.
8. Limit detailed research to qualified leads.
9. Maintain a written rejection log.
10. Never let an auction deadline override due diligence.
