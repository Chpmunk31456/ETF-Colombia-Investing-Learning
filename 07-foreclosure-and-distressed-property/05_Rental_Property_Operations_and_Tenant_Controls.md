# Lesson 23 — Rental Property Operations, Tenant Screening, and Net Income Controls

## Learning Objective

Learn how to evaluate, prepare, lease, operate, monitor, and exit a rental property while controlling vacancy, tenant, maintenance, legal, cash-flow, and concentration risk.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

It does not replace advice from a qualified Colombian attorney, accountant, property manager, insurer, or tax professional.

## Core Principle

Gross rent is not investment income.

The relevant measure is the amount remaining after:

- Vacancy
- Administration
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Maintenance
- Repairs
- Property management
- Utilities
- Legal costs
- Financing
- Taxes
- Capital expenditures

A property can collect rent and still lose money.

## Conservative Rent Estimate

Prepare three estimates:

### Optimistic rent

Assumes strong demand and minimal negotiation.

### Base-case rent

Uses realistic comparable evidence.

### Conservative rent

Assumes slower leasing or tenant negotiation.

The investment should not depend solely on the optimistic estimate.

## Gross Scheduled Rent

Gross scheduled rent is the total contractual rent expected if the property remains fully occupied.

Annual gross scheduled rent equals:

Monthly contractual rent
multiplied by 12

## Effective Gross Income

Effective gross income adjusts for vacancy and collection loss.

Effective gross income equals:

Gross scheduled rent
minus vacancy
minus unpaid rent
plus other recurring property income

Possible other income includes:

- Parking
- Storage
- Furnishings
- Approved utility reimbursement

## Vacancy Allowance

A vacancy allowance should cover:

- Time between tenants
- Marketing period
- Repairs between tenants
- Lease negotiation
- Delayed move-in
- Nonpayment
- Legal recovery period

A property with no current vacancy can still require a vacancy reserve.

## Operating Expenses

Typical operating expenses may include:

- Administration
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Maintenance
- Routine repairs
- Property management
- Leasing commission
- Accounting
- Legal review
- Advertising
- Utilities paid by owner
- Security
- Cleaning
- Pest control
- Replacement reserves

Mortgage principal and interest are normally analyzed separately from operating expenses.

## Net Operating Income

Net operating income equals:

Effective gross income
minus operating expenses

Net operating income is calculated before:

- Mortgage payments
- Income tax
- Major capital improvements

## Cash Flow After Financing

Pre-tax cash flow equals:

Net operating income
minus loan payments
minus capital reserve contributions
minus other financing-related costs

## Capital Expenditures

Capital expenditures are larger, less frequent costs that extend useful life or materially improve the property.

Examples:

- Roof replacement
- Major plumbing replacement
- Electrical modernization
- Kitchen replacement
- Bathroom renovation
- Window replacement
- Structural work
- Elevator assessment
- Major appliance replacement

Do not treat all capital expenditures as routine maintenance.

## Arrears Procedure

When rent is late:

1. Verify payment records.
2. Contact the tenant through documented channels.
3. Issue required notices.
4. Review guarantee coverage.
5. Contact insurer or guarantor where applicable.
6. Preserve communications.
7. Avoid self-help eviction.
8. Obtain legal advice promptly.
9. Track legal and collection costs.
10. Protect the property lawfully.

## No Self-Help Eviction

Do not attempt unauthorized actions such as:

- Changing locks
- Removing belongings
- Cutting utilities
- Threatening occupants
- Entering without authority
- Blocking access

Possession and eviction procedures should follow applicable law.

## Rental Yield

Gross rental yield equals:

Annual gross rent
divided by total property cost

Gross yield ignores expenses.

## Net Rental Yield

Net rental yield equals:

Annual net operating income
divided by total property cost

Use total property cost, including:

- Purchase price
- Closing costs
- Legal costs
- Repairs
- Initial furnishing
- Capital committed

## Cap Rate

Capitalization rate equals:

Annual net operating income
divided by current property value

Cap rate does not include financing.

## Cash-on-Cash Return

Cash-on-cash return equals:

Annual pre-tax cash flow
divided by total cash invested

This measure reflects leverage but does not capture every tax or appreciation effect.

## Break-Even Occupancy

Break-even occupancy estimates the occupancy level needed to cover operating costs and debt service.

A property requiring nearly full occupancy to avoid losses has limited margin for error.

## Rental Stress Tests

## Scenario 1 — Three Months Vacancy

Calculate:

- Lost rent
- Continuing expenses
- Reserve usage
- Ending cash position

## Scenario 2 — Six Months Vacancy

Calculate:

- Total carrying cost
- Loan payments
- Reserve depletion
- Required outside funding

## Scenario 3 — Tenant Nonpayment

Include:

- Lost rent
- Legal cost
- Property damage
- Time to recover possession
- Guarantee recovery
- Administration
- Utilities

## Scenario 4 — Major Repair

Model a repair equal to:

- 3% of property value
- 5% of property value
- 10% of property value

## Scenario 5 — Rent Decline

Assume achievable rent falls by:

- 10%
- 20%

Determine whether the property remains viable.

## Scenario 6 — Administration Increase

Assume administration rises materially or an extraordinary assessment is imposed.

## Scenario 7 — Interest-Rate Increase

For variable financing, calculate the new payment and cash flow.

## Scenario 8 — Property-Manager Failure

Assume:

- Delayed rent transfer
- Missing records
- Unauthorized repairs
- Poor tenant communication
- Need to replace the manager

## Stop Conditions

Pause or reject a rental plan when:

- Building rules prohibit the intended use
- Legal possession is unresolved
- Property is unsafe
- Rent assumptions lack evidence
- Net cash flow depends on full occupancy
- Reserves are inadequate
- Tenant screening is skipped
- Lease has not been legally reviewed
- Owner cannot manage emergencies
- Property management is unverified
- Tax treatment is unresolved
- Emergency household funds would cover property losses

## Beginner Rules

1. Calculate net income, not gross rent.
2. Verify building rental restrictions.
3. Screen tenants consistently and lawfully.
4. Use a professionally reviewed lease.
5. Document move-in and move-out condition.
6. Maintain vacancy and repair reserves.
7. Separate property accounting.
8. Avoid self-help eviction.
9. Review performance annually.
10. Sell or restructure when the property no longer serves its purpose.
