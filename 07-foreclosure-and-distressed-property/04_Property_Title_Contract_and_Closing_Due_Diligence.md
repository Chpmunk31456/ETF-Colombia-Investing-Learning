# Lesson 22 — Property Title, Purchase Contract, and Closing Due Diligence

## Learning Objective

Learn how to organize the legal, financial, physical, and administrative review of a Colombian property before signing a purchase promise, paying a deposit, accepting financing, or completing registration.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

It does not replace review by a qualified Colombian real-estate attorney, notary, tax adviser, lender, engineer, or property inspector.

## Core Principle

Do not treat a property as purchased merely because:

- A deposit was paid
- A promise of sale was signed
- A mortgage was approved
- The seller delivered keys
- The buyer moved in
- A private agreement was signed
- The property appears in an advertisement

The transaction must proceed through the required legal, notarial, tax, payment, and registration steps.

## Main Purchase Structures

Possible structures include:

- Standard purchase
- Financed purchase
- Cash purchase
- Bank-owned property
- Lease with purchase option
- Assignment of contractual rights
- Purchase from an estate
- Purchase from multiple owners
- Judicial auction
- DIAN auction
- Purchase involving a mortgage payoff
- Purchase involving an existing tenant

Each structure requires different documentation and risk controls.

## Parties to the Transaction

Identify every party.

Possible parties include:

- Registered owner
- Buyer
- Co-buyer
- Spouse or permanent partner
- Bank
- Mortgage creditor
- Real-estate agent
- Attorney
- Notary
- Property administrator
- Tenant
- Executor or heirs
- Court or government authority
- Company representative

Verify the authority of anyone signing for another person or entity.

## Seller Identity Review

Confirm:

- Full legal name
- Identification number
- Marital status
- Address
- Contact information
- Registered ownership
- Authority to sell
- Spousal or partner consent where required
- Power-of-attorney validity
- Company representation if seller is a legal entity
- Succession authority if seller inherited the property

Do not send money solely because a person possesses keys or claims to represent the owner.

## Certificate of Tradition and Freedom

Obtain a current Certificate of Tradition and Freedom for the exact matrícula inmobiliaria.

Review:

- Current registered owner
- Property description
- Ownership percentage
- Mortgages
- Embargoes
- Liens
- Attachments
- Usufruct
- Trust or fiduciary interests
- Patrimonio de familia
- Afectación a vivienda familiar
- Court orders
- Transfer restrictions
- Prior ownership history
- Cancellations of prior encumbrances
- Subdivision or consolidation history

The certificate should be reviewed close to signing and again close to closing when appropriate.

## Cadastral Information

Confirm:

- Cadastral identification
- Property address
- Land area
- Constructed area
- Property use
- Cadastral valuation
- Municipality
- Property-tax account
- Whether cadastral and registry descriptions match

Material discrepancies may delay:

- Financing
- Insurance
- Registration
- Future sale
- Construction approval
- Tax calculations

## Property Description

Confirm that the legal description includes everything being purchased.

Examples:

- Apartment
- House
- Lot
- Parking space
- Storage room
- Terrace
- Patio
- Additional land
- Common-area rights
- Exclusive-use area
- Commercial space

Parking and storage areas may have:

- Separate matrícula
- Common ownership
- Exclusive-use rights
- Restrictions on transfer
- Separate administration charges

## Ownership Percentage

For jointly owned property, confirm:

- Percentage owned by each seller
- Whether every owner must sign
- Whether any owner is deceased
- Whether succession proceedings are complete
- Whether one owner acts through a power of attorney
- Whether a minor or protected person owns an interest

A buyer should not assume one co-owner can sell the entire property.

## Marital and Family Restrictions

Review whether the property is affected by:

- Afectación a vivienda familiar
- Patrimonio de familia
- Marital property rights
- Permanent-partner rights
- Court-ordered family protections
- Minor children’s interests

The required release or consent should be completed correctly before or during closing.

## Mortgage Review

When the property has a mortgage, identify:

- Creditor
- Current payoff amount
- Interest through closing
- Required cancellation procedure
- Payment destination
- Release documentation
- Registration of mortgage cancellation
- Timing between payment and cancellation
- Responsibility for related costs

Do not rely only on the seller’s statement of the remaining balance.

## Embargoes and Legal Proceedings

For any embargo, lien, or legal notation, determine:

- Issuing authority
- Case number
- Current status
- Amount claimed
- Whether cancellation is possible
- Required court order
- Payment process
- Registration process
- Whether closing may proceed
- Which party bears the risk

A promise that an embargo will be removed later is not equivalent to completed cancellation.

## Property-Tax Review

Obtain evidence of:

- Current property-tax status
- Prior-year balances
- Penalties
- Interest
- Payment agreements
- Pending reassessment
- Municipal charges
- Betterment or valorización obligations

Confirm how taxes will be divided between buyer and seller.

## Administration Review

For property under horizontal-property rules, request:

- Current administration certificate
- Outstanding administration balance
- Interest or penalties
- Extraordinary assessments
- Pending assessments
- Meeting minutes
- Budget
- Reserve-fund information
- Litigation involving the building
- Major planned repairs
- Insurance information
- Building rules

A property with no individual debt can still be affected by financial problems in the building.

## Utility Review

Confirm balances for:

- Electricity
- Water
- Gas
- Internet
- Waste collection
- Other municipal services

Determine:

- Whether service is active
- Whether meters are legal and separate
- Whether reconnection fees apply
- Whether debt follows the customer or affects the property
- Whether unauthorized connections exist

## Occupancy Review

Identify who currently occupies the property.

Possible occupants include:

- Seller
- Tenant
- Family member
- Employee
- Caretaker
- Unauthorized occupant
- Buyer under an earlier contract

Confirm:

- Legal basis for occupancy
- Lease terms
- Deposit
- Rent status
- Termination requirements
- Delivery date
- Condition at delivery
- Consequences of delayed delivery

Possession should be documented separately from title transfer.

## Lease Review

When a tenant occupies the property, request:

- Signed lease
- Amendments
- Payment history
- Security deposit record
- Inventory
- Notice history
- Tenant identification
- Rent increase history
- Utility responsibilities
- Administration responsibilities
- Termination provisions
- Litigation or complaints

Projected rental income should be based on the actual legal and operating situation.

## Physical Inspection

A property inspection should consider:

- Structure
- Roof
- Foundation
- Walls
- Moisture
- Mold
- Plumbing
- Electrical system
- Gas system
- Windows
- Doors
- Floors
- Bathrooms
- Kitchen
- Water pressure
- Drainage
- Appliances
- Unauthorized construction
- Pest damage
- Safety hazards
- Accessibility
- Parking
- Storage
- Common areas

Photographs should be dated and retained.

## Building and Construction Review

Confirm:

- Construction license
- Approved plans
- Additions
- Renovations
- Area modifications
- Zoning
- Land-use rules
- Building completion
- Occupancy approval
- Structural restrictions
- Common-area permissions

Unauthorized additions may affect financing, insurance, taxes, and resale.

## Environmental and Location Review

Evaluate:

- Flood exposure
- Landslide risk
- Seismic concerns
- Water availability
- Noise
- Pollution
- Road access
- Future construction
- Public transportation
- Security
- School access
- Medical access
- Commercial activity
- Industrial activity
- Planned infrastructure

Visit the area at different times and on different days.

## Purchase Promise

The promesa de compraventa should clearly identify:

- Buyer
- Seller
- Property
- Matrícula
- Purchase price
- Deposit
- Payment schedule
- Financing
- Closing date
- Notary
- Required documents
- Delivery date
- Condition of property
- Taxes and fees
- Encumbrance cancellation
- Administration status
- Utility status
- Penalties
- Termination rights
- Failure of financing
- Force majeure
- Dispute procedure

## Deposit

Before paying a deposit, confirm:

- Amount
- Legal purpose
- Whether refundable
- Conditions for refund
- Consequences of buyer default
- Consequences of seller default
- Receiving account
- Account holder
- Receipt
- Contract reference
- Payment date

Avoid cash or payment to an unrelated personal account.

## Arras

A contract may characterize money as arras.

The legal consequences may depend on the wording and applicable law.

The document should clarify:

- Type of arras
- Whether withdrawal is permitted
- Penalty for withdrawal
- Whether the amount forms part of the price
- Treatment after default
- Refund conditions

Do not rely on informal explanations of what the deposit means.

## Financing Condition

When financing is necessary, the purchase promise should address:

- Required loan amount
- Application deadline
- Approval deadline
- Appraisal
- Required documentation
- Consequence of rejection
- Consequence of a lower appraisal
- Whether deposit is refundable
- Extension process
- Responsibility for lender delays

A prequalification is not the same as final loan approval.

## Appraisal Condition

Confirm:

- Appraiser
- Purpose
- Effective date
- Market assumptions
- Areas included
- Comparable properties
- Physical condition
- Occupancy
- Legal limitations
- Final value

A lender appraisal protects the lender and may not identify all risks for the buyer.

## Purchase Price and Payment Schedule

Record each payment separately.

Possible payments include:

- Reservation payment
- Arras
- Initial deposit
- Down payment
- Mortgage proceeds
- Seller-financed amount
- Final closing balance
- Withholding
- Direct mortgage payoff
- Administration payoff
- Tax payoff

Every payment should have:

- Due date
- Recipient
- Account
- Purpose
- Supporting document
- Confirmation

## Source of Funds

The buyer may need to document:

- Bank statements
- Pension income
- Salary
- Property sale proceeds
- Investment liquidation
- Loan proceeds
- Foreign transfers
- Currency conversion
- Family contribution
- Business income

Keep evidence showing the lawful origin and transfer path of funds.

## Foreign Transfers

When sending funds from outside Colombia, record:

- Sending bank
- Receiving bank
- Date
- Amount
- Currency
- Exchange rate
- Transfer purpose
- Intermediary fees
- Foreign-exchange declaration
- Bank certification
- Amount credited

Confirm applicable foreign-exchange and banking documentation with qualified professionals.

## Notarial Closing

Before the notarial signing, confirm:

- Final deed draft
- Buyer and seller identification
- Property description
- Matrícula
- Price
- Payment statement
- Mortgage
- Encumbrance cancellation
- Tax status
- Administration certificate
- Required family releases
- Powers of attorney
- Required bank documents
- Delivery provisions
- Responsibility for costs

Review the draft before the signing appointment.

## Deed Review

The deed should accurately reflect:

- Parties
- Ownership percentages
- Property
- Parking and storage
- Price
- Payment method
- Existing and new mortgages
- Encumbrance cancellation
- Family-home releases
- Possession
- Tax allocation
- Legal representations
- Attachments
- Required signatures

Do not sign without resolving material discrepancies.

## Registration

Signing the deed is not the final administrative step.

Track:

- Filing date
- Registration office
- Registration fees
- Document number
- Registration status
- Corrections
- Recorded ownership
- New Certificate of Tradition and Freedom
- Mortgage registration
- Cancellation of prior encumbrances

Obtain a new certificate after registration to verify the final result.

## Possession and Key Delivery

Document:

- Date
- Time
- Keys
- Access cards
- Remote controls
- Utility readings
- Inventory
- Appliances
- Damage
- Repairs promised
- Occupants removed
- Building administration notification
- Property documents delivered

Use a signed delivery record with photographs.

## Risk of Early Possession

Moving in before registration or final closing can create risks involving:

- Title
- Financing
- Insurance
- Seller default
- Property damage
- Utilities
- Improvement costs
- Occupancy disputes
- Refund difficulty

Early possession should be reviewed legally and documented clearly.

## Insurance at Closing

Confirm when insurance becomes effective.

Possible coverage includes:

- Property
- Fire
- Earthquake
- Liability
- Mortgage life
- Rental use
- Common-area coverage

Avoid a gap between delivery and insurance activation.

## Tax and Fee Allocation

Clarify responsibility for:

- Notarial costs
- Registration
- Withholding
- Property taxes
- Valorización
- Mortgage cancellation
- New mortgage registration
- Administration certificates
- Brokerage commission
- Legal fees
- Appraisal
- Bank charges

Do not rely on custom or verbal statements; put allocations in writing.

## Closing Reserve

Maintain a reserve for:

- Registration differences
- Unexpected taxes
- Repairs
- Utility activation
- Moving
- Insurance
- Administration
- Furnishings
- Legal corrections
- Payment delays

Do not commit every available peso to the purchase price.

## Post-Closing Tasks

After closing:

1. Verify registration.
2. Obtain updated Certificate of Tradition and Freedom.
3. Update municipal tax ownership.
4. Notify building administration.
5. Transfer utilities.
6. Activate insurance.
7. Store deed and supporting records.
8. Update estate inventory.
9. Update investment records.
10. Record cost basis.
11. Review beneficiary and incapacity planning.
12. Schedule maintenance.

## Cost-Basis File

Retain:

- Purchase price
- Notarial costs
- Registration costs
- legal costs
- commissions
- capital improvements
- appraisal
- eligible closing costs
- currency conversion
- supporting invoices
- payment evidence

A tax professional should determine which costs affect tax basis.

## Stop Conditions

Stop or pause the purchase when:

- Seller is not the registered owner
- A required owner refuses to sign
- Power of attorney cannot be verified
- Matrícula does not match the property
- Material liens are unexplained
- Family-home restrictions are unresolved
- Financing is not final
- Deposit terms are unclear
- Receiving account is inconsistent
- Property areas do not match records
- Occupancy cannot be resolved
- Administration debt is disputed
- Seller refuses independent legal review
- Deed differs materially from the agreement
- Closing funds would eliminate emergency reserves

## Beginner Rules

1. Verify the seller against the property registry.
2. Use an independent Colombian real-estate attorney.
3. Review the current Certificate of Tradition and Freedom.
4. Put financing and refund conditions in writing.
5. Verify every receiving account.
6. Confirm taxes, administration, utilities, and occupancy.
7. Inspect the property before committing where possible.
8. Review the deed before signing.
9. Track registration through completion.
10. Preserve emergency and closing reserves.
