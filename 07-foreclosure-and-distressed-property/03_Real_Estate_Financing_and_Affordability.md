# Lesson 21 — Real-Estate Financing, Mortgage Affordability, and Purchase Reserves

## Learning Objective

Learn how to evaluate the affordability of a residential or investment-property purchase by considering the down payment, loan payment, interest rate, insurance, taxes, administration fees, maintenance, repairs, vacancy, legal costs, and required cash reserves.

## Important Principle

The purchase price is not the monthly cost.

A property must be evaluated using:

- Initial cash required
- Monthly debt payment
- Administration fees
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Maintenance
- Utilities
- Repairs
- Legal and registration costs
- Vacancy
- Currency risk
- Emergency reserves
- Exit costs

A property is not affordable merely because a lender approves the loan.

## Main Financing Structures

Possible financing arrangements include:

- Fixed-rate mortgage
- Variable-rate mortgage
- Peso-denominated mortgage
- UVR-indexed mortgage
- Bank loan
- Developer financing
- Seller financing
- Lease-to-own arrangement
- Secured personal loan
- Cash purchase
- Combination of cash and financing

Each structure has different risks.

## Fixed-Rate Loan

A fixed-rate loan generally keeps the contractual interest rate unchanged during the fixed period.

Potential advantages:

- Predictable payment structure
- Easier budgeting
- Protection from future interest-rate increases

Potential disadvantages:

- Rate may initially be higher
- Refinancing may be costly
- Prepayment rules may apply
- Insurance and administration costs can still rise

## Variable-Rate Loan

A variable-rate loan can change according to a reference rate or contractual formula.

Potential advantages:

- Lower initial payment in some cases
- Possible benefit if rates decline

Potential disadvantages:

- Payment may increase
- Budget uncertainty
- Greater stress during rate increases
- Total interest may be difficult to predict

Stress-test a variable-rate loan at higher rates before accepting it.

## UVR-Indexed Financing

UVR-based obligations are adjusted using an inflation-linked unit.

Potential effects can include:

- Balance changes in peso terms
- Payment changes over time
- Inflation-linked debt growth
- Greater complexity than a simple fixed-peso loan

The borrower should obtain a written amortization explanation and model multiple inflation scenarios.

## Down Payment

A larger down payment can:

- Reduce the loan balance
- Reduce the monthly payment
- Reduce total interest
- Improve lender approval
- Lower leverage
- Create more equity

However, an excessively large down payment can:

- Deplete emergency reserves
- Leave insufficient repair funds
- Reduce investment diversification
- Create liquidity stress
- Concentrate too much wealth in one property

The down payment should not consume all available cash.

## Loan-to-Value Ratio

Loan-to-value ratio compares the loan amount with the property value.

Loan-to-value ratio equals:

Loan amount
divided by property value

A higher ratio generally means:

- More leverage
- Less initial equity
- Greater lender risk
- Greater borrower sensitivity to price declines

## Debt-Service Ratio

Debt-service ratio compares monthly debt payments with monthly income.

Record:

- Mortgage payment
- Vehicle payment
- Credit-card minimums
- Personal loans
- Other required debt payments
- Gross monthly income
- Net monthly income

A lender’s permitted ratio may be higher than the borrower’s personally comfortable ratio.

## Housing-Cost Ratio

Total monthly housing cost may include:

- Mortgage payment
- Administration fee
- Property tax reserve
- Insurance
- Maintenance reserve
- Utilities
- Parking
- Security
- Required assessments

Housing-cost ratio equals:

Total monthly housing cost
divided by net monthly household income

## True Monthly Property Cost

Use:

True monthly property cost =
Loan payment
+ administration
+ property-tax reserve
+ insurance
+ maintenance reserve
+ repair reserve
+ utilities not paid by tenant
+ vacancy reserve
+ property management
+ legal reserve

## Property-Tax Reserve

Annual property tax should be converted into a monthly reserve.

Monthly property-tax reserve equals:

Expected annual property tax
divided by 12

Do not wait until the tax bill arrives to begin saving.

## Administration Fees

For apartments or gated communities, review:

- Current monthly administration
- Extraordinary assessments
- Delinquent amounts
- Planned building projects
- Reserve-fund condition
- Insurance coverage
- Meeting minutes
- Litigation involving the building

Administration fees may increase over time.

## Maintenance Reserve

A property requires ongoing maintenance even when no major repairs are visible.

Possible reserves include:

- Routine maintenance
- Appliance replacement
- Plumbing
- Electrical repairs
- Paint
- Flooring
- Windows
- Roof or common-area assessments
- Security systems
- Pest control

## Repair Reserve

A repair reserve should be separate from the emergency household reserve.

For distressed property, the repair reserve should be larger because:

- Inspection may be limited
- Prior maintenance may be unknown
- Occupants may have damaged the property
- Utilities may be disconnected
- Unauthorized construction may exist

## Insurance

Possible insurance costs include:

- Property insurance
- Mortgage life insurance
- Fire and earthquake coverage
- Liability insurance
- Title-related coverage where available
- Rental-property coverage
- Loss-of-rent coverage

Confirm which insurance is:

- Legally required
- Required by the lender
- Included in the payment
- Charged separately
- Optional but prudent

## Closing and Registration Costs

Possible costs include:

- Appraisal
- Credit study
- Legal review
- Notarial charges
- Registration
- Taxes
- Certificates
- Bank fees
- Mortgage-registration costs
- Property inspection
- Title review

The buyer should obtain a written closing-cost estimate before signing.

## Prepayment

Ask:

- Can principal be prepaid?
- Is there a penalty?
- Can the loan term be reduced?
- Can the monthly payment be reduced?
- How is the extra payment applied?
- Is written instruction required?
- Does the bank apply extra money to future installments instead of principal?

Keep confirmation of every additional principal payment.

## Extra-Payment Strategies

Possible strategies include:

### Reduce term

Maintain the regular payment while shortening the loan duration.

### Reduce payment

Apply principal and recalculate a lower monthly payment.

### Periodic lump-sum payments

Use bonuses, consulting income, or savings to reduce principal.

### Scheduled additional payment

Add a fixed amount each month.

The most suitable method depends on liquidity, loan rules, and financial priorities.

## Savings Versus Extra Principal

Before sending extra money to the loan, compare:

- Mortgage interest rate
- After-tax return on savings
- Emergency-reserve level
- Upcoming expenses
- Required liquidity
- Currency exposure
- Prepayment terms
- Alternative investment opportunities
- Risk tolerance

Paying down debt produces a predictable reduction in interest expense, but it also reduces liquidity.

## Required Purchase Reserves

Before closing, consider separate reserves for:

- Emergency household expenses
- Initial property repairs
- Closing-cost overruns
- Moving
- Furniture and appliances
- Six months of housing payments
- Medical expenses
- Vehicle costs
- Legal or documentation problems
- Vacancy if the property is rented

## Six-Month Ownership Reserve

A conservative reserve may include six months of:

- Mortgage payments
- Administration fees
- Property-tax accrual
- Insurance
- Utilities
- Maintenance
- Property management
- Vacancy
- Legal costs

Distressed property may require a longer reserve period.

## Rental Property Financing

For a rental property, calculate:

Annual net operating income =
Gross rent
minus vacancy
minus administration
minus property tax
minus insurance
minus maintenance
minus management
minus recurring repairs

Debt-service coverage ratio equals:

Annual net operating income
divided by annual loan payments

A ratio below 1 means the property does not generate enough operating income to cover debt service.

## Personal Residence Versus Investment Property

### Personal residence

Benefits may include:

- Housing stability
- Personal use
- Reduced rent exposure
- Long-term ownership

Costs include:

- Mortgage
- Maintenance
- Taxes
- Insurance
- Transaction costs
- Reduced liquidity
- Concentration

### Investment property

Must be evaluated through:

- Net rental income
- Vacancy
- Tenant risk
- Legal costs
- Property management
- Taxes
- Financing
- Resale value

Personal enjoyment should not be counted as investment return.

## Distressed Property Financing Risks

Distressed properties may be difficult to finance because:

- Possession is unresolved
- Title transfer is incomplete
- Property is occupied
- Condition is unknown
- Appraisal is low
- Bank deadlines exceed auction deadlines
- Repairs are required before habitation
- The lender will not accept the property as collateral

Confirm financing before participating in an auction.

## Income Reliability

Do not calculate affordability using only the highest possible income.

Separate:

- Guaranteed pension income
- Stable salary
- Contract income
- Consulting income
- Rental income
- Variable bonuses
- Investment income

Use conservative assumptions for variable income.

## Currency Risk

A person earning USD and paying a COP mortgage faces exchange-rate risk.

If COP strengthens:

- USD income converts into fewer pesos
- The mortgage consumes a larger portion of USD income

If COP weakens:

- USD income converts into more pesos
- Local payments may become easier
- Imported goods and travel may become more expensive

Maintain a COP payment reserve rather than depending on a same-day conversion each month.

## Property Affordability Stress Tests

## Scenario 1 — Income Decline

Assume employment or consulting income stops.

Calculate:

- Income remaining
- Monthly property cost
- Monthly shortfall
- Months covered by reserves
- Expenses that can be reduced

## Scenario 2 — Interest-Rate Increase

For variable financing, model:

- Two-percentage-point increase
- Four-percentage-point increase
- Higher insurance cost
- Higher monthly payment

## Scenario 3 — COP Strengthens

Model a lower COP amount received from USD income.

Questions:

- Can pension income still cover the payment?
- How long will the COP reserve last?
- Would assets need to be sold?

## Scenario 4 — Major Repair

Assume an unexpected repair equal to:

- 3% of property value
- 5% of property value
- 10% of property value

Determine whether the repair reserve is sufficient.

## Scenario 5 — Vacancy

For rental property, assume:

- Three months without rent
- Six months without rent
- Tenant legal dispute
- Repairs between tenants

## Scenario 6 — Property Value Decline

Assume the property value declines by 15% or 25%.

Determine:

- Current equity
- Loan-to-value ratio
- Ability to sell
- Likely transaction loss
- Whether the property can still be held comfortably

## Affordability Decision Rules

Pause when:

- Down payment consumes emergency reserves
- Monthly cost depends on uncertain employment income
- Variable-rate payment has not been stress-tested
- Financing is not confirmed
- Closing costs are estimated too narrowly
- Repair reserves are missing
- Administration debt is unresolved
- Property taxes are unknown
- Loan terms are not understood
- Extra payments may not reduce principal
- One property would dominate total net worth
- The purchase prevents required medical or family reserves

## Questions for the Lender

1. What is the effective annual rate?
2. Is the rate fixed, variable, or indexed?
3. Is the obligation denominated in pesos or UVR?
4. What insurance is mandatory?
5. What fees are charged before closing?
6. What is the total initial cash required?
7. What is the estimated monthly payment?
8. Can principal be prepaid without penalty?
9. How are additional payments applied?
10. Can the term be reduced?
11. Can the payment be reduced?
12. What happens after a late payment?
13. Can the loan be transferred or refinanced?
14. What income documentation is required?
15. What happens if the appraisal is below the purchase price?

## Questions for the Seller or Auction Authority

1. What is the exact legal ownership structure?
2. Is possession delivered?
3. Are taxes current?
4. Are administration fees current?
5. Are utilities current?
6. Are there liens or embargoes?
7. Is the property insurable?
8. Can it be financed?
9. Is an interior inspection permitted?
10. What deadlines apply?

## Beginner Rules

1. Evaluate total ownership cost, not only the loan payment.
2. Preserve emergency reserves after the down payment.
3. Stress-test income and exchange rates.
4. Confirm whether the loan is fixed, variable, or UVR-indexed.
5. Obtain written closing-cost estimates.
6. Maintain repair and payment reserves.
7. Confirm how extra principal payments are applied.
8. Do not rely on projected rental income before possession.
9. Confirm financing before bidding on distressed property.
10. Reject a purchase that depends on optimistic assumptions.
