# Lesson 25 — Property Sale, Exit Planning, and Final Profit Reconciliation

## Learning Objective

Learn how to prepare a Colombian property for sale, estimate net proceeds, verify buyers and payments, control closing risks, preserve tax records, and calculate the actual investment result after all costs.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

It does not replace advice from a qualified Colombian real-estate attorney, accountant, tax professional, appraiser, broker, notary, lender, or property manager.

## Core Principle

The advertised sale price is not the seller's profit.

The relevant result is:

Net sale proceeds
minus total property investment
minus financing costs
minus operating losses
minus applicable taxes

A property can sell for more than its purchase price while still producing a weak or negative investment return.

## Define the Exit Objective

Possible exit objectives include:

- Sell a personal residence
- Sell a rental property
- Sell after renovation
- Sell an adjudicated or distressed property
- Reduce real-estate concentration
- Create liquidity
- Pay debt
- Fund another property
- Support retirement spending
- Simplify estate administration

The objective affects timing, pricing, preparation, and acceptable terms.

## Exit Triggers

Possible planned triggers include:

- Target price achieved
- Target return achieved
- Renovation completed
- Possession resolved
- Rental performance deteriorated
- Major repairs approaching
- Property concentration became excessive
- Liquidity needs changed
- Health or family circumstances changed
- Management burden became excessive
- Neighborhood outlook changed
- Legal or tax risk increased

A written exit trigger reduces emotional decision-making.

## Hold Versus Sell Review

Before listing, compare:

### Continue holding

Consider:

- Expected net rent
- Future repairs
- Administration
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Financing
- Vacancy
- Management burden
- Expected appreciation
- Concentration risk

### Sell now

Consider:

- Current market value
- Buyer demand
- Selling costs
- Tax consequences
- Loan payoff
- Required repairs
- Time to close
- Alternative use of proceeds

- Safety defect

### Optional

## As-Is Sale

An as-is sale may reduce seller work but can:

- Lower price
- Reduce buyer pool
- Complicate financing
- Increase negotiation
- Require clear disclosure
- Attract highly price-sensitive buyers

“As is” does not automatically eliminate disclosure or legal responsibility.

## Independent Valuation

Estimate market value using:

- Independent appraisal
- Same-building transactions
- Similar neighborhood sales
- Comparable size
- Similar age and condition
- Parking and storage
- Occupancy
- Administration fee
- Floor and view
- Renovation quality

Separate asking prices from completed transaction evidence.

## Pricing Scenarios

Prepare three prices:

### Aspirational price

Higher price requiring favorable demand and patience.

### Market price

Supported by current comparable evidence.

### Minimum acceptable price

The lowest price consistent with financial objectives and risk limits.

The minimum should account for all selling and closing costs.

## Net Sale Proceeds

Calculate:

Net sale proceeds =
Gross sale price
minus selling costs
minus loan payoff
minus taxes and withholding
minus unresolved property obligations

## Total Property Investment

Calculate:

Total property investment =
Purchase price
+ acquisition costs
+ legal costs
+ financing costs
+ repairs
+ renovation
+ capital improvements
+ carrying losses
+ possession costs
+ unrecovered operating costs

Tax basis may differ from economic investment cost.

A tax professional should determine the applicable tax basis.

## Economic Profit

Economic profit equals:

Net sale proceeds
+ net income received during ownership
minus total property investment

Include:

- Net rent
- Other property income
- Vacancy losses
- Operating costs
- Financing costs

## Simple Return

Simple return percentage equals:

Economic profit
divided by total cash invested

This does not adjust for the amount of time invested.

## Annualized Return

Annualized return considers the holding period.

It can be more informative when comparing:

- Property held for one year
- Property held for five years
- CDT
- ETF
- Other property

Use a financial calculation tool and verify the inputs.

## Internal Rate of Return

Internal rate of return considers the timing of:

- Initial purchase
- Additional repairs
- Rental cash flows
- Financing
- Sale proceeds

IRR can be useful but is highly sensitive to assumptions and recorded cash-flow dates.

## Return on Equity

For financed property:

Return on equity compares:

- Annual cash flow
- Equity invested

Leverage can increase gains and losses.

## Currency-Adjusted Result

When funds originate in USD but the property is bought and sold in COP, calculate:

- COP investment result
- USD investment result
- Exchange-rate effect
- Conversion fees
- Transfer fees

A COP profit may translate into a smaller or larger USD result.

## Records After Sale

After completion:

1. Reconcile all payments.
2. Confirm loan payoff.
3. Confirm registration.

8. Update asset inventory.
9. Update estate records.
10. Record final investment return.
11. Document lessons learned.

## Use-of-Proceeds Plan

Before closing, define how proceeds will be used.

Possible uses:

- Restore emergency reserve
- Pay debt
- Hold tax reserve
- Purchase another property
- Invest through diversified assets
- Fund retirement expenses
- Support family needs
- Hold in COP
- Convert partly to USD
- Maintain short-term liquidity

Avoid making an immediate replacement investment under pressure.

## Sale Proceeds Buckets

3. Emergency reserve

8. Discretionary use

## Post-Sale Review

Answer:

- Was the original thesis correct?
- Were repair costs accurate?
- Was possession delayed?
- Was rental income realistic?
- Were carrying costs underestimated?
- Was the selling period accurate?
- Were taxes understood?
- Was currency exposure helpful or harmful?
- Was the return worth the effort?
- Would the strategy be repeated?

## Stop Conditions

Pause or reject a sale when:

- Buyer identity is unclear
- Proof of funds is unreliable
- Financing is uncertain
- Payment account is inconsistent
- Deposit terms are unclear
- Seller representations are inaccurate
- Mortgage payoff is unresolved
- Title contains unexplained restrictions
- Tax obligations are unknown
- Possession promise cannot be met
- Deed differs materially from the agreement
- Buyer requests unofficial price reporting
- Buyer requests payment outside documented channels
- Closing pressure prevents professional review

## Beginner Rules

1. Define the exit before listing.
2. Calculate expected net proceeds.
3. Verify the buyer and funding.
4. Compare offers by execution risk, not only price.
5. Obtain a current title review.
6. Confirm mortgage payoff in writing.
7. Review the deed before signing.
8. Document possession delivery.
9. Preserve all tax and cost-basis records.
10. Calculate the final economic return honestly.
