# Lesson 9 — Foreclosure and Distressed Property Investing in Colombia

## Learning Objective

Understand how judicial auctions, DIAN auctions, bank-adjudicated properties, and other distressed-property opportunities work in Colombia, including the legal, possession, renovation, liquidity, and financing risks.

## Terminology

In Colombia, relevant terms may include:

- Remate judicial
- Remate virtual
- Inmueble embargado
- Inmueble adjudicado
- Bien recibido en pago
- Propiedad en recuperación
- Cesión de derechos litigiosos
- Adjudicación sin posesión

These structures are not identical.

The legal documents must establish exactly what is being purchased.

## Main Types of Distressed Property

### Judicial auction

A court orders the sale of a property as part of an enforcement, insolvency, mortgage, tax, or other legal process.

### DIAN auction

DIAN may auction property connected with tax-collection proceedings.

### Bank-adjudicated property

A bank or financial institution may become owner after enforcement or payment arrangements and later sell the property.

### Property sold without possession

The purchaser may acquire title but not immediate physical control.

The property may remain occupied by:

- The previous owner
- A tenant
- A family member
- An unauthorized occupant
- A third party claiming rights

### Assignment of rights

Some advertisements sell rights connected to a legal proceeding rather than completed ownership of the property.

This is materially riskier and requires specialized legal analysis.

## Important Principle

A low auction price does not necessarily mean the property is inexpensive.

The true acquisition cost may include:

- Winning bid
- Deposits
- Taxes
- Registration
- Legal representation
- Outstanding administration fees
- Property taxes
- Utilities
- Repairs
- Eviction or possession proceedings
- Security
- Insurance
- Financing costs
- Lost rental income
- Time spent resolving title or occupancy problems

## DIAN Auction Requirements

For DIAN virtual auctions, current official guidance states that a participant generally must:

- Be properly registered in the RUT
- Have the required DIAN bidder designation
- Possess an active DIAN electronic signature
- Keep the registered email address current
- Establish a judicial deposit equal to 40% of the appraised value
- Submit an offer generally not lower than 70% of the appraised value
- Submit the offer within the permitted period

The offer may be irrevocable.

Always verify the current auction notice and official DIAN rules before participating.

## Judicial Auction Documents

Request and review:

- Court case number
- Court or authority conducting the auction
- Auction notice
- Appraisal
- Property identification
- Matrícula inmobiliaria
- Cadastral identification
- Certificate of title and liens
- Seizure or attachment orders
- Mortgage registrations
- Ownership history
- Administration-fee status
- Property-tax status
- Utility status
- Occupancy information
- Possession and delivery provisions
- Auction approval order
- Required payment deadlines

## Certificate of Tradition and Freedom

The Certificado de Tradición y Libertad is central to the legal review.

It can show information such as:

- Registered owner
- Prior transfers
- Mortgages
- Embargoes
- Liens
- Limitations on ownership
- Court orders
- Ownership disputes
- Property-registration history

Obtain it only through the official Superintendencia de Notariado y Registro service.

A general property search is not a substitute for the formal certificate.

## Title Due Diligence

A Colombian real-estate attorney should evaluate:

1. Current registered owner
2. Chain of title
3. Mortgages
4. Embargoes
5. Affected family-home status
6. Patrimonio de familia
7. Usufruct rights
8. Succession claims
9. Litigation
10. Restrictions on transfer
11. Court authority to sell
12. Whether the auction order cancels identified liens
13. What obligations survive the sale
14. Whether registration can be completed
15. Whether possession will be delivered

## Possession Risk

Title and physical possession are different issues.

A purchaser may win an auction yet face delays before occupying, renovating, renting, or selling the property.

Possible costs include:

- Attorney fees
- Court filings
- Bailiff or police coordination
- Locksmith
- Security
- Storage of abandoned property
- Temporary property management
- Vandalism
- Delayed rental income

Do not assume immediate possession unless the official documents clearly establish it.

## Property Inspection Risk

Some distressed properties may not be available for a full interior inspection.

Unknown conditions may include:

- Structural damage
- Water intrusion
- Electrical hazards
- Plumbing failure
- Unauthorized alterations
- Mold
- Pest infestation
- Roof damage
- Missing fixtures
- Occupant damage
- Code violations
- Unpaid utilities
- Incomplete construction

When inspection is unavailable, increase the repair contingency substantially.

## Appraisal Risk

The auction appraisal is not necessarily:

- Current market value
- Renovated value
- Vacant-possession value
- Quick-sale value
- Rental value

Obtain independent evidence through:

- Comparable sales
- Local brokers
- Independent appraisal
- Rental comparables
- Neighborhood vacancy
- Days on market
- Building-condition review
- Planned infrastructure
- Zoning and land-use review

## Maximum Bid Formula

A conservative maximum bid should consider:

- Realistic market value after possession
- Repair costs
- Legal costs
- Carrying costs
- Selling costs
- Desired safety margin
- Desired return

Educational formula:

Maximum bid =
Conservative completed value
minus repairs
minus legal and possession costs
minus taxes and registration
minus financing and carrying costs
minus sale costs
minus required safety margin

Do not bid based only on the percentage discount from the appraisal.

## Renovation Contingency

For a property with full inspection, a contingency may still be required.

For a property without interior inspection, the contingency should generally be larger because major defects may be undiscovered.

Possible budget categories:

- Structural
- Roof
- Electrical
- Plumbing
- Kitchen
- Bathrooms
- Flooring
- Paint
- Windows and doors
- Appliances
- Common-area assessments
- Permits
- Professional fees

## Financing Risk

Auction purchases frequently require funds on short deadlines.

Potential problems include:

- Mortgage financing unavailable
- Appraisal below purchase price
- Bank unwilling to finance occupied property
- Loan approval slower than auction deadlines
- Foreign-income documentation requirements
- Title issues preventing financing
- Need for immediate cash

Do not submit a bid without confirming the funding source and payment timetable.

## Liquidity Risk

Real estate is not a liquid investment.

A distressed property may take longer to sell because of:

- Title history
- Location
- Occupancy
- Condition
- Building problems
- Special assessments
- Limited buyer financing
- Market downturn
- High administration fees

Emergency funds should not be used for auction deposits or renovation.

## Rental Analysis

If the property is intended for rent, calculate:

- Expected gross monthly rent
- Vacancy
- Administration fee
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Maintenance
- Property management
- Repairs
- Legal reserve
- Income tax
- Financing
- Expected net monthly cash flow

Do not rely on gross rent alone.

## Exit Strategies

Before bidding, define at least two realistic exits:

### Hold and rent

Requires:

- Legal possession
- Habitable condition
- Tenant demand
- Positive or acceptable net cash flow

### Renovate and sell

Requires:

- Accurate repair estimate
- Sufficient buyer demand
- Sale costs
- Time allowance
- Tax review

### Personal residence

Requires:

- Location suitability
- Habitability
- Possession
- Renovation affordability
- Long-term ownership plan

### Wholesale or assignment

May involve legal restrictions and should not be attempted without professional advice.

## Red Flags

Avoid or pause when:

- Seller cannot identify the court or official authority
- Case number cannot be verified
- Property matrícula is missing
- Payment is requested to a private person
- Guaranteed profit is promised
- Occupancy status is unclear
- Interior inspection is impossible and no contingency is allowed
- Title contains unexplained annotations
- The transaction sells only litigated rights
- Immediate payment is demanded outside the official process
- The property is advertised far below market without documentation
- The representative discourages independent legal review
- The auction notice is available only through social media
- A supposed official requests cryptocurrency
- The buyer is told possession is automatic without documentary support

## Due-Diligence Team

Before purchasing, consider retaining:

- Colombian real-estate attorney
- Accountant or tax adviser
- Independent appraiser
- Structural inspector
- Contractor
- Local broker
- Property manager
- Title and registration specialist

The attorney should represent the buyer, not the auction promoter.

## Personal Suitability Questions

1. Would the purchase use emergency savings?
2. Can I fund the entire transaction within the required deadline?
3. Can I absorb a six- to twelve-month possession delay?
4. Can I afford major repairs?
5. Do I have access to a trusted Colombian property attorney?
6. Would the property be for residence, rent, or resale?
7. How concentrated would my wealth become?
8. What happens if the property produces no income for one year?
9. Can I manage litigation or occupant issues?
10. Is this more suitable than a diversified financial investment?

## Beginner Rules

1. Never bid without reviewing the complete official case file.
2. Obtain a current Certificate of Tradition and Freedom.
3. Use an independent Colombian attorney.
4. Confirm possession status in writing.
5. Assume repair costs are higher when inspection is limited.
6. Calculate total cost, not just auction price.
7. Confirm funding before bidding.
8. Keep emergency reserves separate.
9. Do not rely on promised discounts or profit projections.
10. Be willing to walk away.
