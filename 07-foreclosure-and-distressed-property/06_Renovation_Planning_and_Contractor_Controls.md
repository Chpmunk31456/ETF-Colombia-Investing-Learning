# Lesson 24 — Renovation Planning, Contractor Controls, and Construction Budget Management

## Learning Objective

Learn how to define renovation scope, select contractors, control payments, document changes, manage timelines, preserve evidence, and reduce the risk of budget overruns, defective work, fraud, and unfinished construction.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

It does not replace advice from a qualified Colombian attorney, architect, engineer, licensed contractor, property administrator, insurer, tax adviser, or municipal authority.

## Core Principle

A renovation should not begin with demolition.

It should begin with:

- Verified ownership or possession rights
- Written scope
- Technical review
- Budget
- Contingency
- Permits
- Building approval
- Insurance review
- Contractor verification
- Payment controls
- Completion standards

## Renovation Objectives

Define the primary purpose.

Possible objectives include:

- Make property habitable
- Correct safety defects
- Prepare for personal occupancy
- Prepare for long-term rental
- Improve resale value
- Reduce maintenance
- Improve accessibility
- Improve energy efficiency
- Correct unauthorized construction
- Repair damage from prior occupants

Do not combine unnecessary cosmetic work with urgent safety work without prioritization.

## Priority Categories

### Priority 3 — Functional repairs

Examples:

- Kitchen

### Priority 4 — Optional improvements

Examples:

- Premium finishes

Safety and legal requirements should be funded before optional improvements.

## Contingency

A contingency covers unknown conditions and changes.

Educational ranges may include:

- Limited cosmetic work: 10% to 15%
- Moderate renovation: 15% to 25%
- Older or distressed property: 25% to 35%
- No interior inspection before purchase: potentially more

The appropriate contingency depends on professional evaluation.

## Payment Controls

Avoid paying the full price in advance.

Possible milestone structure:

- Small mobilization payment
- Payment after demolition completion
- Payment after rough plumbing and electrical
- Payment after walls and waterproofing
- Payment after installation
- Payment after final inspection
- Retention until defects are corrected

The exact structure should reflect the project.

## Mobilization Payment

A mobilization payment may fund:

- Initial materials
- Equipment
- Worker setup
- Building deposits
- Transportation

Before paying:

- Verify recipient
- Confirm contract
- Confirm amount
- Confirm use
- Obtain receipt
- Record payment
- Confirm start date

## Retention

Retention is an amount withheld until:

- Work is complete
- Defects are corrected
- Final documents are delivered
- Cleanup is complete
- Warranties are received

The contract should define:

- Retention percentage
- Release conditions
- Release date
- Dispute process

## Materials Purchasing

Possible arrangements include:

### Contractor purchases materials

Advantages:

- Simpler coordination

Risks:

- Markup

### Owner purchases materials

Advantages:

- Greater price and quality control

Risks:

- Storage

- Warranty complexity

## Change Orders

A change order modifies:

- Scope
- Price
- Materials
- Schedule
- Design
- Responsibility

Every change order should include:

- Change number

- Cost
- Time impact

- Approval date

Verbal changes create disputes.

## Change-Order Rule

Do not approve a change until:

- Need is verified
- Cost is documented
- Schedule impact is understood
- Alternative options are reviewed
- Contingency balance is updated
- Written approval is issued

## Over-Improvement Risk

A renovation may exceed what the location or market supports.

Warning signs:

- Finishes far above comparable properties
- Layout too specialized
- Luxury upgrades in low-rent market
- Renovation cost exceeds value increase
- Personal preferences reduce buyer appeal
- High-cost materials with low resale effect

## Renovation Payback

Simple payback period equals:

Total renovation cost
divided by annual increase in net income

This does not account for:

- Financing
- Taxes
- Time value of money
- Future repairs
- Property-value changes

## Stop Conditions

Stop or pause when:

- Ownership or possession is unresolved
- Structural risk is not evaluated
- Required approvals are missing
- Contractor identity is unclear
- Contractor demands full advance payment
- Bank account does not match contract party
- Scope is vague
- No written change process exists
- Budget has no contingency
- Emergency reserve would be used
- Unauthorized work is proposed
- Contractor refuses inspection
- Hidden work is covered before documentation
- Material substitutions occur without approval
- Schedule and cost no longer support the investment objective

## Beginner Rules

1. Define scope before obtaining bids.
2. Correct safety and legal issues first.
3. Verify contractors independently.
4. Use a written contract.
5. Avoid full payment in advance.
6. Tie payments to verified milestones.
7. Require written change orders.
8. Photograph hidden work.
9. Maintain contingency and reserves.
10. Release final payment only after completion review.
