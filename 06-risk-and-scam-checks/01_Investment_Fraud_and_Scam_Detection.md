# Lesson 15 — Investment Fraud, Scam Detection, and Verification

## Learning Objective

Learn how to identify common investment scams, verify financial institutions and property opportunities, protect personal information, and stop a transaction before money is lost.

## Important Principle

Fraud prevention begins before money is transferred.

Once funds are sent through:

- Cryptocurrency
- Wire transfer
- Cash
- Payment to a personal account
- Unregulated platform
- Irreversible payment method

Recovery may be difficult or impossible.

## Common Investment Scam Types

### Guaranteed-return schemes

The promoter promises:

- Fixed high monthly returns
- No risk
- Capital protection
- Guaranteed profits
- Immediate withdrawals
- Exclusive access

High returns without meaningful risk are a major warning sign.

### Fake broker or investment platform

The website or app may imitate:

- A legitimate broker
- A bank
- A regulator
- A well-known investment company
- A cryptocurrency exchange

The platform may display false profits while preventing withdrawals.

### Recovery scam

After an earlier loss, a second scammer claims to recover the money in exchange for:

- Advance fee
- Tax payment
- Legal deposit
- Blockchain validation fee
- Release charge

A legitimate recovery process should be independently verified.

### Romance or relationship investment scam

The fraudster builds trust before recommending:

- Cryptocurrency
- Foreign exchange
- Trading platform
- Private investment
- High-yield opportunity

The emotional relationship is used to reduce skepticism.

### Affinity fraud

The promoter gains trust through a shared:

- Religion
- Profession
- Nationality
- Community
- Family connection
- Social organization

Shared identity is not evidence of legitimacy.

### Ponzi scheme

Existing participants are paid using money from newer participants rather than genuine investment profits.

Common signs:

- Consistent returns
- Few or no losing periods
- Referral incentives
- Pressure to reinvest
- Delayed withdrawals
- Unclear underlying assets

### Pump-and-dump scheme

A promoter encourages purchases of a thinly traded asset, then sells after the price rises.

Possible targets include:

- Small company shares
- Cryptocurrency tokens
- Low-volume securities
- Thematic investments
- Private offerings

### AI trading scam

The promoter claims an AI system can:

- Predict markets accurately
- Trade without losses
- Generate guaranteed income
- Eliminate volatility
- Produce passive daily profit

Artificial intelligence does not remove market risk.

### Property fraud

Common property-related schemes include:

- False auction listings
- Fake judicial remates
- Duplicate property sales
- Nonexistent properties
- Forged ownership records
- Fake deposits
- Sale of rights presented as full ownership
- Payment requests outside official channels
- False foreclosure discounts

## Behavioral Red Flags

Pause immediately when someone:

- Creates urgency
- Says the opportunity is secret
- Discourages independent advice
- Claims regulators do not understand the product
- Pressures same-day payment
- Uses emotional manipulation
- Promises guaranteed profits
- Says risk disclosure is unnecessary
- Requests remote computer access
- Refuses written documentation
- Asks for secrecy
- Claims everyone else is already profiting
- Becomes hostile when questioned

## Payment Red Flags

Do not send money when payment is requested through:

- Personal bank account
- Cryptocurrency wallet
- Gift card
- Cash courier
- Unrelated company
- Unknown payment processor
- Foreign account with no documented relationship
- Multiple accounts under different names

The receiving account should match the verified legal entity.

## Identity Verification

Before investing, confirm:

- Full legal entity name
- Registration number
- Physical address
- Official website
- Official email domain
- Telephone number
- Regulator
- License or authorization
- Directors or representatives
- Custodian
- Receiving bank account

Do not rely solely on documents supplied by the promoter.

## Website Verification

Check:

- Domain spelling
- Domain age
- Secure connection
- Contact information
- Legal disclosures
- Privacy policy
- Regulatory information
- Corporate registration
- Broken links
- Copied text
- Unusual grammar
- Pressure messages
- Fake testimonials

Scammers may use domains that differ by one letter from legitimate websites.

## Email Verification

Warning signs include:

- Free email account
- Misspelled company domain
- Urgent payment request
- Changed bank instructions
- Unexpected attachment
- Link-shortening service
- Request for password or MFA code
- Request to bypass normal procedures

Verify payment instructions using a known official telephone number.

## Social Media Verification

Do not treat the following as proof:

- Follower count
- Verified-looking badge
- Celebrity image
- Testimonials
- Profit screenshots
- Lifestyle photographs
- Private messaging group
- Referral code
- Live-stream presentation

Screenshots and account identities can be fabricated.

## Broker Verification Process

Before opening an account:

1. Identify the legal entity.
2. Verify the regulator independently.
3. Confirm the exact license.
4. Confirm the authorized business activity.
5. Check disciplinary history.
6. Verify the official website through the regulator.
7. Review custody arrangements.
8. Read the fee schedule.
9. Test a small deposit.
10. Test a small withdrawal.

## Property Verification Process

Before paying a deposit:

1. Confirm the matrícula inmobiliaria.
2. Obtain a current Certificate of Tradition and Freedom.
3. Verify the registered owner.
4. Confirm the official court or authority.
5. Verify the case number.
6. Review the official auction notice.
7. Confirm the receiving account.
8. Use an independent attorney.
9. Inspect the property where possible.
10. Document possession status.

## AI Verification Rules

When AI is used for research:

- Verify names
- Verify dates
- Verify prices
- Verify regulations
- Verify tax thresholds
- Verify court information
- Verify official links
- Verify calculations

AI-generated content may be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect.

## Scam Risk Scoring

Use a 0-to-3 score for each category:

- 0 = No concern identified
- 1 = Minor concern
- 2 = Significant concern
- 3 = Severe concern

| Category | Score |
|---|---:|
| Guaranteed-return claim |  |
| Urgency or pressure |  |
| Regulatory uncertainty |  |
| Legal entity uncertainty |  |
| Unusual payment method |  |
| Withdrawal restrictions |  |
| Unverified testimonials |  |
| Remote-access request |  |
| Missing documentation |  |
| Inconsistent identity |  |

Interpretation:

- 0 to 4: Continue verification
- 5 to 9: High caution
- 10 or more: Stop and obtain independent review
- Any request for passwords, MFA codes, or remote access: Stop immediately

## Stop-Transaction Procedure

When fraud is suspected:

1. Do not send additional money.
2. Do not disclose passwords.
3. Do not approve MFA requests.
4. Save all communications.
5. Save account numbers and wallet addresses.
6. Capture screenshots.
7. Contact the legitimate financial institution.
8. Change compromised passwords.
9. Revoke API keys.
10. Report the incident to the appropriate authority.

## Evidence Preservation

Retain:

- Emails
- Chat messages
- Phone numbers
- Names
- Websites
- Domain names
- Payment instructions
- Bank account details
- Cryptocurrency addresses
- Receipts
- Contracts
- Advertisements
- Screenshots
- Call dates and times

Do not alter original evidence.

## Data Protection

Never provide:

- Password
- MFA code
- Recovery phrase
- Private key
- Full card PIN
- Remote desktop access
- Brokerage API key with withdrawal permission
- Identity documents without verifying the recipient
- Unnecessary banking details

## Family Protection Plan

Discuss scams with household members.

Establish rules such as:

- No investment payment without written review
- No remote-access software
- No cryptocurrency transfer under pressure
- No lending money to recover an investment
- No sharing MFA codes
- No payment to unknown personal accounts
- Second-person review for large transfers

## Beginner Rules

1. Never trust guaranteed returns.
2. Verify the legal entity independently.
3. Verify the regulator independently.
4. Never send money to a personal account for an investment.
5. Never share passwords or MFA codes.
6. Never allow remote computer access.
7. Test withdrawals before increasing deposits.
8. Preserve evidence.
9. Use a second-person review for large transactions.
10. Stop immediately when identity or payment details are inconsistent.
