# Lesson 41 — Financial Professional, Adviser, and Service-Provider Due Diligence

## Learning Objective

Learn how to identify, verify, compare, approve, monitor, and terminate financial professionals and service providers whose work may affect investments, property, taxes, legal rights, account security, or household finances.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

Licensing, registration, professional duties, complaint procedures, insurance requirements, fiduciary standards, and consumer protections vary by profession and jurisdiction.

Verify current requirements through official sources and obtain independent professional advice when the engagement is material.

## Core Principle

A professional title, referral, polished website, prestigious employer, or persuasive presentation does not prove:

- Legal authority
- Current licensing
- Relevant competence
- Independence
- Adequate insurance
- Appropriate scope
- Honest compensation
- Secure handling of information
- Suitability for the assignment

Trust should follow verification rather than replace it.

## Provider Role

Define the exact role before searching.

Examples:

- Prepare a tax return
- Provide cross-border tax advice

- Conduct a title review
- Inspect a property
- Manage a rental
- Recommend investments

- Prepare an appraisal

A vague assignment makes competence and accountability difficult to evaluate.

- Fees

- Dependencies
- Confidentiality
- Data access

- Dispute process

## Professional Identity

Verify:

- Full legal name
- Business name
- Professional title
- Physical or registered address
- Official telephone number
- Official email domain
- Government or professional identifier where applicable
- Employer or firm affiliation
- Authorized representatives

Do not rely solely on information supplied through messaging applications.

## Independent Verification

Verify information through sources independent of the provider.

Possible sources include:

- Official professional registry
- Government business registry
- Regulator
- Professional association
- Court or disciplinary record where lawfully available
- Insurer
- Employer directory
- verified institutional website
- prior client references
- official property or corporate records

## Identity-Matching Control

Confirm that the following refer to the same person or entity:

- Contract name
- Invoice name
- Bank-account beneficiary
- Professional registration
- email domain
- business registration
- insurance certificate
- tax documentation

A mismatch requires explanation before payment.

## Licensing and Registration

Where a profession requires licensing or registration, confirm:

- Registration number
- Legal name
- Current status
- Authorized activities
- Jurisdiction
- Effective date
- Expiration date
- Restrictions
- disciplinary status
- verification source
- verification date

Do not assume a foreign credential authorizes local practice.

## Professional Competence

Evaluate whether the provider has experience relevant to the assignment.

Possible evidence includes:

- Education
- Current credentials
- relevant projects
- years of applicable experience
- publications
- references
- work samples
- technical explanation
- continuing education
- professional affiliations

Years of experience alone do not prove competence in the specific matter.

## Specialized Competence

Examples include:

- Cross-border taxation
- Colombian real-estate title
- US retirement accounts
- estate planning across jurisdictions
- rental-property management
- judicial auction procedures
- structural inspection
- renovation cost estimation
- investment-product analysis
- financial fraud response

Confirm exact specialization rather than broad professional category.

## Credential Verification

For each claimed credential, record:

- Credential
- Issuing organization
- Identifier
- Issue date
- expiration date
- current status
- verification source
- permitted scope
- continuing requirements

Certificates of attendance should not be treated as professional licenses.

## References

Request references appropriate to the assignment.

Ask references:

- What work was completed?
- Was the scope clear?
- Were deadlines met?
- Were costs controlled?
- Were problems disclosed?
- Was communication reliable?
- Were documents handled securely?
- Was the final work usable?
- Would the provider be engaged again?

Do not request or retain unnecessary confidential information about prior clients.

## Online Reviews

Online reviews may provide leads but can be:

- Fabricated
- selectively requested
- outdated
- unrelated to the proposed service
- emotionally exaggerated
- posted by competitors
- based on different staff

Use reviews as one input, not decisive proof.

## Complaints and Discipline

Where legally and practically available, review:

- Formal disciplinary actions
- Registration restrictions
- regulatory warnings
- litigation relevant to professional conduct
- unresolved complaints
- repeated billing disputes
- fraud allegations
- sanctions
- bankruptcy or business closure where relevant

Distinguish verified findings from unproven allegations.

## Conflicts of Interest

A conflict exists when the provider’s interests may compete with the client’s interests.

Examples:

- Adviser receives product commission
- Broker is paid by the seller
- Property agent represents both parties
- Inspector is recommended and paid by contractor
- Appraiser depends on transaction completion
- Attorney represents multiple parties
- Tax preparer receives referral compensation
- Property manager selects affiliated contractors
- Mortgage intermediary is paid by lender

A disclosed conflict may still require mitigation or rejection.

## Conflict Questions

Ask:

- Who pays you?
- Do you receive commissions?
- Do you receive referral compensation?
- Are you affiliated with the provider you recommend?
- Do you represent another party?
- Do you receive incentives for transaction completion?
- Do you manage client assets?
- Can you recommend alternatives?
- Will all compensation be disclosed in writing?

Total expected cost may include:

- Professional fee

## Fee Estimate

A fee estimate should identify:

- Included work
- excluded work
- assumptions
- hourly rates
- estimated hours
- fixed-fee conditions
- tax
- third-party costs
- payment dates
- refundability
- expiration

## Low-Fee Risk

An unusually low fee may indicate:

- Incomplete scope
- inexperienced provider
- later change orders
- automated work without review
- inadequate insurance
- referral compensation
- data monetization
- scam

Price should be evaluated with competence, scope, and risk.

Before material work begins, obtain a written agreement addressing:

- Parties
- scope
- fees
- deliverables
- timeline
- responsibilities
- confidentiality
- data security
- intellectual property
- conflicts
- subcontractors
- insurance

- dispute process
- termination

## Deliverables

Deliverables should be objective where possible.

Examples:

- Written tax memorandum
- completed filing package
- title opinion
- inspection report
- appraisal
- renovation scope
- monthly property statement
- investment recommendation
- security assessment
- signed professional certification

## Advice Versus Execution

Clarify whether the provider will:

- Educate
- Recommend
- decide
- execute
- hold money
- access accounts
- file documents
- negotiate
- sign
- supervise
- certify

Advice authority should not be confused with transaction authority.

## Custody and Control

Determine whether the provider can:

- Hold client money
- withdraw money
- initiate transfers
- trade securities
- change beneficiaries
- receive rent
- pay contractors
- access tax accounts
- access email
- store identity documents
- sign on behalf of the client

Greater authority requires stronger verification and monitoring.

## Least-Privilege Principle

Provide only the minimum access required.

Examples:

- Read-only statement instead of login credentials
- redacted document instead of full account statement
- temporary file access instead of permanent access
- transaction-specific authority instead of general authority
- limited property-management account instead of personal banking access

## Credential-Sharing Prohibition

Do not provide a provider with:

- Password
- MFA code
- password-manager access
- full email access
- security answers
- recovery codes

Use authorized institutional delegation where available.

## Sensitive-Data Handling

Before sending sensitive information, ask:

- Why is it required?
- Is a redacted copy sufficient?
- How will it be transmitted?
- Where will it be stored?
- Who can access it?
- Will subcontractors receive it?
- How long will it be retained?
- How will it be deleted?
- Is encryption used?
- What happens after termination?

## Confidentiality

Confidentiality terms should address:

- Financial information
- identification documents
- medical information
- tax records
- property records
- family information
- business information
- communications
- work product

Professional confidentiality duties vary and should be verified.

## Cybersecurity Review

For providers receiving sensitive data, review:

- Secure portal
- encryption
- MFA
- access control
- backup
- breach notification
- subcontractor access
- retention
- deletion
- incident-response process
- cyber insurance where relevant

Ordinary email may be inappropriate for highly sensitive documents.

## Professional Insurance

Where relevant, request evidence of:

- Professional liability
- errors and omissions
- general liability
- cyber insurance
- worker injury coverage
- vehicle coverage
- contractor coverage

Verify:

- Named insured
- insurer
- effective dates
- limits
- covered activity
- exclusions
- certificate authenticity

A certificate does not replace review of relevant policy terms.

## Financial Stability

For long-term or high-value providers, consider:

- Years in business
- business registration
- staffing
- continuity
- dependency on one individual
- financial distress indicators
- unpaid subcontractors
- unresolved legal problems
- business insurance
- succession plan

## Subcontractors

Require disclosure of:

- Subcontractor identity
- role
- qualifications
- access to data
- insurance
- compensation
- supervision
- location
- confidentiality obligations

The primary provider should remain accountable for contracted deliverables unless the agreement states otherwise.

## Outsourcing and Automation

Ask whether work will be:

- Performed personally
- delegated
- outsourced internationally
- generated by software
- supported by AI
- reviewed by a qualified professional

Automated assistance should not be presented as independent professional judgment without review.

## Artificial Intelligence Use

When AI may be used, confirm:

- What information is entered
- whether data trains external systems
- whether personal information is removed
- how outputs are verified
- who is professionally accountable
- whether confidential material is permitted
- how errors are identified

Do not send sensitive data into an unapproved AI service.

## Bank-Account Verification

Before payment, verify:

- Beneficiary legal name
- Bank
- account type
- currency
- account identifier
- source of instructions
- relationship to provider
- reason for any mismatch

Unexpected payment changes require independent callback verification.

## Payment Controls

Possible controls include:

- Written invoice
- contract reference
- milestone approval
- verified beneficiary
- small initial payment
- retention until acceptance
- receipt
- payment register
- second-person review
- no cash payment without documented reason

## Advance Payment

Evaluate:

- Percentage requested
- materials required
- provider reputation
- recoverability
- milestone protection
- escrow or controlled payment where available
- refund terms
- proof of expenditure

A large nonrefundable advance increases risk.

## Milestone Payments

Tie payments to measurable events.

Examples:

- Contract signed
- document review complete
- draft delivered
- inspection completed
- materials delivered
- milestone accepted
- final report issued
- defects resolved

## Change Order

A change order should document:

- Original scope
- requested change
- reason
- added cost
- schedule effect
- risks
- approval
- revised completion date

Do not approve repeated informal increases through messaging alone.

## Work-Product Review

Before acceptance, check:

- Scope completed
- facts supported
- names correct
- dates correct
- calculations correct
- assumptions identified
- limitations disclosed
- required signatures present
- source documents returned
- unresolved issues listed
- format usable

## Independent Second Opinion

A second opinion may be appropriate when:

- Financial exposure is high
- advice is irreversible
- provider has a conflict
- recommendation is unusual
- tax or legal positions are aggressive
- property title is complex
- major surgery or health decisions affect finances
- one provider controls multiple stages
- prior errors occurred

## Provider Comparison

Possible criteria include:

## Weighted Scoring

Use a scoring model only after mandatory conditions are satisfied.

A high score cannot override:

- Invalid license
- identity mismatch
- fraud warning
- undisclosed conflict
- inability to provide contract
- insecure credential request
- unverified payment destination

## Mandatory Requirements

Possible mandatory requirements include:

- Identity verified
- legal status verified
- required license active
- written scope
- written fees
- conflict disclosure
- secure data method
- verified payment account
- acceptable insurance
- no critical disciplinary concern

## Provider Risk Levels

### Low

- Limited scope
- low financial exposure
- no account access
- no sensitive data
- easily reversible

### Moderate

- Sensitive documents
- material advice
- recurring payment
- moderate financial effect

### High

- Account or transaction authority
- large payment
- property control
- tax filing
- legal representation
- renovation supervision
- identity-document access

### Critical

- Custody of money
- broad power of attorney
- unrestricted account access
- ability to change beneficiaries
- control over property-sale proceeds
- unexplained payment structure
- unresolved identity or license concern

Examples include:

## Issue Escalation

For a material issue:

1. Preserve evidence.
2. stop additional payment where appropriate.
3. restrict access.
4. request written explanation.
5. define corrective action.
6. establish deadline.
7. obtain second opinion.
8. notify insurer or authority where appropriate.
9. terminate if risk remains unacceptable.
10. recover records and access.

## Provider Suspension

Suspend new work when:

- License cannot be verified
- insurance expired
- material deadline missed
- unexplained invoice appears
- access exceeds authorization
- security concern exists
- conflict was not disclosed
- provider refuses documentation
- payment instructions change unexpectedly
- work contains material errors

## Termination

A termination process should address:

- Written notice

- revocation of access

## Access Revocation

After termination:

- Remove portal access

- change shared secrets

## Provider Fraud Warning Signs

Stop when a provider:

- Refuses legal identity
- claims unverifiable credentials
- requests payment to unrelated person
- requests credentials or MFA codes
- guarantees investment returns
- guarantees legal or tax outcome
- discourages second opinions
- applies extreme urgency
- refuses written scope
- hides compensation
- uses copied documents
- changes payment instructions without verification
- requests secrecy from spouse or professionals
- offers to falsify information
- recommends evading reporting duties

## Professional Advice Register

For material advice, record:

- Provider
- qualification
- question
- facts supplied
- documents reviewed
- advice date
- conclusion
- limitations
- reliance decision
- follow-up
- expiration or reassessment date

Advice can become stale when facts or rules change.

## Reliance Limits

Do not rely on advice outside:

- Provider’s licensed scope
- agreed engagement
- stated assumptions
- relevant jurisdiction
- effective time period
- provider’s technical competence

## Cross-Border Coordination

Cross-border matters may require separate professionals for:

- US tax
- Colombian tax
- immigration
- estate planning
- property
- securities
- pensions

One professional may coordinate but should not claim competence in every jurisdiction without evidence.

## Contradictory Advice

When professionals disagree:

1. Confirm they received the same facts.
2. identify the exact point of disagreement.
3. request supporting authority or reasoning.
4. review jurisdiction and scope.
5. obtain a qualified second opinion.
6. document the final decision.
7. preserve uncertainty where unresolved.

## Decision Responsibility

Professional advice supports but does not automatically replace the household’s responsibility to:

- Understand the decision
- verify scope
- evaluate affordability
- preserve records
- disclose complete facts
- question inconsistency
- avoid prohibited conduct

## Annual Provider Review

Review:

- Current status
- license
- insurance
- conflicts
- scope
- fees
- performance
- data access
- retained documents
- complaints
- continued need
- replacement options

## Automatic Stop Conditions

Do not engage, pay, or continue when:

- Legal identity is unresolved
- Required license is inactive
- Contracting party differs without explanation
- Payment beneficiary is unrelated
- Provider requests passwords or MFA codes
- Fees or compensation are concealed
- Work requires falsifying information
- Material conflict is undisclosed
- Sensitive data method is unacceptable
- Insurance is required but cannot be verified
- Professional refuses written scope
- Prior material errors remain unresolved
- Independent review is discouraged

## Beginner Rules

1. Define the exact assignment before selecting a provider.
2. Verify identity and credentials independently.
3. Use written scope and fees.
4. Identify all compensation and conflicts.
5. Limit access to the minimum necessary.
6. Never share passwords or MFA codes.
7. Verify payment instructions independently.
8. Tie payments to measurable milestones.
9. Monitor recurring providers annually.
10. Suspend work when mandatory requirements fail.
