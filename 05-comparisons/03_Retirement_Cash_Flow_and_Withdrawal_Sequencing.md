# Lesson 19 — Retirement Cash Flow, Income Buckets, and Withdrawal Sequencing

## Learning Objective

Learn how to coordinate pension income, employment income, cash reserves, CDTs, ETF distributions, and property income so household expenses can be paid without unnecessary investment sales or excessive risk.

## Important Principle

A retirement portfolio is not only an investment portfolio.

It is also a cash-flow system.

The system should answer:

- What income arrives each month?
- Which expenses must be paid in COP?
- Which obligations remain in USD?
- How much income is reliable?
- How much income is variable?
- Which assets will fund a shortfall?
- Which assets should not be sold during a market decline?
- How much liquidity is required?

## Income Sources

List all current and possible income sources.

Examples:

- US Social Security
- Employer salary
- Consulting income
- Colombian pension income
- CDT interest
- Bond interest
- ETF dividends
- Stock dividends
- Rental income
- Property-sale proceeds
- Business distributions
- Cash-account interest

Do not count uncertain income until it is received or contractually reliable.

## Income Classification

Classify each source as:

### Reliable recurring income

Examples:

- Pension
- Social Security
- Fixed contractual payment

### Variable recurring income

Examples:

- Consulting income
- Rental income
- Dividends
- Business income

### Irregular income

Examples:

- Bonus
- Property sale
- Investment gain
- One-time project payment

### Capital withdrawal

Examples:

- Selling ETF shares
- Redeeming fixed income
- Spending principal
- Selling property

Capital withdrawals are not the same as income.

## Essential Versus Discretionary Expenses

### Essential expenses

Examples:

- Housing
- Food
- Utilities
- Medical care
- Insurance
- Transportation
- Taxes
- Debt payments
- Family support obligations

### Discretionary expenses

Examples:

- Travel
- Dining out
- Entertainment
- Luxury purchases
- Optional vehicle upgrades
- Nonessential renovations

Reliable income should ideally cover most essential expenses.

## Monthly Cash-Flow Formula

Monthly surplus or deficit equals:

Total monthly net income
minus total monthly expenses

A positive result creates investable surplus.

A negative result requires:

- Expense reduction
- Additional income
- Planned cash withdrawal
- Investment sale
- Currency conversion
- Use of reserves

## Gross Versus Net Income

Record income after:

- Tax withholding
- Transfer fees
- Currency conversion
- Bank fees
- Brokerage fees
- Property expenses
- Management fees

The amount available for spending is the net amount received.

## COP and USD Cash Flow

A Colombia-based household may receive income in USD while spending primarily in COP.

Track separately:

- USD income
- COP income
- USD obligations
- COP obligations
- Conversion amount
- Conversion rate
- Conversion cost
- Timing of conversion

## Currency-Matching Rule

Near-term expenses should generally be funded in the currency in which they will be paid.

Examples:

- Colombian rent and groceries require COP
- US tax payment may require USD
- International travel may require USD or another currency
- Colombian vehicle payments require COP

This reduces the risk of being forced to convert currency at an unfavorable moment.

## Currency Reserve

Consider maintaining:

- A COP operating reserve for local expenses
- A USD reserve for US obligations
- A separate emergency reserve
- A planned conversion schedule

The appropriate amounts depend on household needs.

## Income Bucket 1 — Monthly Operating Cash

Purpose:

- Current-month expenses
- Automatic payments
- Routine household costs

Possible location:

- Checking or savings account

Target:

- Approximately one to two months of normal spending

## Income Bucket 2 — Emergency Reserve

Purpose:

- Medical emergency
- Income interruption
- Family emergency
- Major repair
- Unexpected travel
- Legal expense

Possible target:

- Several months of essential expenses

The target should reflect:

- Health risk
- Dependents
- Income reliability
- Insurance
- Access to credit
- Employment uncertainty

## Income Bucket 3 — Near-Term Spending Reserve

Purpose:

- Expenses expected during the next one to three years

Possible holdings:

- Savings
- Short CDTs
- CDT ladder
- Short-duration fixed income
- Other low-volatility products

This bucket can reduce the need to sell equities during market declines.

## Income Bucket 4 — Long-Term Growth

Purpose:

- Inflation protection
- Long-term purchasing power
- Future family support
- Later-life expenses

Possible holdings:

- Broad equity ETFs
- Diversified long-term investments

This bucket should not normally fund routine monthly spending when markets are depressed.

## Income Bucket 5 — Illiquid Assets

Examples:

- Direct property
- Distressed property
- Private investments
- Long-term restricted funds

These assets should not be relied upon for immediate household cash flow.

## Withdrawal Sequencing

Withdrawal sequencing determines which source is used first when income is insufficient.

A general educational sequence may be:

1. Current income
2. Operating cash
3. Interest and dividends
4. Maturing short-term fixed income
5. Planned reserve withdrawals
6. Portfolio rebalancing sales
7. Long-term asset sales
8. Property sale as a last planned liquidity event

The correct sequence depends on tax, legal, and personal circumstances.

## Use Income Before Principal

Where practical, use:

- Pension income
- Interest
- Dividends
- Rental income
- Contract income

before selling long-term assets.

However, chasing high yield solely to avoid selling principal can increase risk.

## Total Return Approach

A total-return approach considers:

- Interest
- Dividends
- Capital appreciation
- Planned sales
- Taxes
- Fees

A portfolio does not need to produce all spending through dividends.

Selling a controlled amount of a diversified investment may be more rational than owning a concentrated high-yield product.

## Sequence-of-Returns Risk

Sequence-of-returns risk occurs when major investment losses happen early during a withdrawal period.

Example:

- Portfolio declines
- Investor still needs monthly withdrawals
- Shares are sold at depressed prices
- Fewer shares remain for recovery

Possible controls:

- Emergency reserve
- Short-term spending bucket
- Flexible discretionary spending
- Reliable pension income
- Diversified assets
- Lower withdrawal needs during market stress

## Withdrawal Rate

A withdrawal rate is:

Annual portfolio withdrawals
divided by beginning portfolio value

Example:

- Beginning portfolio: COP 500,000,000
- Annual withdrawal: COP 20,000,000
- Withdrawal rate: 4%

This is a measurement, not a guaranteed safe rate.

A suitable withdrawal level depends on:

- Age
- Health
- Life expectancy
- Income reliability
- Portfolio allocation
- Inflation
- Fees
- Taxes
- Family goals
- Market conditions

## Dynamic Withdrawals

A dynamic withdrawal policy changes spending when circumstances change.

Possible rules:

- Reduce discretionary withdrawals after major market losses
- Increase withdrawals only after portfolio recovery
- Use fixed income during equity declines
- Replenish cash after strong market periods
- Review annually rather than monthly

## Floor-and-Upside Structure

A floor-and-upside approach separates:

### Income floor

Reliable resources intended to cover essential expenses.

Examples:

- Pension
- Social Security
- Cash reserve
- Short-term fixed income

### Upside portfolio

Assets intended for:

- Growth
- Inflation protection
- Discretionary spending
- Legacy goals

This structure can reduce pressure to sell volatile assets.

## Dividend Income

Dividend income may vary.

Risks include:

- Dividend cuts
- Company concentration
- Sector concentration
- Foreign withholding
- Currency changes
- High-yield traps

Track both:

- Distribution income
- Total return

## CDT Income

CDTs may provide predictable maturity proceeds, but consider:

- Reinvestment risk
- Inflation
- Withholding
- Liquidity
- Maturity concentration
- Institution concentration

A ladder can help distribute maturities over time.

## Property Income

Rental income should be recorded after:

- Vacancy
- Administration fees
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Repairs
- Management
- Legal expense
- Financing
- Income tax

Gross rent is not spendable net income.

## Distressed Property Cash Flow

Distressed property may initially produce negative cash flow because of:

- No possession
- Legal delay
- Repairs
- Security
- Administration debt
- Property taxes
- No tenant
- Financing costs

Do not include projected rent until possession and habitability are established.

## Medical and Long-Term Care Reserve

A retirement plan should consider:

- Insurance premiums
- Copayments
- Medications
- Dental care
- Vision care
- Home assistance
- Mobility equipment
- Caregiver support
- Major procedures
- Travel for treatment

Medical reserves should remain liquid and accessible.

## Family Support Policy

Document:

- Regular family support
- Temporary support
- Education expenses
- Medical assistance
- Maximum monthly amount
- Maximum annual amount
- Conditions for exceptions

Unplanned family support can materially change investment sustainability.

## Vehicle and Property Purchases

Large purchases should have separate funding plans.

Do not assume investment growth will fund a purchase on a fixed date.

For each planned purchase, record:

- Target date
- Expected cost
- Currency
- Down payment
- Financing
- Monthly payment
- Insurance
- Taxes
- Maintenance
- Effect on emergency reserves

## Cash-Flow Stress Tests

### Scenario 1 — Employment income stops

Calculate:

- Monthly shortfall
- Months covered by pension
- Months covered by reserves
- Expenses that can be reduced
- Investments that would remain untouched

### Scenario 2 — COP strengthens

Calculate:

- Reduction in COP received from USD income
- Effect on household budget
- Required reserve adjustment

### Scenario 3 — Major medical expense

Calculate:

- Immediate cash required
- Insurance coverage
- Reserve available
- Assets that can be accessed safely

### Scenario 4 — Rental income stops

Calculate:

- Lost net income
- Property carrying cost
- Duration reserves can cover the loss

### Scenario 5 — Equity market falls 30%

Calculate:

- Portfolio decline
- Planned withdrawals
- Ability to avoid equity sales
- Discretionary expenses that can be delayed

## Withdrawal Decision Checklist

Before selling an investment:

- [ ] Purpose of withdrawal documented
- [ ] Amount required confirmed
- [ ] Currency required confirmed
- [ ] Operating cash reviewed
- [ ] Emergency reserve reviewed
- [ ] Maturing fixed income reviewed
- [ ] Tax consequences reviewed
- [ ] Trading costs reviewed
- [ ] Portfolio allocation reviewed
- [ ] Market conditions considered without attempting prediction
- [ ] Alternative funding sources reviewed
- [ ] Decision log updated

## Warning Signs

Pause when:

- Essential expenses depend on speculative gains
- Emergency reserves are being used routinely
- Property income is recorded before possession
- Gross rent is treated as net income
- Dividend yield is prioritized over total return
- Large purchases have no separate funding plan
- Currency conversion is unplanned
- Withdrawals exceed documented policy
- Long-term assets are repeatedly sold for routine expenses
- Family support has no financial limit

## Beginner Rules

1. Separate income from capital withdrawals.
2. Match near-term expenses with liquid assets.
3. Track COP and USD cash flows separately.
4. Use net rather than gross income.
5. Maintain an emergency reserve.
6. Do not rely on projected property rent.
7. Avoid selling equities during declines when reserves can reasonably cover expenses.
8. Review withdrawal needs annually.
9. Establish limits for discretionary and family spending.
10. Document every portfolio withdrawal.
