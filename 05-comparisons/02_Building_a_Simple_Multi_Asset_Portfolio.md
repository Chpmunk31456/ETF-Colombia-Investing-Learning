# Lesson 14 — Building a Simple Multi-Asset Portfolio

## Learning Objective

Learn how to combine cash, CDTs, bonds, ETFs, Colombian investments, and real estate into a coherent portfolio based on purpose, time horizon, liquidity, and risk.

## Important Principle

A portfolio should not be a random collection of investments.

Every holding should have a defined role.

Possible roles include:

- Emergency liquidity
- Short-term capital preservation
- Stable income
- Long-term growth
- Inflation protection
- Currency diversification
- Property exposure
- Speculative or specialized exposure

## Start With Financial Buckets

Separate money according to when it may be needed.

## Bucket 1 — Immediate Liquidity

Purpose:

- Emergency expenses
- Medical costs
- Housing
- Utilities
- Food
- Transportation
- Unexpected family needs

Possible holdings:

- Regulated savings account
- Highly liquid cash-equivalent product
- Short-access deposit product

Priority:

- Accessibility
- Capital stability
- Low complexity

This bucket should not depend on market prices or property sales.

## Bucket 2 — Short-Term Obligations

Time horizon:

- Approximately 1 to 3 years

Possible purposes:

- Vehicle purchase
- Property down payment
- Insurance
- Travel
- Education
- Planned medical expenses

Possible holdings:

- Short-term CDTs
- CDT ladder
- Short-duration fixed income
- High-liquidity regulated products

Priority:

- Capital preservation
- Matching maturities to expenses
- Limited volatility

## Bucket 3 — Medium-Term Capital

Time horizon:

- Approximately 3 to 7 years

Possible purposes:

- Property purchase
- Major home renovation
- Business funding
- Family support
- Larger planned expenses

Possible holdings:

- Diversified fixed income
- Balanced allocation
- Moderate equity exposure
- Selected Colombian investments
- Some inflation-sensitive assets

Priority:

- Controlled growth
- Reasonable liquidity
- Moderate risk

## Bucket 4 — Long-Term Growth

Time horizon:

- More than 7 years

Possible holdings:

- Broad equity ETFs
- Global equity exposure
- Selected bond ETFs
- Diversified real-estate exposure
- Long-term inflation protection

Priority:

- Growth
- Diversification
- Patience
- Risk discipline

## Bucket 5 — Specialized and Illiquid Investments

Possible holdings:

- Distressed property
- Direct rental property
- Sector ETFs
- Thematic ETFs
- Individual stocks
- Private investments

Priority:

- Strict position limits
- High due diligence
- Separate risk budget
- Clear exit plan

These holdings should not replace emergency reserves or the diversified core.

## Core and Satellite Structure

### Core portfolio

The core may include:

- Emergency cash
- CDTs or short-term fixed income
- Broad equity ETFs
- Broad bond exposure

The core should generally be:

- Diversified
- Understandable
- Lower cost
- Easier to monitor
- Suitable for long-term objectives

### Satellite portfolio

Satellite investments may include:

- Colombian individual stocks
- Sector ETFs
- Dividend strategies
- Thematic ETFs
- Distressed property
- Direct real estate

Satellite holdings should generally remain smaller because they are more concentrated or complex.

## Allocation Is Personal

Allocation depends on:

- Age
- Income stability
- Pension income
- Employment income
- Dependents
- Health costs
- Emergency reserves
- Debt
- Time horizon
- Risk tolerance
- Tax status
- Currency needs
- Property goals

There is no universally correct percentage.

## Risk Budget

A risk budget defines how much of the portfolio may be exposed to:

- Equity volatility
- Interest-rate risk
- Credit risk
- Currency risk
- Property concentration
- Illiquidity
- Legal risk
- Speculative themes

Higher-risk assets should compete for a limited risk budget.

## Liquidity Budget

A liquidity budget identifies how much must be available within:

- One day
- One week
- One month
- Three months
- One year

Do not count the following as immediate liquidity:

- Occupied property
- Distressed property
- Long-term CDT
- Private fund
- Property under legal dispute
- Thinly traded security

## Currency Allocation

A portfolio may contain:

- COP cash
- COP fixed income
- USD cash
- US-listed securities
- Global securities
- Colombian property

Currency allocation should reflect expected obligations.

Examples:

- COP for routine Colombian expenses
- USD for US obligations or international travel
- Mixed exposure for long-term diversification

Do not treat currency movements as guaranteed investment returns.

## Concentration Limits

Set maximum exposure to:

- One company
- One ETF
- One bank
- One broker
- One country
- One currency
- One property
- One neighborhood
- One investment strategy

A portfolio can appear diversified while remaining concentrated in one economic risk.

## Property Concentration

Direct property can dominate a portfolio because it requires substantial capital.

Calculate:

Property concentration =
Total property investment
divided by total investable assets

Include:

- Purchase price
- Legal costs
- Repairs
- financing
- Carrying costs
- Additional capital committed

Do not calculate concentration using only the down payment.

## Portfolio Overlap

Two ETFs may hold many of the same companies.

Examples:

- Broad US equity ETF plus large-cap ETF
- Global ETF plus US ETF
- Technology ETF plus growth ETF
- Dividend ETF plus value ETF

Review:

- Shared holdings
- Sector overlap
- Country overlap
- Factor overlap
- Currency overlap

More funds do not always mean more diversification.

## Home-Country Bias

Home-country bias means investing heavily in the country where the investor lives.

Reasons may include:

- Familiarity
- Local currency
- Easier access
- Local property ownership
- Confidence in known companies

Potential risks:

- Political concentration
- Economic concentration
- Currency concentration
- Employment and investment exposed to the same economy

A person living in Colombia may already have substantial Colombian exposure through:

- Housing
- Daily expenses
- Employment
- Bank accounts
- Property
- Family obligations

## Rebalancing

Rebalancing restores the portfolio toward target allocations.

Methods include:

### Contribution rebalancing

Direct new money toward underweight categories.

### Withdrawal rebalancing

Take withdrawals from overweight categories.

### Sale-and-purchase rebalancing

Sell overweight assets and buy underweight assets.

Before selling, consider:

- Taxes
- Commissions
- Spreads
- Currency conversion
- Market liquidity
- Professional advice

## Rebalancing Bands

Example educational rule:

- Review allocation every six months
- Rebalance when a category differs from target by more than 5 percentage points
- Prefer using new contributions before selling

The appropriate rule depends on the investor.

## Sequence-of-Returns Risk

A person withdrawing from a portfolio can be harmed when large market losses occur early in the withdrawal period.

Possible controls include:

- Adequate cash reserve
- Short-term fixed-income bucket
- Reduced need to sell equities during downturns
- Flexible withdrawal policy
- Diversified income sources

## Stress Testing

Test the portfolio under several scenarios.

### Scenario 1 — Equity decline

Assume equity markets fall by 30%.

Questions:

- How much does the total portfolio fall?
- Would any investments need to be sold?
- Are household expenses still covered?

### Scenario 2 — Peso decline

Assume COP weakens materially against USD.

Questions:

- Do Colombian expenses become easier or harder to fund?
- How does foreign investment value change in COP?
- Are there tax or conversion consequences?

### Scenario 3 — Peso strengthens

Assume COP strengthens materially.

Questions:

- Does USD income buy fewer pesos?
- Are COP obligations still fully covered?
- Should more funds be held locally?

### Scenario 4 — Property delay

Assume distressed property produces no income for 12 months.

Questions:

- Can carrying costs be paid?
- Does emergency liquidity remain intact?
- Must other investments be sold?

### Scenario 5 — Interest-rate decline

Assume CDT renewal rates fall substantially.

Questions:

- Is expected income still sufficient?
- Is the ladder diversified across maturities?
- Are alternative investments required?

## Portfolio Decision Order

A disciplined order may be:

1. Define essential expenses
2. Build emergency reserve
3. Address high-cost debt
4. Fund known short-term obligations
5. Establish diversified core
6. Add local and currency exposure
7. Consider real estate
8. Consider specialized investments
9. Rebalance periodically
10. Review tax and legal consequences

## Warning Signs

Pause when:

- Emergency reserves are incomplete
- Allocation depends on optimistic returns
- One property dominates total wealth
- Multiple ETFs hold the same securities
- Investments cannot be explained clearly
- Tax treatment is unresolved
- Currency exposure is accidental
- Liquidity needs are underestimated
- Borrowing is required to maintain investments
- Investment decisions are driven by recent performance

Answer:

2. Which expenses are expected within three years?
3. What percentage of assets is already exposed to Colombia?
4. What percentage is already exposed to property?
5. How much equity volatility can be tolerated?
6. What is the maximum distressed-property allocation?
7. Which currency matches each future obligation?
8. What assets form the diversified core?
9. Which holdings are satellites?
10. What event would require rebalancing?

## Beginner Rules

1. Assign every holding a purpose.
2. Build liquidity before seeking higher returns.
3. Match short-term obligations with stable assets.
4. Use diversified assets for the core.
5. Limit property concentration.
6. Count total committed property capital.
7. Review ETF overlap.
8. Set currency exposure intentionally.
9. Stress-test before investing.
10. Rebalance according to written rules.
