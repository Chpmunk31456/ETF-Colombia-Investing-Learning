# Lesson 13 — How to Screen and Compare ETFs

## Learning Objective

Learn how to narrow a large ETF universe into a small, understandable group of candidates using objective criteria.

## Important Principle

Do not begin ETF research by asking:

- Which ETF performed best last year?
- Which ETF has the highest dividend?
- Which ETF is most popular online?
- Which ETF has the lowest share price?

Begin by defining the investment purpose.

## Step 1 — Define the Purpose

Choose one primary purpose:

- Broad long-term growth
- Capital preservation
- Income
- Bond exposure
- International diversification
- Inflation protection
- Currency diversification
- Sector exposure
- Short-term cash management

One ETF should not be expected to solve every objective.

## Step 2 — Define the Asset Class

Identify whether the ETF holds:

- US equities
- Global equities
- Emerging-market equities
- Colombian equities
- Government bonds
- Corporate bonds
- Real estate
- Commodities
- Cash equivalents
- Multiple asset classes

## Step 3 — Identify the Fund Domicile

Record:

- Legal fund name
- Country of domicile
- Issuer
- Exchange
- Ticker
- ISIN
- Prospectus date

For a US citizen, fund domicile can materially affect tax treatment.

Do not assume the trading exchange determines domicile.

## Step 4 — Review the Index or Strategy

Determine:

- Index name
- Index provider
- Selection method
- Weighting method
- Rebalancing frequency
- Reconstitution frequency
- Concentration limits
- Number of holdings
- Sampling or full replication

## Step 5 — Review Costs

Record:

- Expense ratio
- Trading commission
- Bid-ask spread
- Currency-conversion cost
- Platform fee
- Custody fee
- Withdrawal fee
- Tax-preparation cost
- Dividend withholding

The expense ratio is only one part of the total cost.

## Step 6 — Review Size and Liquidity

Review:

- Assets under management
- Average daily volume
- Bid-ask spread
- Fund launch date
- Number of market makers
- Liquidity of underlying holdings

Small or newly launched funds may face:

- Wider spreads
- Closure risk
- Lower trading volume
- Limited operating history

## Step 7 — Review Diversification

Record:

- Number of holdings
- Largest holding
- Top 10 concentration
- Largest sector
- Largest country
- Currency exposure
- Overlap with existing investments

A fund with many holdings may still be concentrated.

## Step 8 — Review Historical Risk

Evaluate:

- Volatility
- Maximum drawdown
- Worst calendar year
- Recovery time
- Performance during market stress
- Interest-rate sensitivity
- Credit quality
- Duration

Historical results do not guarantee future results, but they help reveal behavior under stress.

## Step 9 — Review Distributions

Determine whether the ETF:

- Pays dividends
- Reinvests income
- Distributes monthly
- Distributes quarterly
- Distributes annually
- Uses options to generate income

Review:

- Distribution yield
- Total return
- Distribution consistency
- Return of capital
- Tax withholding
- Dividend growth

High yield does not automatically mean high quality.

## Step 10 — Review Tracking Quality

Compare the ETF with its benchmark.

Look for:

- Tracking difference
- Tracking error
- Tax drag
- Cash drag
- Sampling differences
- Securities-lending income

A slightly higher expense ratio may still produce better net tracking.

## Step 11 — Review Tax and Reporting

Questions include:

- Is the fund US-domiciled?
- Could PFIC rules apply?
- Is Form 8621 relevant?
- Is the brokerage account foreign?
- Could FBAR apply?
- Could Form 8938 apply?
- Does Colombian foreign-asset reporting apply?
- How are dividends taxed?
- How are capital gains taxed?
- What records must be retained?

## Step 12 — Review Closure Risk

ETF issuers may close funds.

Possible consequences include:

- Forced liquidation
- Unexpected capital gain
- Need to reinvest
- Temporary spread widening
- Loss of access through a platform

Consider:

- Fund size
- Issuer strength
- Trading activity
- Strategic importance to the issuer

## ETF Screening Rules

A beginner screening process may require:

- Clear investment objective
- Understandable strategy
- Established issuer
- Sufficient fund size
- Reasonable expense ratio
- Acceptable liquidity
- Diversified holdings
- No leverage
- No inverse exposure
- No unclear derivative structure
- Legal domicile verified
- Tax consequences understood

## Automatic Rejection Rules

Reject or pause research when:

- Legal domicile is unclear
- Prospectus is unavailable
- Strategy cannot be explained
- The fund is leveraged or inverse
- The fund depends on short-term options trading
- The fund is extremely concentrated
- Fees are difficult to identify
- Bid-ask spread is unusually wide
- PFIC treatment is unresolved
- The purchase depends only on recent performance
- The fund is promoted using guaranteed-return language

## Four-Fund Educational Comparison

Compare:

1. Broad US equity ETF
2. Global equity ETF
3. Investment-grade bond ETF
4. Dividend-oriented ETF

Do not select them for purchase yet.

The purpose is to learn how the funds differ.

## Weighted Scoring System

Use a 1-to-5 score:

- 1 = Poor
- 2 = Below average
- 3 = Acceptable
- 4 = Strong
- 5 = Excellent

Possible weights:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Strategy clarity | 15% |
| Diversification | 15% |
| Total cost | 15% |
| Liquidity | 10% |
| Fund size and stability | 10% |
| Tracking quality | 10% |
| Tax simplicity | 15% |
| Currency suitability | 5% |
| Risk fit | 5% |

A high score does not replace professional tax or legal review.

## Research Sources

Prefer:

- Official prospectus
- Official issuer factsheet
- Official index methodology
- Official regulator filings
- Exchange information
- Audited fund reports
- Broker fee schedule

Use third-party websites only for confirmation and comparison.

## Beginner Rules

1. Define the investment purpose first.
2. Confirm legal domicile.
3. Read the official prospectus.
4. Compare total cost, not only expense ratio.
5. Review concentration and overlap.
6. Check liquidity and spreads.
7. Understand the index methodology.
8. Review tax consequences before purchase.
9. Reject products that cannot be explained clearly.
10. Document the reason for selecting or rejecting each ETF.

## Knowledge Check

1. Why should ETF research begin with purpose?
2. Why is legal domicile important?
3. Why is the expense ratio not the total cost?
4. What is ETF overlap?
5. What is tracking difference?
6. Why can a high dividend yield be misleading?
7. Why might a small ETF close?
8. Why should leveraged ETFs be rejected by beginners?
9. Why should official sources be preferred?
10. Why should tax-preparation cost be included?
