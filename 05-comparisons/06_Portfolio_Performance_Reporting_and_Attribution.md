# Lesson 38 — Portfolio Performance Reporting, Benchmarking, and Attribution

## Learning Objective

Learn how to prepare a reliable portfolio performance report, select appropriate benchmarks, separate investment return from cash flows, explain major return drivers, and identify whether results came from asset allocation, security selection, currency movement, property valuation, or leverage.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

Performance-reporting methods, tax rules, benchmark conventions, property valuations, and account statements may differ by institution and jurisdiction.

Material calculations should be independently reviewed before being used for tax filings, professional presentations, estate decisions, or major investment changes.

## Core Principle

A change in account value is not automatically investment performance.

Account value can change because of:

- Contributions
- Withdrawals
- Dividends
- Interest
- Market gains or losses
- Currency movement
- Fees
- Taxes
- Property revaluation
- Debt repayment
- Asset transfers
- Corporate actions

Performance reporting must separate these effects.

## Beginning and Ending Value

For each reporting period, record:

- Beginning market value
- Contributions
- Withdrawals
- Income
- Fees
- Taxes
- Ending market value

## Net Investment Result

A simplified net investment result equals:

Ending value
minus beginning value
minus contributions
plus withdrawals

This basic calculation may still be distorted when cash flows occur during the period.

## Personal Cash Flow Versus Investment Return

### Personal cash flow

Money added to or removed from the portfolio.

Examples:

- New contribution
- Retirement withdrawal
- Property down payment
- Tax payment
- Transfer between institutions

### Investment return

Change caused by:

- Market performance
- Interest
- Dividends
- Currency movement
- Fees
- Other investment effects

Transfers between the investor’s own accounts should not be treated as income or investment return.

## Internal Transfers

Internal transfers include:

- Bank to broker
- Broker to broker
- Savings to CDT
- CDT maturity to savings
- Sale proceeds moved to another owned account
- Currency conversion within the same household

Internal transfers should be eliminated from consolidated performance calculations.

## Consolidated Reporting

A consolidated portfolio report may include:

- Cash
- CDTs
- Bonds
- ETFs
- Stocks
- Retirement accounts
- Property equity
- Investment-property cash flow
- Other investable assets
- Related debt

Avoid double counting.

## Reporting Scope

Define whether the report covers:

- One account
- One broker
- Financial investments only
- Retirement assets only
- Property only
- All household investable assets
- Full household net worth

Do not compare reports with different scopes without adjustment.

## Reporting Period

Possible periods include:

- Monthly
- Quarterly
- Year to date
- One year
- Three years
- Five years
- Since inception

Always record exact start and end dates.

## Time-Weighted Return

Time-weighted return measures investment performance while reducing the effect of investor-controlled cash flows.

It is useful for evaluating:

- Portfolio strategy
- Fund manager
- Adviser performance
- Benchmark comparison

The period is divided at significant cash-flow dates.

Subperiod returns are geometrically linked.

## Money-Weighted Return

Money-weighted return reflects the timing and size of the investor’s contributions and withdrawals.

It is useful for evaluating:

- Personal experience
- Actual capital deployment
- Property investment
- Irregular investment schedule
- Retirement withdrawals

Money-weighted return is closely related to internal rate of return.

## Difference Between Time-Weighted and Money-Weighted Return

Time-weighted return answers:

- How did the investment strategy perform?

Money-weighted return answers:

- How did the investor’s actual money perform?

The results may differ substantially when large cash flows occur before gains or losses.

## Example

Assume:

- Portfolio begins at COP 100 million
- Falls to COP 80 million
- Investor adds COP 100 million
- Portfolio then rises 10%

The personal money-weighted result differs from the strategy’s time-weighted result because a large contribution occurred after the decline.

## Total Return

Total return includes:

- Price changes
- Interest
- Dividends
- Distributions
- Reinvested income

It should generally be reported before and after fees where practical.

## Gross Performance

Gross performance is measured before some or all costs.

Possible exclusions include:

- Advisory fee
- Brokerage fee
- Tax
- Currency conversion
- custody fee

Gross performance may be useful for strategy evaluation but does not represent the investor’s actual result.

## Net Performance

Net performance includes applicable costs.

Possible levels include:

- Net of fund expenses
- Net of trading costs
- Net of advisory fees
- Net of withholding
- Net of all known taxes

The report should state exactly what “net” means.

## Income Return

Income return includes:

- Interest
- Dividends
- Net rental income
- Other distributions

Income return should be separated from price appreciation when useful.

## Capital Return

Capital return reflects changes in asset value.

Examples:

- ETF price increase
- Bond price movement
- Property appreciation
- Currency-adjusted value change

## Realized and Unrealized Return

### Realized return

Result from completed transactions.

### Unrealized return

Change in value of assets still owned.

Unrealized gains are not the same as available cash.

## Benchmark

A benchmark is a reference used to evaluate results.

A benchmark should be:

- Relevant
- Measurable
- Available
- Appropriate to the asset
- Consistent with the reporting currency
- Similar in risk
- Similar in investment category

## Benchmark Examples

Possible educational examples include:

- Broad global equity index
- Colombian equity index
- Short-term fixed-income index
- Government-bond index
- Inflation measure
- Comparable CDT rate
- Local property-market evidence
- Blended portfolio benchmark

The exact benchmark should match the investment objective.

## Inappropriate Benchmarking

Avoid comparisons such as:

- Property gross appreciation versus ETF total return
- One-year CDT versus speculative equity
- Pre-tax investment versus after-tax investment
- COP performance versus USD performance without adjustment
- Leveraged strategy versus unleveraged benchmark
- Rental income alone versus total portfolio return

## Blended Benchmark

A blended benchmark combines reference returns according to target allocation.

Example:

- 40% global equity benchmark
- 40% fixed-income benchmark
- 20% cash benchmark

Blended benchmark return equals:

Weight of each benchmark
multiplied by its return
summed across all categories

## Target-Weight Benchmark

Use the Investment Policy Statement target weights rather than current drifted weights when evaluating strategic performance.

## Benchmark Currency

A benchmark may be reported in:

- COP
- USD
- Local market currency

Convert consistently.

Currency conversion can materially change the result.

## Currency Attribution

Currency attribution estimates how much return came from exchange-rate movement.

Separate:

- Local asset return
- Currency return
- Combined reporting-currency return

## Approximate Combined Return

Combined return equals:

(1 + local asset return)
multiplied by
(1 + currency return)
minus 1

Do not simply add the two rates except as a rough approximation.

## Inflation Benchmark

Inflation can be used as a purchasing-power benchmark.

Real return equals:

(1 + nominal return)
divided by
(1 + inflation)
minus 1

A positive nominal return can still produce a negative real return.

## Cash Benchmark

A cash benchmark should reflect a realistic low-risk alternative available during the period.

Possible comparisons include:

- Savings rate
- short-term regulated deposit
- short-term government instrument
- comparable cash-equivalent rate

## Property Benchmarking

Property performance should include:

- Net rental income
- vacancy
- administration
- tax
- insurance
- maintenance
- financing
- renovation
- selling costs
- legal costs
- change in conservative market value

Property should not be compared using gross price change alone.

## Property Valuation

Property valuation should record:

- Valuation date
- method
- appraiser or source
- condition
- occupancy
- comparable evidence
- confidence level
- estimated selling costs

Avoid frequent optimistic revaluation.

## Debt Attribution

Debt can affect return through:

- Interest cost
- leverage
- principal repayment
- currency movement
- refinancing
- fees

Principal repayment increases equity but is not investment profit.

## Return Attribution

Return attribution explains why the portfolio gained or lost value.

Possible drivers include:

- Asset allocation
- Security selection
- Currency
- Income
- Fees
- Taxes
- Property valuation
- Leverage
- Timing of contributions
- Timing of withdrawals

## Asset-Allocation Effect

Asset-allocation effect reflects the result of holding more or less of an asset category than the benchmark.

Example:

Holding more equities than the benchmark during an equity rally may improve relative return.

## Security-Selection Effect

Security-selection effect reflects the result of choosing investments that performed differently from their category benchmark.

Example:

A selected ETF underperforms the broad index it was intended to represent.

## Interaction Effect

Interaction effect reflects the combined result of allocation and selection decisions.

Detailed professional attribution may calculate this separately.

## Contribution by Asset

Approximate contribution to portfolio return equals:

Beginning asset weight
multiplied by asset return

Example:

- Asset weight: 30%
- Asset return: 10%

Approximate contribution:

3 percentage points

## Contribution Versus Return

An asset can have a high return but contribute little if its portfolio weight is small.

A large holding with a modest loss may dominate total results.

## Fee Attribution

Track fees by type:

- Fund expense ratio
- Brokerage commission
- Advisory fee
- custody
- currency conversion
- banking
- property management
- legal
- tax preparation

## Fee Ratio

Fee ratio equals:

Total annual investment-related fees
divided by average portfolio value

## Tax Attribution

Tax attribution may include:

- Withholding
- realized-gain tax
- interest tax
- dividend tax
- property tax
- professional compliance cost

Do not treat all taxes as investment losses without defining the reporting purpose.

## Average Capital

Average capital can be estimated using:

Beginning value
plus ending value
divided by 2

This approximation may be unreliable when large cash flows occur.

## Daily or Monthly Valuation

More frequent valuations improve time-weighted return accuracy.

Possible frequencies:

- Daily
- monthly
- at every major cash flow
- quarterly for illiquid assets

## Data Sources

Use:

- Official broker statements
- Bank statements
- Trade confirmations
- Custodian reports
- Tax documents
- Official exchange rates
- Property appraisals
- Loan statements
- Rental ledgers

## Data Hierarchy

Suggested evidence hierarchy:

1. Official statement
2. Transaction confirmation
3. Bank record
4. Independent appraisal
5. Official public data
6. Internal calculation
7. Estimate

Label estimates clearly.

## Missing Data

When data is missing:

- Do not invent it
- use a documented estimate only where necessary
- label confidence
- record missing evidence
- exclude unreliable comparisons where material

## Valuation Confidence

Possible ratings:

- High
- Moderate
- Low

Examples:

### High

Current broker market value.

### Moderate

Recent professional property appraisal.

### Low

Unverified asking-price estimate.

## Performance Reconciliation

Beginning value
plus contributions
minus withdrawals
plus investment gain or loss
equals ending value

The reconciliation difference should be zero or explained.

## Performance Exception

Create an exception when:

- Ending value does not reconcile
- Contribution is missing
- Transfer was counted twice
- fee is unexplained
- property value changed without evidence
- currency rate is missing
- benchmark is inconsistent
- cost basis is incomplete

## Annual Performance Report

A complete report may include:

- Scope
- Reporting period
- Beginning value
- Ending value
- Contributions
- Withdrawals
- Net investment result
- Time-weighted return
- Money-weighted return
- Benchmark return
- Relative return
- Real return
- Income
- Fees
- Taxes
- Currency effect
- Major contributors
- Major detractors
- Risk
- Allocation
- Next actions

## Relative Return

Relative return equals:

Portfolio return
minus benchmark return

Positive relative return means the portfolio outperformed the selected benchmark.

It does not prove the portfolio was suitable or low risk.

## Tracking Difference

Tracking difference compares an index fund’s return with its index.

Possible causes include:

- Expense ratio
- withholding
- trading cost
- sampling
- cash drag
- replication method
- securities lending
- timing

## Tracking Error

Tracking error measures variability in the difference between portfolio and benchmark returns.

It is different from tracking difference.

## Risk Reporting

Performance should be reviewed with risk.

Possible measures include:

- Volatility
- maximum drawdown
- recovery period
- downside deviation
- concentration
- liquidity
- leverage
- currency exposure

## Drawdown

Drawdown equals:

Current value
minus previous peak
divided by previous peak

Maximum drawdown is the largest decline from peak to trough.

## Recovery Period

Recovery period is the time required to return to the previous peak.

A portfolio may have an acceptable long-term return but an intolerable recovery period.

## Income Coverage

For retirement planning, calculate:

Reliable income
plus portfolio income
divided by essential expenses

Avoid counting unrealized gains as recurring income.

## Withdrawal Effect

Portfolio performance should distinguish:

- Return before withdrawals
- ending balance after withdrawals
- withdrawal rate
- sustainability

A declining balance may result from planned withdrawals rather than poor performance alone.

## Contribution Effect

A rising balance may result primarily from new contributions rather than investment success.

Report both:

- Dollar or peso growth
- investment return

## Performance Against Objective

The most important benchmark may be the financial objective.

Examples:

- Preserve purchasing power
- fund annual withdrawals
- maintain six-month reserve
- reduce property concentration
- generate stable income
- limit drawdown
- maintain currency coverage

## Objective Success

A portfolio may underperform an equity index while still successfully meeting a lower-risk retirement objective.

## Performance Review Questions

Ask:

- Did the portfolio meet its objective?
- Was risk within limits?
- Was liquidity adequate?
- Did fees remain acceptable?
- Did taxes create avoidable drag?
- Did currency exposure behave as expected?
- Did property concentration increase?
- Was rebalancing followed?
- Were withdrawals sustainable?
- Were decisions consistent with policy?

## Performance-Chasing Risk

Do not replace investments solely because of:

- One weak quarter
- one strong competitor
- recent ranking
- short-term benchmark difference
- social-media commentary

Review:

- Objective
- risk
- structure
- fees
- tracking
- longer period
- tax cost
- thesis

## Benchmark-Chasing Risk

Changing strategy merely to match the best recent benchmark can increase:

- Turnover
- taxes
- concentration
- timing risk
- behavioral error

## Annual Attribution Review

For each major result, identify:

- What happened
- Why it happened
- Whether it was expected
- Whether it came from policy or luck
- Whether action is required
- Whether the Investment Policy Statement should change

## Decision Quality Review

Separate:

- Good decision and good outcome
- Good decision and poor outcome
- Poor decision and good outcome
- Poor decision and poor outcome

## Performance Status

### Green

- Objective met
- risk within limits
- report reconciled
- fees acceptable
- no material unexplained difference

### Yellow

- Objective partly met
- data incomplete
- material drift
- fee or tax concern
- benchmark mismatch
- review required

### Red

- Reconciliation failure
- unauthorized transaction
- unexplained loss
- excessive drawdown
- liquidity failure
- performance report based on unreliable data

## Stop Conditions

Do not publish or rely on the report when:

- Scope is unclear
- Contributions and withdrawals are missing
- Internal transfers are double counted
- Currency treatment is inconsistent
- Property values are unsupported
- Benchmark is inappropriate
- fees are omitted
- performance does not reconcile
- tax figures are unverified
- current statements are unavailable
- material estimates are presented as facts

## Beginner Rules

1. Separate investment return from contributions and withdrawals.
2. Define the reporting scope.
3. Use exact reporting dates.
4. Eliminate internal transfers.
5. Compare with an appropriate benchmark.
6. Report both time-weighted and money-weighted return where useful.
7. Include fees, currency, and inflation.
8. Value property conservatively.
9. Reconcile beginning and ending values.
10. Evaluate performance against the actual objective.
