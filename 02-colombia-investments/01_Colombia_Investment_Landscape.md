# Lesson 5 — Colombia Investment Landscape

## Learning Objective

Understand the main investment products available to a person living in Colombia and how they differ in risk, liquidity, currency exposure, regulation, and complexity.

## Important Principle

The name of an investment does not determine whether it is suitable.

Before investing, evaluate:

- Purpose
- Expected holding period
- Liquidity
- Credit risk
- Market risk
- Currency exposure
- Fees
- Tax treatment
- Regulatory status
- Protection of client assets
- Consequences of early withdrawal

## Main Investment Categories in Colombia

Common categories include:

- Savings accounts
- High-yield savings products
- CDTs
- Colombian government bonds
- Corporate bonds
- Collective investment funds
- Voluntary pension funds
- Colombian stocks
- International stocks and ETFs
- Mercado Global Colombiano securities
- Real-estate investment vehicles
- Direct real estate
- Foreign brokerage accounts

Each category serves a different purpose.

## Savings Accounts

Savings accounts are primarily cash-management tools.

Potential advantages:

- High liquidity
- Easy access
- Familiar banking structure
- Suitable for short-term expenses and emergency reserves

Potential disadvantages:

- Returns may not keep pace with inflation
- Promotional rates may change
- Account fees may reduce returns
- Interest may be taxable, subject to current law and the account holder’s circumstances

Questions to ask:

1. Is the institution supervised?
2. Is deposit insurance applicable?
3. What fees apply?
4. Is the advertised rate temporary?
5. Is there a minimum balance?
6. How quickly can funds be withdrawn?

## CDTs

A CDT is a fixed-term deposit issued by a financial institution.

The investor agrees to leave money deposited for a specified period in exchange for interest.

Common terms may include:

- 30 days
- 90 days
- 180 days
- 360 days
- Longer periods

Potential advantages:

- Predictable contractual rate
- Simpler than market-traded securities
- Useful for known future expenses
- May offer higher rates than ordinary savings accounts

Potential disadvantages:

- Limited access before maturity
- Reinvestment risk
- Inflation risk
- Institution-specific credit exposure
- Early redemption may be difficult or unavailable
- Returns may be less attractive after taxes and inflation

## CDT Ladder

A CDT ladder divides money among several maturity dates.

Example:

- Portion 1: 90-day CDT
- Portion 2: 180-day CDT
- Portion 3: 270-day CDT
- Portion 4: 360-day CDT

As each deposit matures, the investor can:

- Use the money
- Reinvest it
- Adjust to new interest rates
- Preserve periodic liquidity

A ladder does not eliminate inflation, credit, or reinvestment risk.

## Colombian Government Bonds

Government bonds represent debt issued by the Colombian government.

They may include fixed-rate, inflation-linked, or other structures.

Potential advantages:

- Access to sovereign fixed income
- Potential income
- Different maturity options
- Possible diversification from equities

Important risks:

- Interest-rate risk
- Inflation risk
- Market-price volatility
- Sovereign risk
- Liquidity risk
- Currency risk for investors measuring wealth in US dollars

A government bond can lose market value before maturity.

## Corporate Bonds

Corporate bonds represent loans to companies.

Potential advantages:

- Income
- Higher yields than some government debt
- Diversification among issuers and sectors

Important risks:

- Default risk
- Credit-rating changes
- Interest-rate risk
- Liquidity risk
- Issuer concentration
- Complex covenants

Higher yield usually reflects higher risk.

## Collective Investment Funds

Collective investment funds pool money from multiple investors.

They may invest in:

- Cash instruments
- Fixed income
- Equities
- Real estate
- International securities
- Mixed portfolios

Potential advantages:

- Professional management
- Diversification
- Access to markets that may be difficult to enter individually
- Different risk profiles

Potential disadvantages:

- Management fees
- Entry or withdrawal restrictions
- Performance fees
- Market risk
- Possible complexity
- Tax consequences
- Limited transparency in some products

## Questions for Any Collective Fund

Ask:

1. What assets does it hold?
2. Who manages the fund?
3. Who acts as custodian?
4. What is the risk classification?
5. What fees are charged?
6. How often can money be withdrawn?
7. Is there a minimum holding period?
8. Is there a withdrawal penalty?
9. How has the fund behaved during market stress?
10. Are returns reported before or after fees?

## Voluntary Pension Funds

Voluntary pension products may provide long-term investment and tax-planning features.

Potential advantages:

- Long-term saving discipline
- Professional portfolio management
- Possible tax benefits under qualifying conditions
- Beneficiary planning

Potential disadvantages:

- Withdrawal restrictions
- Conditions attached to tax benefits
- Management fees
- Investment risk
- Complex cross-border tax treatment
- Possible US reporting complications for US citizens

A US citizen should obtain qualified cross-border tax guidance before using a non-US pooled investment or pension vehicle. Depending on its legal structure and the investor’s circumstances, a non-US fund may create PFIC analysis, Form 8621 reporting, foreign-account reporting, or other US tax obligations.

## Colombian Stocks

A stock represents ownership in a publicly traded company.

Potential advantages:

- Growth potential
- Dividend income
- Direct participation in Colombian businesses
- Exposure to the Colombian economy

Important risks:

- Company-specific risk
- Sector concentration
- Political and regulatory risk
- Currency risk
- Lower liquidity in some securities
- Dividend variability
- Corporate-governance risk

The Colombian equity market may be more concentrated than larger international markets.

## Mercado Global Colombiano

The Mercado Global Colombiano provides access through Colombian market infrastructure to certain foreign securities.

Potential advantages:

- Local-market access
- Potentially simpler funding in Colombia
- Access to selected international companies or funds
- Local brokerage relationship

Potential disadvantages:

- Limited security selection
- Lower liquidity than the original foreign exchange
- Bid-ask spreads
- Currency exposure
- Foreign-asset reporting questions
- Tax treatment that may differ from investor assumptions

The trading venue or Colombian market infrastructure does not, by itself, establish the security’s tax classification. Classification and reporting treatment should be verified using current Colombian and applicable foreign tax rules.

## International ETFs

International ETFs may provide exposure to:

- US stocks
- Global stocks
- Bonds
- Real estate
- Commodities
- Specific sectors

A Colombia-based investor must consider:

- Fund domicile
- Trading currency
- Underlying currency exposure
- Brokerage costs
- Dividend withholding
- Colombian reporting
- US reporting
- Estate-tax exposure
- PFIC risk for non-US funds

## Foreign Brokerage Accounts

A foreign brokerage may offer broader access to international stocks and ETFs.

Potential advantages:

- Larger product selection
- Potentially lower trading costs
- Access to highly liquid markets
- Fractional shares at some brokers

Potential disadvantages:

- International transfer costs
- Currency conversion
- Tax reporting
- Estate issues
- Foreign-account reporting
- Customer-support challenges
- Jurisdiction and investor-protection differences
- Difficulty transferring money back to Colombia

## Real-Estate Investment Vehicles

Real-estate investment funds or listed property vehicles may provide exposure to:

- Offices
- Warehouses
- Retail properties
- Housing
- Hotels
- Infrastructure-related property

Potential advantages:

- Income potential
- Diversification
- Professional management
- Easier access than buying an entire property

Important risks:

- Property-market cycles
- Interest rates
- Tenant defaults
- Vacancy
- Valuation uncertainty
- Management fees
- Limited liquidity

## Direct Real Estate

Direct property ownership differs significantly from owning a financial security.

Potential advantages:

- Tangible asset
- Rental income
- Possible inflation protection
- Personal use

Potential disadvantages:

- Large initial capital requirement
- Low liquidity
- Maintenance
- Taxes
- HOA or administration fees
- Vacancy
- Tenant risk
- Legal expenses
- Concentration in one property and location

## COP Versus USD Exposure

A Colombia-based investor may receive income in US dollars while spending in Colombian pesos.

This creates important planning questions:

- How much should remain in COP for near-term expenses?
- How much USD exposure is appropriate?
- What happens if the peso strengthens?
- What happens if the peso weakens?
- What are the conversion costs?
- Which currency matches future liabilities?

Currency exposure should be intentional, not accidental.

## Liquidity Categories

### Immediate liquidity

Examples:

- Savings accounts
- Money-market-like products

### Short-term liquidity

Examples:

- Short CDTs
- Short-duration fixed-income funds

### Market liquidity

Examples:

- Public stocks
- ETFs
- Listed bonds

These can usually be sold, but the price may be unfavorable.

### Limited liquidity

Examples:

- Long-term funds
- Private investments
- Direct real estate
- Certain pension products

## Regulatory Verification

Before sending money:

1. Identify the exact legal entity.
2. Verify the institution through the relevant regulator.
3. Confirm that the website and contact details are official.
4. Understand where client assets are held.
5. Review withdrawal procedures.
6. Confirm investor-protection arrangements.
7. Avoid transferring money to an individual.
8. Avoid guaranteed-return claims.
9. Never share brokerage passwords.
10. Enable multi-factor authentication.

## Preliminary Suitability Framework

### Emergency reserve

Priority:

- Capital preservation
- Immediate access
- Low complexity

### Money needed within three years

Priority:

- Liquidity
- Low volatility
- Known maturity dates
- Limited market exposure

### Long-term growth capital

Possible considerations:

- Diversified equity exposure
- Bond exposure
- Currency diversification
- Controlled volatility

### Income-oriented capital

Possible considerations:

- Fixed income
- CDTs
- Dividend-paying investments
- Distribution reliability
- Tax effects

Income should be evaluated using total return and risk, not yield alone.

## Personal Reflection

1. How much money must remain immediately accessible?
2. What expenses are expected during the next three years?
3. Which obligations are denominated in COP?
4. Which income sources are denominated in USD?
5. How much market volatility is financially tolerable?
6. Is regular income or long-term growth more important?
7. Which products require professional tax review?
8. Which products are too complex at this stage?
9. What maximum percentage should be held with one institution?
10. What documentation must be retained?

## Beginner Rules

1. Match the investment to the purpose of the money.
2. Do not invest emergency funds in volatile assets.
3. Understand withdrawal restrictions before depositing.
4. Compare returns after fees, tax, inflation, and currency conversion.
5. Verify every institution independently.
6. Avoid products that cannot be explained clearly.
7. Do not chase the highest advertised rate.
8. Diversify institutions as well as investments when appropriate.
9. Obtain cross-border tax advice before buying non-US pooled funds.
10. Keep written records of every decision and official source.
