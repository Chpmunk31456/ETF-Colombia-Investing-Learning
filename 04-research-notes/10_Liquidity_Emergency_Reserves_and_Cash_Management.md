# Lesson 34 — Liquidity Planning, Emergency Reserves, and Cash-Management Controls

## Learning Objective

Learn how to organize household cash, emergency reserves, near-term obligations, investment funding, property reserves, and currency needs so long-term assets are not forced to cover short-term expenses.

## Important Privacy Rule

This repository should contain only:

- Educational material
- Blank templates
- Hypothetical examples
- Redacted summaries

Completed cash-flow records, account balances, bank statements, and account identifiers should remain in an encrypted private location outside Git.

## Core Principle

Money needed soon should not depend on:

- Favorable stock-market prices
- Property sales
- Auction outcomes
- Loan approval
- CDT early withdrawal
- Currency conversion at a favorable rate
- Uncertain consulting income
- Unsettled insurance claims
- Family repayment promises

Liquidity should be planned before investing.

## What Liquidity Means

Liquidity describes how quickly an asset can be converted into usable money without:

- Significant loss
- Legal delay
- Market uncertainty
- Penalty
- Operational difficulty
- Dependency on another party

An asset may be valuable but illiquid.

## Examples of Liquid Assets

Possible liquid assets include:

- Cash
- Checking account
- Savings account
- Immediately accessible deposit
- Short-term instrument near maturity
- Marketable securities under normal conditions

Accessibility can still be limited by:

- Bank outage
- Account freeze
- Transfer limit
- settlement period
- foreign-exchange process
- identity verification
- holiday
- fraud investigation

## Examples of Illiquid Assets

Possible illiquid assets include:

- Primary residence
- Rental property
- Distressed property
- Property under renovation
- Business ownership
- Restricted pension account
- Long-duration investment with withdrawal penalties
- Litigated rights
- Uncollected receivable
- Asset requiring a buyer

## Liquidity Versus Safety

Liquid does not always mean safe.

Examples:

- A publicly traded ETF may be liquid but volatile.
- Cash may be stable in nominal terms but lose purchasing power.
- A foreign-currency balance may be liquid but fluctuate in COP value.
- An uninsured account may be accessible but exposed to institutional risk.

Evaluate both:

- Accessibility
- Capital stability

## Liquidity Versus Return

Higher expected return often requires accepting more:

- Volatility
- time
- credit risk
- lockup
- legal complexity
- currency exposure
- uncertainty

Emergency reserves should prioritize reliability and accessibility over maximum return.

## Cash Categories

Separate household cash into defined categories.

### Operating Cash

Used for normal monthly expenses.

Examples:

- Housing
- food
- transportation
- utilities
- insurance
- health care
- debt payments

### Emergency Reserve

Used for unexpected and essential events.

### Near-Term Goal Funds

Used for known expenses expected soon.

Examples:

- Vehicle purchase
- property down payment
- travel
- taxes
- medical procedure
- education
- legal fees
- renovation

### Investment Cash

Capital not needed for current expenses, reserves, or committed goals.

### Opportunity Reserve

A limited amount reserved for qualified future opportunities.

Opportunity reserves should not replace emergency reserves.

## Emergency Reserve Purpose

An emergency reserve may cover:

- Income interruption
- delayed pension or salary payment
- medical expense
- urgent travel
- family emergency
- vehicle repair
- urgent property repair
- legal consultation
- insurance deductible
- temporary relocation
- banking interruption

## Emergency Reserve Is Not For

The emergency reserve should not normally fund:

- Speculative investments
- auction deposits
- optional renovation
- discretionary travel
- lifestyle upgrades
- recovery of investment losses
- additional trading
- loans to others
- routine overspending

## Essential Expenses

Essential expenses may include:

- Housing
- food
- basic utilities
- health coverage
- medication
- transportation
- debt minimums
- insurance
- dependent support
- required taxes
- essential communication

Separate essential expenses from discretionary expenses.

## Emergency-Reserve Calculation

Educational formula:

Emergency reserve target equals:

Monthly essential expenses
multiplied by target number of months

Possible target considerations include:

- Stability of pension income
- Employment uncertainty
- consulting variability
- number of dependents
- health needs
- insurance deductibles
- property ownership
- currency exposure
- access to credit
- family obligations
- time required to replace income

## Reserve Duration

Possible ranges for analysis include:

- Three months
- Six months
- Nine months
- Twelve months
- Longer for unusual circumstances

The appropriate target depends on household risk and should not be selected mechanically.

## Income Stability

Classify income as:

### Highly reliable

Examples may include:

- Established pension
- long-term contractual income
- stable employment

### Moderately reliable

Examples:

- Renewable contract
- rental income
- consulting relationship
- variable commission

### Uncertain

Examples:

- New freelance work
- expected job offer
- projected business income
- planned property rent
- investment distributions not yet established

Do not fund fixed obligations using income that has not begun.

## Reserve Layers

A layered reserve can improve control.

### Layer 1 — Immediate Access

Funds available the same day or next business day.

Purpose:

- Daily emergency
- urgent medical cost
- essential payment interruption

### Layer 2 — Short-Term Reserve

Funds available within several days or weeks.

Purpose:

- Larger repair
- delayed income
- insurance deductible
- temporary housing

### Layer 3 — Extended Contingency

Funds available over one to three months with limited risk.

Purpose:

- prolonged income loss
- major property issue
- legal process
- family emergency

## Cash-Bucket Structure

Possible buckets include:

1. Monthly operating account
2. Emergency reserve
3. Medical reserve
4. Tax reserve
5. Property reserve
6. Vehicle reserve
7. Insurance-deductible reserve
8. Near-term goal funds
9. Investment funding
10. Opportunity reserve

Each peso should have one primary purpose.

## Avoid Double Counting

The same money cannot simultaneously serve as:

- Emergency reserve
- property down payment
- vehicle deposit
- investment capital
- tax reserve
- renovation reserve

Assign each balance to one primary purpose.

## Reserve Funding Order

A conservative sequence may be:

1. Current essential expenses
2. Minimum debt obligations
3. Emergency reserve
4. Medical and insurance reserves
5. Tax obligations
6. Known near-term commitments
7. Property or vehicle reserve
8. Long-term investment
9. Optional opportunity capital

## Monthly Cash-Flow Baseline

Record:

- Reliable monthly income
- Variable income
- essential expenses
- discretionary expenses
- debt payments
- reserve contributions
- investment contributions
- monthly surplus or deficit

## Monthly Surplus

Monthly surplus equals:

Net income
minus total spending
minus required reserve contributions

A projected surplus is not available until income has actually been received and obligations have cleared.

## Cash-Flow Deficit

A recurring deficit should be addressed before increasing investment risk.

Possible responses include:

- Reduce discretionary spending
- restructure debt
- adjust investment contributions
- increase reliable income
- delay property purchase
- reduce fixed commitments
- use reserves temporarily under a written plan

## Irregular Expenses

Convert irregular expenses into monthly reserves.

Examples:

- Annual insurance
- property tax
- vehicle registration
- medical deductible
- professional fees
- tax preparation
- property maintenance
- travel
- software subscriptions
- school expenses

Monthly reserve equals:

Expected annual expense
divided by 12

## Sinking Fund

A sinking fund accumulates money for a known future expense.

Examples:

- Vehicle replacement
- roof repair
- annual property tax
- insurance renewal
- appliance replacement
- legal filing
- travel
- education

A sinking fund is different from an emergency reserve because the expense is expected.

## Tax Reserve

A tax reserve may be required for:

- Consulting income
- investment income
- property sale
- rental income
- foreign income
- estimated payments
- withholding shortfall

Do not invest money reasonably expected to be owed in taxes.

## Medical Reserve

Medical reserves may cover:

- Copayments
- uncovered medication
- diagnostic tests
- private consultation
- travel for treatment
- dental care
- vision care
- caregiver expense
- policy deductible
- excluded procedure

## Property Reserve

A property reserve may cover:

- Mortgage payment
- administration
- property tax
- insurance
- maintenance
- urgent repair
- vacancy
- legal fees
- special assessment
- utilities during vacancy

Maintain separate reserves for personal and rental property when practical.

## Renovation Reserve

Renovation funding should include:

- Approved scope
- contingency
- temporary accommodation
- permit expense
- professional fees
- delay costs
- final retention
- defect correction

Do not begin renovation using the emergency reserve as the contingency.

## Vehicle Reserve

Possible vehicle reserves include:

- Insurance
- tax or registration
- maintenance
- tires
- deductible
- repair
- replacement
- loan payment buffer

## Currency Reserve

A household earning in one currency and spending in another may maintain a reserve in the spending currency.

Examples:

- COP reserve for local expenses
- USD reserve for US obligations
- travel currency reserve

This reduces dependence on a favorable same-day exchange rate.

## Currency Conversion Schedule

Possible approaches include:

### Immediate conversion

Convert income when received.

### Periodic conversion

Convert at scheduled intervals.

### Layered conversion

Convert enough for near-term obligations while retaining some foreign currency.

### Threshold-based conversion

Convert according to predetermined rules.

Avoid making all conversions based on emotion or short-term exchange-rate predictions.

## Currency-Mismatch Risk

Currency mismatch occurs when:

- Income is in USD
- debt and expenses are in COP
- reserves are held mostly in USD

or the reverse.

Model the effect of:

- COP strengthening
- COP weakening
- transfer delay
- bank holiday
- account restriction
- conversion spread

## Bank Concentration

Holding all cash at one institution can create operational risk.

Possible risks include:

- Account freeze
- fraud investigation
- outage
- transfer interruption
- card failure
- institution-specific limits

Consider whether operational redundancy is appropriate.

## Access Redundancy

Possible controls include:

- More than one payment method
- More than one bank relationship
- Backup card
- small physical cash reserve stored safely
- secure access to emergency contacts
- updated identity documentation
- tested transfer method

Avoid excessive fragmentation that becomes difficult to manage.

## Cash at Home

A limited physical cash reserve may help during:

- Banking outage
- card failure
- network disruption
- local emergency

Risks include:

- Theft
- fire
- loss
- no interest
- lack of insurance
- family access confusion

Do not store large undocumented amounts of cash.

## Settlement Risk

Sale proceeds from investments may not become immediately available.

Possible delays include:

- Trade settlement
- fund withdrawal
- bank processing
- foreign-exchange conversion
- compliance review
- weekend or holiday
- destination-bank hold

Do not assume an investment can cover an obligation due the same day.

## Market-Loss Risk During Withdrawal

Selling an ETF during a decline may convert a temporary market loss into a permanent withdrawal loss.

Emergency reserves reduce forced selling.

## Property-Sale Liquidity Risk

A property sale may require:

- Marketing
- negotiation
- buyer financing
- legal review
- notarial closing
- registration
- mortgage cancellation
- possession delivery

Property should not be treated as short-term emergency liquidity.

## CDT Liquidity Risk

Before using a CDT for reserves, confirm:

- Maturity date
- early-withdrawal rules
- transferability
- penalty
- renewal instructions
- bank processing
- beneficiary or succession process

A high rate does not compensate for unavailable money during an emergency.

## Reserve Maturity Ladder

A maturity ladder can divide funds across dates.

Example:

- Immediate cash
- 30-day maturity
- 60-day maturity
- 90-day maturity
- longer-term maturity

The structure should match expected access needs.

## Automatic Renewal Risk

A deposit may renew automatically.

Track:

- Maturity date
- notice deadline
- renewal terms
- new rate
- withdrawal window
- intended use

Automatic renewal can trap funds needed for another purpose.

## Opportunity-Cost Review

Holding cash has an opportunity cost.

However, insufficient cash can cause:

- high-interest borrowing
- forced asset sale
- missed essential payment
- property default
- insurance lapse
- inability to handle medical needs

The goal is not maximum cash or minimum cash.

The goal is sufficient liquidity.

## Reserve Adequacy Ratio

Educational formula:

Reserve adequacy ratio equals:

Available qualified reserves
divided by reserve target

Interpretation:

- Below 1.0: target not fully funded
- 1.0: target funded
- Above 1.0: excess may exist, subject to other obligations

## Liquidity Coverage Ratio

Educational formula:

Liquidity coverage ratio equals:

Assets accessible within the required period
divided by obligations due within that period

Calculate separately for:

- Seven days
- Thirty days
- Ninety days
- Twelve months

## Seven-Day Liquidity

Include only funds realistically available within seven days.

Compare with:

- Essential bills
- emergency medical needs
- urgent travel
- debt payments
- insurance deductibles

## Thirty-Day Liquidity

Include funds accessible within thirty days with low loss risk.

Compare with:

- One month essential spending
- loan payments
- taxes
- family support
- urgent repairs

## Ninety-Day Liquidity

Include funds accessible within ninety days.

Compare with:

- Three months essential spending
- property obligations
- tax payments
- known major expenses

## Reserve Stress Tests

### Scenario 1 — Income Delay

Assume reliable income is delayed by:

- Two weeks
- One month
- Three months

Calculate:

- Available reserves
- bills due
- reserve remaining
- required action

### Scenario 2 — Employment Income Stops

Remove salary or consulting income.

Determine:

- Monthly deficit
- reserve duration
- investment contributions suspended
- discretionary reductions
- property affordability

### Scenario 3 — Medical Expense

Assume a major immediate medical cost.

Determine:

- deductible
- uncovered amount
- emergency reserve impact
- ability to continue essential payments

### Scenario 4 — Property Emergency

Assume:

- urgent repair
- administration assessment
- vacancy
- mortgage payment
- legal consultation

### Scenario 5 — COP Strengthens

Reduce the COP value of USD income and reserves.

Determine:

- monthly coverage
- conversion needs
- mortgage affordability
- reserve duration

### Scenario 6 — Banking Interruption

Assume the primary bank is inaccessible for seven days.

Determine:

- alternate payment method
- accessible cash
- critical bills
- communication process

### Scenario 7 — Simultaneous Events

Assume:

- income interruption
- medical cost
- property repair

Combined-event testing is more realistic than testing every risk separately.

## Liquidity Trigger Levels

Possible status levels:

### Green

- Emergency reserve fully funded
- Near-term obligations funded
- Tax reserve funded
- Adequate access redundancy
- No forced-sale dependency

### Yellow

- Reserve below target
- One major expense unfunded
- excessive institution concentration
- currency mismatch
- upcoming maturity or tax obligation

### Red

- Essential payment depends on asset sale
- emergency reserve committed elsewhere
- high-interest borrowing required
- property purchase would eliminate liquidity
- tax obligation unfunded
- no backup payment access

## Investment Funding Gate

Before transferring money to an investment, confirm:

- Current bills covered
- emergency reserve funded
- medical reserve reviewed
- tax reserve funded
- near-term obligations funded
- property commitments funded
- currency needs funded
- investment amount remains after all reserves
- worst-case scenario affordable

## Property-Purchase Liquidity Gate

Before paying a property deposit, confirm:

- Down payment
- closing costs
- legal costs
- registration
- immediate repairs
- moving
- furniture
- six-month ownership reserve
- emergency reserve
- medical reserve
- tax reserve

The deposit should not consume funds required to complete the transaction safely.

## Auction Liquidity Gate

Before bidding, confirm:

- Deposit available
- remaining purchase funds available
- transfer deadlines understood
- legal costs reserved
- possession costs reserved
- repair contingency reserved
- carrying costs reserved
- emergency reserve untouched

## Investment Contribution Rule

Investment contributions should be based on actual surplus, not anticipated income.

Possible rule:

Invest only after:

- Income received
- bills paid
- reserve contribution completed
- upcoming obligations funded
- account reconciliation completed

## Withdrawal Sequencing

When cash is needed, a possible review sequence is:

1. Operating cash
2. Dedicated sinking fund
3. Appropriate reserve
4. Maturing low-risk asset
5. Marketable investment
6. Long-term or illiquid asset
7. Borrowing only after full cost review

The appropriate order depends on tax, legal, and personal circumstances.

## Emergency Reserve Replenishment

After reserve use:

1. Record the event.
2. Confirm the withdrawal was appropriate.
3. Define replenishment target.
4. Pause optional investment contributions if necessary.
5. Redirect surplus.
6. review whether the reserve target was adequate.
7. Update insurance or operational controls.

## Warning Signs

Pause new investment activity when:

- Bills are paid from credit
- reserve balances are falling
- taxes are unfunded
- emergency money is used for opportunities
- property obligations depend on future income
- one bank holds all accessible funds
- cash is locked beyond the obligation date
- near-term expenses are financed through volatile assets
- upcoming maturity dates are unknown
- currency conversion is required at the last minute
- completed cash records are being stored in Git

## Beginner Rules

1. Separate operating cash from investment capital.
2. Assign each reserve one primary purpose.
3. Do not double-count money.
4. Fund known obligations before investing.
5. Keep emergency reserves accessible and stable.
6. Match maturity dates to spending dates.
7. Maintain enough spending-currency liquidity.
8. Avoid dependence on property or market sales.
9. Stress-test simultaneous emergencies.
10. Review liquidity before every material transaction.
