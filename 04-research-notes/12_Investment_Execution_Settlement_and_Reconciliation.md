# Lesson 36 — Investment Execution, Trade Settlement, and Account Reconciliation

## Learning Objective

Learn how to verify funding instructions, place controlled investment orders, review executions, track settlement, reconcile account balances, preserve cost-basis records, and investigate transaction discrepancies.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

Trading rules, settlement periods, order types, tax treatment, investor protections, and account procedures vary by broker, exchange, country, security, and account type.

Confirm current requirements through official broker documents and qualified professional advice before placing a material transaction.

## Core Principle

An investment decision is not complete when the order is submitted.

Completion requires verification of:

- Correct account
- Correct security
- Correct quantity
- Correct order type
- Correct price or limit
- Correct currency
- Correct fees
- Correct settlement
- Correct cash balance
- Correct cost basis
- Correct tax classification
- Correct supporting records

## Investment Execution Lifecycle

A controlled transaction follows these stages:

1. Investment approved
2. Account verified
3. Funding instructions verified
4. Funds transferred
5. Cash credited
6. Security identified
7. Order prepared
8. Order reviewed
9. Order submitted
10. Execution confirmed
11. Settlement completed
12. Account reconciled
13. Cost basis recorded
14. Decision log updated
15. Documents stored securely

## Pre-Trade Approval

Before placing an order, confirm:

- Investment thesis remains valid
- Investment Policy Statement permits the transaction
- Position-size limit is respected
- Liquidity gate is green
- Emergency reserves remain intact
- Tax questions are resolved
- Broker and security have been verified
- Currency requirement is understood
- Fees are acceptable
- Cooling-off period is complete
- Decision gate is approved

## Account Verification

Confirm:

- Broker legal entity
- Account holder
- Account type
- Account jurisdiction
- Base currency
- Trading permissions
- Available cash
- withdrawal restrictions
- tax documentation
- beneficiary or trusted-contact status
- security controls
- MFA enabled

Do not place a trade through an unfamiliar account reached from an unsolicited link.

## Funding Instructions

Verify funding instructions through an official authenticated channel.

Record:

- Receiving institution
- Beneficiary
- Account identifier
- Reference code
- Currency
- Transfer type
- Minimum and maximum
- Expected processing time
- Fees
- Return-of-funds procedure

## Payment-Instructions Change

An unexpected change in bank or payment instructions is a fraud warning.

Before sending money:

1. Stop.
2. Contact the institution through a previously verified channel.
3. Confirm the change independently.
4. Record the representative.
5. Retain written confirmation.
6. Send a small test transfer where appropriate.
7. Confirm receipt before sending a material amount.

## Test Transfer

A test transfer can reduce operational risk.

Confirm:

- Correct destination
- Expected currency
- Fees
- transfer time
- reference code
- credited amount
- withdrawal functionality where relevant

A successful deposit does not by itself prove the investment platform is legitimate.

Effective currency cost may include:

## Security Identification

Before trading, verify:

- Full legal name
- Ticker
- ISIN or equivalent identifier
- Exchange
- Currency
- Fund domicile
- Issuer
- Share class
- Distribution or accumulation policy
- Hedged or unhedged status
- Leveraged or inverse status

Check for:

- Similar ticker

- Derivative product

Do not rely on the ticker alone.

## Order Types

## Market Order

A market order seeks immediate execution at available market prices.

Potential advantages:

- High probability of execution
- Simple process

Potential risks:

- Execution price uncertain
- poor result in thin markets
- larger spread
- volatility
- partial fills

## Limit Order

A limit order specifies the highest purchase price or lowest sale price acceptable.

Potential advantages:

- Price control
- protection from unexpected execution

Potential risks:

- Order may not execute
- partial execution
- opportunity may pass
- order may remain active unexpectedly

## Stop Order

A stop order becomes active when a trigger price is reached.

Risks include:

- Execution below or above expected level
- price gaps
- temporary volatility
- false sense of guaranteed protection

## Stop-Limit Order

A stop-limit order combines a trigger with a limit.

It may fail to execute when the market moves beyond the limit.

## Day Order

A day order normally expires at the end of the applicable trading session if not executed.

## Good-Til-Cancelled Order

A good-til-cancelled order may remain active until:

- Executed
- cancelled
- broker expiration
- corporate action
- account restriction

Review all open orders regularly.

## Order Preview

Before submission, review:

- Account
- buy or sell
- security
- ticker
- exchange
- quantity
- order type
- limit or trigger
- estimated value
- currency
- estimated commission
- estimated regulatory fees
- estimated settlement
- available cash
- effect on allocation

## Decimal and Quantity Errors

Common errors include:

- Extra zero
- omitted decimal
- shares confused with currency amount
- price entered as quantity
- quantity entered as price
- COP confused with USD
- percentage confused with decimal
- total position confused with additional purchase

Read the order aloud or use a two-person review for material transactions.

## Fractional Shares

If supported, verify:

- Minimum amount
- execution method
- pricing
- voting rights
- transferability
- dividend treatment
- liquidation rules
- fees

Fractional shares may not transfer between brokers.

## Trading Hours

Review:

- Exchange trading hours
- local time zone
- holidays
- pre-market or after-hours availability
- liquidity
- order handling outside normal hours

Extended-hours trading may have wider spreads and lower liquidity.

## Bid and Ask

### Bid

Highest current displayed buying price.

### Ask

Lowest current displayed selling price.

### Spread

Ask minus bid.

A wider spread increases transaction cost.

## Bid-Ask Spread Percentage

Spread percentage may be estimated as:

Ask minus bid
divided by midpoint price

Review the spread before submitting a material order.

## Market Depth

Market depth reflects available buy and sell quantities at different prices.

A displayed price may apply only to a small quantity.

Large orders may execute across multiple price levels.

## Partial Fill

A partial fill occurs when only part of the order executes.

Confirm:

- Quantity executed
- quantity remaining
- average price
- commission treatment
- order expiration
- need to cancel remainder

## Average Execution Price

When an order fills at several prices:

Average execution price equals:

Total transaction value
divided by total quantity executed

## Slippage

Slippage is the difference between:

- Expected price
- actual execution price

Possible causes include:

- Volatility
- low liquidity
- large order
- market order
- delayed data
- wide spread

## Trade Confirmation

After execution, review the official trade confirmation.

Confirm:

## Trade Date Versus Settlement Date

### Trade date

The date the transaction executes.

### Settlement date

The date cash and securities are formally exchanged.

Do not assume unsettled sale proceeds are immediately withdrawable.

## Settlement Risk

Potential settlement problems include:

- Insufficient cash
- failed transfer
- account restriction
- security delivery failure
- corporate action
- holiday
- currency conversion delay
- incorrect account registration

## Unsettled Funds

Using unsettled funds may create:

- account restriction
- trading violation
- interest
- failed settlement
- forced liquidation

Confirm broker rules before reusing sale proceeds.

## Cash Balance Categories

A brokerage account may display:

- Total cash
- settled cash
- available to trade
- available to withdraw
- reserved cash
- margin availability
- pending deposits
- pending withdrawals

These values are not interchangeable.

## Margin Availability

Margin availability is borrowed capacity, not cash owned by the investor.

Do not treat margin as part of emergency reserves or investable capital.

## Reconciliation

Reconciliation compares independent records to identify differences.

Reconcile:

- Bank transfer
- broker cash credit
- currency conversion
- trade confirmation
- settlement
- account statement
- investment register
- tax records

## Funding Reconciliation

Confirm:

Bank amount sent
minus transfer fees
minus conversion costs
equals amount credited

Investigate every material difference.

## Trade Reconciliation

Confirm:

Quantity multiplied by execution price
plus commission and fees
equals net cash movement

## Position Reconciliation

Confirm:

Beginning quantity
plus purchases
minus sales
plus reinvested shares
plus corporate-action adjustments
equals ending quantity

## Cash Reconciliation

Confirm:

Beginning cash
plus deposits
plus sale proceeds
plus income
minus purchases
minus withdrawals
minus fees
minus taxes
equals ending cash

## Cost Basis

Cost basis generally records the economic or tax starting value of an investment.

Possible components include:

- Purchase price
- commission
- eligible fees
- reinvested distributions
- corporate actions
- currency conversion

Tax rules determine the official reportable basis.

## Cost-Basis Methods

Possible methods include:

- First in, first out
- specific identification
- average cost
- other jurisdiction-specific methods

Confirm the applicable method before selling.

## Specific-Lot Selection

When supported, specific-lot selection may allow the investor to identify which shares are sold.

Before settlement, confirm:

- Lot date
- quantity
- cost basis
- holding period
- gain or loss
- broker election deadline

## Reinvested Distributions

A reinvested distribution may create:

- Taxable income
- additional shares
- additional cost basis
- separate acquisition lot

Failure to record reinvestment can overstate future taxable gain.

## Accumulating Funds

An accumulating fund may retain and reinvest income internally.

Tax treatment may differ by jurisdiction.

Do not assume absence of a cash distribution means absence of taxable income or reporting.

## Dividend and Interest Reconciliation

For each payment, record:

- Security
- record date
- ex-dividend date
- payment date
- gross amount
- withholding
- net amount
- currency
- reinvestment
- tax classification

## Foreign Withholding

Foreign withholding may appear on:

- Dividends
- interest
- fund distributions

Record the gross and withheld amounts separately.

Do not assume withholding completes all tax obligations.

## Corporate Actions

Possible corporate actions include:

- Stock split
- reverse split
- merger
- acquisition
- tender offer
- spin-off
- name change
- ticker change
- liquidation
- rights offering
- return of capital

Review official communications and update:

- Quantity
- cost basis
- security identifier
- tax records
- investment thesis

## Automatic Dividend Reinvestment

Before enabling automatic reinvestment, confirm:

- Fees
- fractional-share treatment
- tax records
- allocation impact
- currency conversion
- liquidity needs
- ability to disable

Automatic reinvestment may conflict with cash-flow or rebalancing plans.

## Recurring Investment Plan

A recurring purchase plan should define:

- Security
- amount
- frequency
- funding source
- reserve requirement
- maximum allocation
- cancellation process
- review date

Pause automatic investments when:

- Emergency reserve falls below target
- debt status changes
- tax obligation arises
- investment thesis changes
- account security concern exists

## Order Cancellation

After cancellation, confirm:

- Cancellation accepted
- no partial fill occurred
- no active remainder exists
- cash is released
- account records updated

Do not assume clicking cancel guarantees cancellation.

## Duplicate Order Risk

Duplicate orders can result from:

- Slow application response
- repeated submission
- mobile and desktop use
- recurring plan plus manual order
- browser refresh
- delayed confirmation

Before resubmitting, review open orders and executions.

## Incorrect Trade Response

If an incorrect trade occurs:

1. Stop additional trading.
2. Document the error.
3. Review open orders.
4. Contact the broker through an official channel.
5. Record representative and case number.
6. Determine whether correction is possible.
7. Avoid creating additional loss without analysis.
8. Review tax consequences.
9. Update incident records.
10. Strengthen the control that failed.

## Broker Error Versus Investor Error

Distinguish:

### Broker or system error

Examples:

- Incorrect execution
- duplicate processing
- account posting error
- fee discrepancy

### Investor entry error

Examples:

- Wrong ticker
- wrong quantity
- wrong order type
- wrong account

Document facts before assigning responsibility.

## Exception Classification

Classify discrepancies as:

### Informational

Small timing difference or expected display issue.

### Operational

Processing delay, missing document, or reconciliation difference.

### Financial

Incorrect fee, price, quantity, or cash amount.

### Security

Unauthorized activity or account compromise.

### Tax

Missing or incorrect cost basis, withholding, or classification.

## Escalation

For a material discrepancy:

1. Preserve screenshots and documents.
2. Record date and time.
3. Contact official support.
4. obtain case number.
5. confirm expected resolution.
6. record deadlines.
7. escalate through formal complaint channels where necessary.
8. obtain legal or tax review when material.
9. do not delete evidence.
10. update the exception register.

## Execution Stress Tests

### Scenario 1 — Price Moves Before Execution

Determine whether:

- Limit remains valid
- position size remains acceptable
- thesis changed
- order should be cancelled

### Scenario 2 — Currency Conversion Is Worse Than Expected

Recalculate:

- Net amount
- position size
- total fee
- expected return

### Scenario 3 — Partial Fill

Determine:

- Remaining order
- fees
- allocation
- whether to cancel

### Scenario 4 — Settlement Delay

Determine:

- Upcoming obligations
- withdrawal need
- alternative liquidity
- broker escalation

### Scenario 5 — Account Access Is Lost

Confirm:

- Recovery process
- trusted contact
- emergency support
- account security
- alternate liquidity

### Scenario 6 — Incorrect Security Purchased

Determine:

- Market exposure
- selling cost
- tax effect
- correction process
- operational-control failure

## Automatic Stop Conditions

Do not submit or continue a trade when:

- Security identifier is uncertain
- Funding instructions changed unexpectedly
- Account access appears compromised
- Order quantity cannot be independently verified
- Currency is unclear
- available cash includes margin unintentionally
- fee estimate is missing
- spread is unusually wide
- order preview differs from intention
- required tax review is incomplete
- transaction exceeds allocation limits
- emergency reserves would be affected
- emotional or rushed conditions exist

## Beginner Rules

1. Verify the account and security before trading.
2. Use official funding instructions.
3. Review the complete order preview.
4. Distinguish settled cash from trading availability.
5. Review every trade confirmation.
6. Reconcile cash and positions monthly.
7. Preserve cost-basis records.
8. Record distributions and withholding separately.
9. Test withdrawals from unfamiliar accounts.
10. Stop immediately when account or payment details appear inconsistent.
