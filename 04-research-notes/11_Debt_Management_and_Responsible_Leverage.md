# Lesson 35 — Debt Management, Interest-Cost Control, and Responsible Leverage

## Learning Objective

Learn how to inventory debt, calculate its true cost, prioritize repayment, evaluate refinancing, distinguish productive from destructive leverage, and prevent debt from undermining emergency reserves and long-term investing.

## Important Disclaimer

This lesson is educational.

Loan terms, prepayment rights, collection procedures, tax consequences, consumer protections, and secured-credit rules vary by jurisdiction and contract.

Current loan documents and qualified professional advice should be reviewed before refinancing, consolidating, transferring, or accelerating a material debt.

## Core Principle

Debt is not automatically good or bad.

Its effect depends on:

- Purpose
- Interest rate
- Fees
- Currency
- Collateral
- Payment stability
- Tax treatment
- Prepayment terms
- Income reliability
- Liquidity
- Risk of loss
- Alternatives

The correct question is not:

- Can I make the payment?

The correct question is:

- Can the household carry this obligation safely under adverse conditions?

## Current Balance Versus Payoff Amount

The displayed balance may differ from the amount required to close the loan.

The payoff amount may include:

- Accrued interest
- Administrative fees
- Insurance
- late charges
- prepayment adjustments
- legal costs
- cancellation costs

Request a current written payoff statement before final repayment or refinancing.

## Principal and Interest

A loan payment may include:

- Principal
- Interest
- Insurance
- Fees
- Taxes
- Indexation adjustment

Principal reduces the outstanding debt.

Interest is the financing cost.

A payment can be large while reducing principal slowly.

## Nominal and Effective Rates

A quoted rate may be:

- Monthly
- Nominal annual
- Effective annual
- Fixed
- Variable
- Promotional
- Before fees
- After fees
- Indexed

Rates should be converted to a consistent effective annual basis before comparison.

## Total Financing Cost

Total financing cost may include:

- Interest
- Origination fee
- appraisal
- legal fee
- mandatory insurance
- account fee
- late-payment fee
- currency conversion
- prepayment fee
- lien cancellation
- refinancing cost

A loan with a lower stated rate may have a higher total cost.

## Debt-Service Burden

Monthly debt service includes required payments for:

- Mortgage
- Vehicle
- Credit cards
- Personal loans
- Tax agreements
- Other recurring debt

Debt-service burden equals:

Total required monthly debt payments
divided by net monthly household income

Use net income for practical household planning.

## Fixed Versus Variable Debt

### Fixed-rate debt

Potential advantages:

- Predictable payment
- Easier cash-flow planning
- Protection from rate increases

Potential risks:

- Higher initial rate
- refinancing cost
- limited flexibility

### Variable-rate debt

Potential advantages:

- Lower initial cost in some cases
- benefit if reference rates decline

Potential risks:

- Payment increases
- uncertain total cost
- affordability pressure
- refinancing difficulty during stress

## Indexed Debt

Indexed obligations may change based on:

- Inflation
- reference rate
- currency
- published financial index

Record:

- Index
- adjustment frequency
- payment effect
- principal effect
- cap or floor
- historical variability

## Currency-Mismatch Debt

Currency mismatch occurs when debt and income are in different currencies.

Example:

- Debt in COP
- Income primarily in USD

Risks include:

- Exchange-rate movement
- transfer delays
- conversion spreads
- banking restrictions
- holiday timing

Maintain an appropriate payment reserve in the debt currency.

## Secured Debt

Secured debt is backed by collateral.

Examples:

- Mortgage
- Vehicle loan
- securities-backed loan
- property-secured personal loan

Failure to pay may result in loss of the collateral.

Secured debt may have a lower rate but higher consequence.

## Unsecured Debt

Unsecured debt may include:

- Credit cards
- Personal loans
- Some tax obligations
- Informal family loans

It may have:

- Higher interest
- shorter term
- collection risk
- legal consequences
- credit-reporting impact

## Productive Debt

Debt may be productive when it finances an asset or activity that reasonably supports:

- Income
- essential housing
- business capacity
- long-term value
- necessary transportation
- education with supportable economics

Productive purpose does not guarantee the debt is affordable.

## Destructive Debt

Debt becomes financially destructive when it:

- Funds recurring consumption
- covers chronic monthly deficits
- finances speculative investing
- replaces emergency reserves
- carries high compounding interest
- depends on uncertain future income
- cannot be repaid without selling essential assets

## Investment Leverage

Investment leverage uses borrowed funds to increase exposure.

Possible examples:

- Margin loan
- financed property
- securities-backed borrowing
- personal loan used to invest
- leveraged ETF
- derivative exposure

Leverage increases both gains and losses.

## Leverage Example

Without leverage:

- Investor contributes COP 100 million
- Asset falls 20%
- Loss equals COP 20 million

With 50% debt financing:

- Investor contributes COP 50 million
- Borrows COP 50 million
- Asset falls from COP 100 million to COP 80 million
- Debt remains approximately COP 50 million before costs
- Equity falls from COP 50 million to COP 30 million

The asset declined 20%, but investor equity declined 40%.

## Negative Equity

Negative equity occurs when:

Asset value
is less than
outstanding debt and selling costs

This can arise through:

- Property decline
- vehicle depreciation
- leveraged investment loss
- renovation overrun
- forced sale

## Debt and Emergency Reserves

Emergency reserves should not be reduced below policy limits merely to make an optional extra payment.

Compare:

- Interest saved
- liquidity lost
- upcoming obligations
- income reliability
- medical needs
- property risk
- refinancing access

## High-Interest Debt Priority

High-interest revolving debt often deserves priority because:

- Interest compounds rapidly
- Minimum payments reduce principal slowly
- New spending may continue
- Effective cost can exceed likely investment returns
- Liquidity becomes fragile

Investing aggressively while carrying expensive revolving debt can increase household risk.

## Debt-Payoff Methods

## Avalanche Method

Pay minimums on all debts and direct extra funds to the highest effective interest rate.

Potential advantage:

- Usually minimizes interest cost

Potential challenge:

- First payoff may take longer

## Snowball Method

Pay minimums on all debts and direct extra funds to the smallest balance.

Potential advantage:

- Faster visible progress
- behavioral motivation

Potential challenge:

- May cost more interest

## Hybrid Method

Possible approach:

1. Resolve delinquent or legally urgent debt.
2. Eliminate a small high-stress balance.
3. Prioritize highest-rate debt.
4. Preserve required reserves.
5. Review secured debt separately.

## Payoff Priority Factors

Consider:

- Effective rate
- variable-rate risk
- delinquency
- collateral risk
- tax consequences
- small-balance simplification
- currency mismatch
- prepayment terms
- legal urgency
- household stress
- liquidity

## Minimum Payment Trap

Paying only the minimum may:

- Extend repayment for years
- produce high cumulative interest
- keep utilization elevated
- create little principal reduction

Record the projected payoff period under minimum payments.

## Additional Principal Payments

Before sending extra money, confirm:

- Payment is applied to principal
- No prepayment penalty
- Written instructions required
- Term or payment changes
- interest recalculation
- future installments not merely prepaid
- receipt and balance update

## Reduce Term Versus Reduce Payment

### Reduce term

Continue similar payment and finish sooner.

Potential effect:

- Larger interest savings
- same monthly commitment

### Reduce payment

Recalculate to a lower payment.

Potential effect:

- Improved monthly cash flow
- possibly less interest savings

## Debt Consolidation

Debt consolidation combines obligations into one loan.

Potential benefits:

- Lower rate
- simpler payment
- fixed term
- reduced monthly burden

Potential risks:

- Longer repayment
- secured collateral
- higher total interest
- origination fees
- continued credit-card use
- temporary promotional rate

Consolidation does not solve overspending by itself.

## Refinancing

Refinancing replaces one debt with another.

Calculate:

Refinancing benefit =
Expected interest and fee savings
minus refinancing costs
minus prepayment and cancellation costs

Also consider:

- New term
- collateral
- variable-rate exposure
- insurance
- legal fees
- time to break even

## Refinancing Break-Even

Break-even months equal:

Total refinancing cost
divided by monthly payment savings

A refinance may be unattractive when the expected holding period is shorter than the break-even period.

## Balance Transfer

A balance transfer may offer:

- Promotional rate
- transfer fee
- limited period
- higher post-promotional rate

Record:

- Transfer fee
- promotional expiration
- required monthly payoff
- new purchases treatment
- penalty for late payment

## Debt Payoff Versus Investing

Compare:

### Paying debt

Potential benefits:

- Predictable interest savings
- reduced required payments
- lower risk
- improved cash flow
- less collateral exposure

### Investing

Potential benefits:

- Long-term growth
- diversification
- liquidity depending on product
- inflation protection

Investment returns are uncertain.

Interest avoided through payoff is more predictable, subject to contract and tax treatment.

## After-Tax Debt Cost

Where tax treatment is relevant, compare:

- Gross interest rate
- deductible portion
- actual tax benefit
- fees
- insurance
- currency effect

Do not assume interest is deductible without professional confirmation.

## Debt Snowball Risk After Payoff

When a debt is eliminated, the former payment can be redirected to:

- Next debt
- emergency reserve
- retirement investment
- property reserve
- tax reserve

Without a plan, the freed payment may disappear into spending.

## Credit Utilization

Credit utilization generally compares revolving balances with limits.

High utilization may indicate:

- liquidity pressure
- increased interest cost
- dependence on credit
- reduced borrowing flexibility

Do not open or close accounts solely for a score without reviewing total consequences.

## Delinquent Debt

For delinquent debt:

1. Confirm creditor.
2. Verify balance.
3. obtain written account history.
4. identify collection status.
5. review legal deadlines.
6. avoid unauthorized payment channels.
7. obtain professional advice where material.
8. retain settlement documentation.

## Debt Settlement

A proposed settlement should be reviewed for:

- Amount
- creditor authority
- tax consequences
- legal release
- reporting
- payment deadline
- receiving account
- final confirmation
- remaining balance

A payment without written release may not resolve the obligation.

## Co-Signed Debt

A co-signer or guarantor may be responsible when the primary borrower does not pay.

Record:

- Borrower
- creditor
- maximum exposure
- current balance
- payment status
- collateral
- release conditions

Treat guaranteed debt as a contingent liability.

## Family Loans

Document family loans with:

- Parties
- amount
- currency
- date
- interest
- payment terms
- purpose
- remaining balance
- forgiveness terms
- estate implications

Informal debt can create family conflict and unclear estate records.

## Debt and Retirement

Debt in retirement can increase sensitivity to:

- income interruption
- medical costs
- inflation
- currency changes
- market decline
- property repair

Evaluate whether required payments can be supported by reliable income without forced asset sales.

## Mortgage in Retirement

Review:

- payment percentage of reliable income
- remaining term
- rate type
- insurance
- maintenance
- property taxes
- currency
- prepayment
- emergency reserve
- estate plan

A low rate may support retaining the mortgage, but only if liquidity and risk remain acceptable.

## Debt and Property Investing

Before financing a property, calculate:

- Total acquisition cost
- Monthly debt service
- administration
- taxes
- insurance
- vacancy
- repairs
- legal costs
- payment reserve
- debt-service coverage

Rental income should be modeled conservatively.

## Debt-Service Coverage Ratio

For income-producing property:

Debt-service coverage ratio equals:

Net operating income
divided by annual debt payments

A value below 1 means operating income is insufficient to cover debt service.

## Interest-Coverage Reserve

A property reserve may hold several months of:

- principal and interest
- administration
- insurance
- property tax
- utilities
- maintenance

## Margin Debt

Margin debt can create:

- forced liquidation
- margin call
- rapid loss
- interest-rate risk
- sale during market decline

A broker may liquidate positions without waiting for the investor’s preferred timing.

## Leveraged ETFs

Leveraged ETFs typically target a multiple of daily index performance.

Risks include:

- path dependency
- daily reset
- volatility drag
- compounding effects
- large losses
- mismatch with long-term expectations

They are not equivalent to ordinary broad-market ETFs.

## Debt Stress Tests

## Scenario 1 — Income Decline

Reduce employment or consulting income.

Calculate:

- debt-service ratio
- monthly deficit
- reserve duration
- required reductions

## Scenario 2 — Rate Increase

For variable debt, model:

- 2 percentage-point increase
- 4 percentage-point increase
- payment recalculation

## Scenario 3 — COP Strengthens

For debt in COP supported by USD income, reduce converted income.

## Scenario 4 — Property Vacancy

Remove rental income for:

- Three months
- Six months

## Scenario 5 — Major Medical Expense

Add a large immediate cost while debt payments continue.

## Scenario 6 — Asset Value Decline

Reduce collateral value while leaving debt unchanged.

## Scenario 7 — Combined Stress

Assume:

- Income decline
- rate increase
- medical expense
- asset decline

## Debt Risk Status

### Green

- Payments affordable from reliable income
- no delinquency
- reserves funded
- high-interest debt controlled
- variable-rate exposure manageable
- collateral risk acceptable

### Yellow

- Payments depend partly on variable income
- reserve below target
- high utilization
- refinancing required
- currency mismatch
- one payment delay would create stress

### Red

- Debt paid through new debt
- delinquency
- essential assets at risk
- emergency reserve depleted
- speculative investment financed
- payment depends on expected job or sale
- minimum payments unsustainable

## Debt Decision Gate

Before borrowing, confirm:

- Purpose is defined
- total cost is known
- payment fits reliable income
- adverse scenarios are affordable
- emergency reserve remains funded
- collateral risk is understood
- currency risk is reviewed
- alternative financing was compared
- prepayment terms are understood
- professional review completed where required

## New-Debt Prohibitions

Do not borrow for:

- Recovering an investment loss
- Unverified opportunity
- Emergency reserve replacement
- Auction bid without full funding plan
- Lifestyle spending unsupported by income
- Investment promising guaranteed returns
- Property with unresolved title or possession
- Renovation without scope and contingency

## Debt Review Calendar

### Monthly

- Confirm payments
- reconcile balances
- review interest
- check utilization
- update extra payments

### Quarterly

- Review payoff priority
- review refinancing options
- check reserve adequacy
- review variable rates
- update contingent liabilities

### Annually

- Obtain current payoff balances
- update debt inventory
- recalculate total interest
- review mortgage strategy
- review estate implications
- update debt-free targets

## Debt-Free Date

For each debt, calculate or estimate:

- Current scheduled payoff date
- Payoff date with extra payment
- Interest saved
- Liquidity effect
- reserve effect

## Debt Reduction Plan

A complete plan includes:

- Priority order
- minimum payments
- additional payment
- source of extra funds
- reserve floor
- target dates
- refinancing conditions
- stop conditions
- review schedule

## Warning Signs

Pause new investment activity when:

- Credit-card balance is revolving at high cost
- Debt inventory is incomplete
- Payments depend on anticipated income
- One missed payment threatens collateral
- Currency mismatch is untested
- Variable-rate debt is unaffordable after an increase
- Extra payments would deplete emergency reserves
- Investment is financed with unsecured debt
- Refinancing extends debt without clear benefit
- Family guarantees are undocumented

## Beginner Rules

1. Inventory every debt.
2. Compare effective annual cost, not only the payment.
3. Preserve required emergency reserves.
4. Prioritize delinquent and high-cost debt.
5. Confirm how extra payments are applied.
6. Evaluate refinancing using total cost.
7. Avoid borrowing to speculate.
8. Stress-test variable and foreign-currency debt.
9. Treat guarantees as contingent liabilities.
10. Review debt before every material investment.
