# Lesson 64 — Capstone Investment Plan, Integrated Case Study, and Competency Assessment

## Learning Objective

Apply the complete curriculum to a realistic household investment case by producing an evidence-based investment plan, ETF comparison, risk assessment, implementation decision, monitoring framework, and final competency record.

## Important Disclaimer

This capstone is educational.

It is not individualized investment, tax, legal, estate, accounting, insurance, or real-estate advice.

A completed capstone demonstrates a structured learning process. It does not prove professional licensing, regulatory authorization, guaranteed competence, or investment suitability.

## Capstone Purpose

The capstone tests whether the learner can integrate:

- Household financial goals
- emergency reserves
- debt and liquidity
- Colombian investments
- cross-border taxation
- ETF due diligence
- broker and custody controls
- portfolio construction
- execution
- monitoring
- fraud prevention
- continuity planning
- evidence governance

## Core Principle

A strong investment decision is not simply:

- Selecting a popular ETF
- choosing the highest historical return
- following an online recommendation
- maximizing yield
- minimizing one fee

A strong decision connects:

1. Household objective
2. financial capacity
3. evidence
4. risk
5. product structure
6. tax and legal considerations
7. execution controls
8. monitoring
9. continuity
10. documented approval

## Case Study

Use the fictional household below.

### Household

Names:

- Carlos Rivera, age 67
- Elena Rivera, age 55
- one dependent child, age 13

Residence:

- Chía, Cundinamarca, Colombia

Status:

- Carlos receives retirement income from the United States.
- Elena currently has no employment income.
- The household expects to remain in Colombia.
- The family may purchase a vehicle or residential property within several years.

### Monthly Income

United States pension income:

USD 2,900 gross per month

Estimated net amount after routine deductions:

USD 2,750 per month

Other reliable income:

COP 0

### Monthly Household Spending

Essential spending:

COP 7,200,000

Discretionary spending:

COP 1,200,000

Medical and insurance reserve contribution:

COP 600,000

Total monthly planned cash need:

COP 9,000,000

### Current Assets

Colombian bank cash:

COP 42,000,000

United States bank cash:

USD 18,000

Existing diversified ETF portfolio:

USD 38,000

Potential vehicle down payment:

COP 25,000,000

Possible property objective:

COP 180,000,000 within five years

### Liabilities

Credit-card balance:

COP 6,000,000

Interest rate:

Assume materially higher than expected investment return

Vehicle loan:

None

Mortgage:

None

### Risk Considerations

- Household depends primarily on one pension.
- Spending is primarily in COP.
- Investments are primarily in USD.
- Carlos is already retired.
- Elena may require survivor-income planning.
- The dependent child creates an additional continuity need.
- The household has medical-cost uncertainty.
- Property and vehicle goals compete with long-term investing.
- Cross-border tax and estate questions require professional review.

## Case Assumptions

For the exercise:

- Use only fictional values.
- Do not use live trading.
- Do not present assumed tax rates as current law.
- Do not identify one real ETF as automatically suitable.
- Use clearly labeled sample assumptions.
- Cite official sources when actual products are researched.
- Separate known facts from estimates.

## Phase 1 — Household Financial Foundation

### Step 1 — Define Goals

Identify:

- Emergency reserve
- monthly operating liquidity
- vehicle goal
- property goal
- retirement-income stability
- long-term growth
- survivor protection
- dependent support
- estate objective

### Step 2 — Prioritize Goals

Classify each goal as:

- Essential
- important
- optional

### Step 3 — Define Time Horizon

Use:

- Immediate: less than 1 year
- short term: 1 to 3 years
- medium term: 3 to 7 years
- long term: more than 7 years

### Step 4 — Define Currency

Identify whether each goal is primarily in:

- COP
- USD
- another currency

### Step 5 — Review Debt

High-cost debt should be compared with:

- Expected investment return
- guaranteed interest savings
- liquidity needs
- emergency reserves

### Step 6 — Calculate Emergency Reserve

Emergency reserve target may be based on:

Essential monthly spending
multiplied by
target months

### Step 7 — Avoid Double Counting

Funds assigned to:

- Emergency reserve
- property down payment
- vehicle purchase
- tax reserve
- medical reserve

cannot simultaneously be treated as long-term investment capital.

## Phase 2 — Investable Capital

### Gross Financial Assets

Add:

- COP bank cash
- USD bank cash converted to reporting currency
- existing ETF portfolio
- other liquid investments

### Less Restricted Funds

Subtract:

- Emergency reserve
- medical reserve
- tax reserve
- near-term vehicle funds
- property funds
- debt payoff amount
- pending large expenses

### Investable Capital

Investable capital equals:

Gross liquid financial assets
minus
restricted and required funds

## Phase 5 — ETF Due Diligence

Complete full due diligence for at least three fictional or clearly identified educational candidates.

Candidate categories:

1. Broad global-equity ETF
2. high-quality short-duration bond ETF
3. emerging-market or Colombia-related investment candidate

## ETF Review Questions

1. What index does the fund track?
2. What is the fund domicile?
3. What is the distribution policy?
4. What are the main holdings?
5. What are the largest sector and country exposures?
6. What currency risks exist?
7. What is the expense ratio?
8. What is the bid-ask spread?
9. What is the fund size?
10. What is the tracking history?
11. Does the fund lend securities?
12. Does it use derivatives?
13. What broker and account restrictions apply?
14. What tax and estate questions remain?
15. What would cause the position to be rejected?

## Phase 10 — Stress Testing

Complete at least four scenarios.

### Scenario 1 — USD Weakens Against COP

Assess:

- Pension purchasing power
- ETF value in COP
- monthly funding gap
- reserve sufficiency

### Scenario 2 — Equity Market Falls 30%

Assess:

- Portfolio drawdown
- withdrawal requirement
- property-goal effect
- behavioral response
- rebalancing decision

### Scenario 3 — Pension Payment Is Delayed 90 Days

Assess:

- Operating reserve
- emergency reserve
- transfer backup
- essential spending

### Scenario 4 — Medical Expense of COP 25,000,000

Assess:

- Reserve use
- portfolio sale
- property-goal delay
- insurance
- household continuity

### Optional Scenario 5 — Combined Shock

Combine:

- Equity decline
- COP appreciation
- pension delay
- medical expense

## Reverse Stress Test

Determine what combination of:

- Market loss
- currency movement
- income interruption
- medical cost
- withdrawal
- inflation

would cause the household to fail to meet essential spending.

## Evidence Standard

Every material factual claim should identify:

- Source
- access date
- relevant document
- confidence
- limitation

## Assumption Standard

Every assumption should identify:

- Value
- purpose

## Uncertainty Standard

Do not conceal uncertainty.

Use language such as:

- Estimated

- requires verification

- professional conclusion required

## Scoring Model

Each domain may receive:

- 0 — Not demonstrated
- 1 — Partially demonstrated
- 2 — Adequately demonstrated
- 3 — Strongly demonstrated
- 4 — Demonstrated with clear evidence and limitations

Maximum score:

40

## Suggested Interpretation

### 34 to 40

Strong educational mastery

### 27 to 33

Adequate mastery with targeted improvement required

### 20 to 26

Partial mastery; additional study required

### Below 20

Foundational review required before implementation

This scoring method is educational and not a professional credential.

## Critical-Failure Conditions

Regardless of total score, the capstone should not pass when the learner:

- Uses emergency funds as unrestricted investment capital
- ignores high-cost debt without analysis
- recommends a product that cannot be identified
- omits tax and estate uncertainty
- uses unsupported guaranteed-return claims
- fails to assess currency mismatch
- omits fraud and custody controls
- relies on unverified data
- proposes live execution without approval controls
- publishes private household information

- the final recommendation contradicts the analysis
