# Lesson 31 — Personal Balance Sheet, Net Worth, and Investable Capital

## Learning Objective

Learn how to prepare a personal balance sheet, calculate net worth, distinguish liquid from illiquid assets, identify financial concentration, and determine how much capital is genuinely available for investment.

## Important Privacy Rule

The repository should contain only:

- This educational lesson
- Blank templates
- Hypothetical examples
- Redacted summaries

A completed personal balance sheet should be stored in an encrypted private vault outside the Git repository.

Do not commit:

- Account numbers
- Exact institution identifiers
- Identification numbers
- Private statements
- Tax returns
- Property deeds
- Beneficiary information
- Passwords or credentials

## Core Principle

Net worth is not the same as investable capital.

A household may have substantial net worth while having limited money available for investment because wealth is held in:

- A primary residence
- Restricted retirement assets
- Long-term CDTs
- Illiquid property
- Business ownership
- Assets required for near-term expenses
- Emergency reserves

## Personal Balance Sheet

A personal balance sheet records:

### Assets

What the household owns.

### Liabilities

What the household owes.

### Net worth

Assets minus liabilities.

## Net-Worth Formula

Net worth equals:

Total assets
minus total liabilities

A positive net worth does not automatically mean the household can tolerate investment losses.

## Valuation Date

Every balance sheet should use one specific valuation date.

Record:

- Date
- Exchange rate
- Market prices
- Property-value source
- Loan balances
- Account balances
- Unpaid obligations

Do not combine values from different dates without explanation.

## Base Reporting Currency

Select one base reporting currency.

Possible choices include:

- Colombian pesos
- US dollars

For a Colombia-based household, COP may be practical for household planning.

For cross-border comparison, a parallel USD summary may also be useful.

Use a documented exchange rate and valuation date.

## Asset Categories

Common asset categories include:

- Cash
- Savings accounts
- CDTs
- Brokerage accounts
- ETFs
- Stocks
- Bonds
- Pension or retirement accounts
- Primary residence
- Rental property
- Distressed property
- Vehicle
- Business ownership
- Insurance cash value where applicable
- Other valuable property
- Receivables

## Cash and Cash Equivalents

Examples:

- Checking account
- Savings account
- Money-market-like account
- Short-term deposit
- Immediately redeemable cash product

Record:

- Currency
- Current balance
- Institution
- Availability
- Deposit protection review
- Purpose

## Emergency Reserve

The emergency reserve is an asset, but it should not normally be classified as investable capital.

Its purpose may include:

- Medical emergency
- Income interruption
- Family emergency
- Housing
- Vehicle repair
- Unexpected travel
- Legal expense

## CDTs

For each CDT, record:

- Principal
- Maturity value
- Maturity date
- Currency
- Institution
- Early-withdrawal restriction
- Intended purpose

A CDT maturing next year may be liquid eventually but unavailable today.

## Investment Accounts

Record:

- Account type
- Currency
- Current market value
- Tax jurisdiction
- Liquidity
- Restrictions
- Cost basis where relevant
- Intended objective

Do not double-count cash held inside an investment account.

## Retirement and Pension Assets

Possible entries include:

- Employer retirement plan
- Individual retirement account
- Foreign pension
- Voluntary pension
- Other retirement account

Record:

- Current value
- Access restrictions
- Tax questions
- Beneficiary status
- Whether the asset can be used before a certain age or condition

Restricted retirement assets should not be counted as immediately investable capital.

## Primary Residence

The primary residence may represent substantial wealth but limited liquidity.

Record:

- Conservative current value
- Mortgage balance
- Estimated selling cost
- Estimated net equity
- Ownership percentage
- Currency
- Valuation source

Do not use the highest advertised neighborhood price.

## Property Equity

Approximate property equity equals:

Conservative property value
minus mortgage balance
minus estimated selling costs

Equity is not equivalent to cash unless the property is sold or financed.

## Rental Property

Record:

- Conservative market value
- Loan balance
- Estimated selling costs
- Net equity
- Monthly net cash flow
- Vacancy
- Repair reserve
- Legal or occupancy issues

Do not value a property without considering its condition and occupancy.

## Distressed Property

Record separately:

- Cash already committed
- Current registered status
- Possession status
- Estimated current value
- Legal cost remaining
- Repair cost remaining
- Carrying cost
- Estimated net realizable value

Do not value unresolved distressed property as though it were renovated, vacant, and immediately saleable.

## Vehicles

A vehicle may be an asset but generally depreciates.

Record:

- Conservative resale value
- Loan balance
- Estimated net value
- Essential-use status

A necessary household vehicle should not normally be treated as investable capital.

## Personal Property

Examples:

- Jewelry
- Art
- Collectibles
- Electronics
- Furniture

Use conservative resale values, not replacement cost.

Exclude ordinary personal property when valuation is unreliable or immaterial.

## Business Ownership

Business value may be difficult to estimate.

Record:

- Ownership percentage
- Conservative value
- Business debt
- Distributions
- Restrictions on sale
- Dependence on the owner
- Valuation method

Do not count projected future business revenue as a current asset.

## Receivables

Money owed to the household may include:

- Formal loan
- Business receivable
- Property-sale balance
- Tax refund

Record:

- Debtor
- Amount
- Due date
- Documentation
- Collection risk
- Probability of payment

An uncertain receivable should be discounted or excluded.

## Liability Categories

Common liabilities include:

- Mortgage
- Vehicle loan
- Credit cards
- Personal loans
- Business debt
- Taxes payable
- Medical debt
- Property obligations
- Family loans
- Legal obligations
- Unpaid administration fees
- Contractor balances

## Current Versus Long-Term Liabilities

### Current liabilities

Amounts due within approximately twelve months.

Examples:

- Credit-card balances
- Taxes payable
- Current loan payments
- Contractor invoices
- Property closing obligations

### Long-term liabilities

Amounts due beyond twelve months.

Examples:

- Mortgage
- Long-term vehicle loan
- Long-term personal loan

## Mortgage Liability

Record:

- Current principal
- Interest rate
- Currency or index
- Monthly payment
- Remaining term
- Prepayment rules
- Collateral

Do not use the original loan amount as the current liability.

## Credit-Card Liability

Record:

- Current statement balance
- Total balance
- Interest rate
- Minimum payment
- Due date
- Currency

High-interest revolving debt may take priority over new investments.

## Tax Liabilities

Record known or estimated obligations for:

- US tax
- Colombian tax
- Property tax
- Foreign-assets reporting penalties where relevant
- Withholding shortfalls
- Estimated tax payments

Maintain a separate tax reserve when an obligation is expected.

## Contingent Liabilities

A contingent liability may arise only if an uncertain event occurs.

Examples:

- Legal claim
- Loan guarantee
- Property dispute
- Contractor dispute
- Tax audit
- Co-signed debt
- Family obligation

Document:

- Maximum exposure
- Probability
- Professional assessment
- Reserve required

## Gross Assets

Gross assets equal the total value of all assets before liabilities.

This number can be misleading if most assets are illiquid.

## Liquid Net Worth

Liquid net worth focuses on assets that can be accessed within a reasonable period.

Possible formula:

Liquid financial assets
minus short-term liabilities

Exclude or separately identify:

- Primary residence
- Illiquid property
- Restricted pensions
- Personal vehicles
- Business interests
- Disputed assets

## Investable Assets

Investable assets may include:

- Cash beyond required reserves
- Brokerage holdings
- Maturing investments not assigned to short-term goals
- Long-term investment accounts
- Capital specifically designated for investment

## Investable Capital

Investable capital is the amount available for a new investment after preserving required reserves and obligations.

Educational formula:

Investable capital equals:

Liquid assets
minus emergency reserve
minus near-term spending needs
minus tax reserve
minus property reserve
minus medical reserve
minus required debt payments
minus other committed funds

## Committed Capital

Committed capital includes money already assigned to:

- Vehicle purchase
- Property down payment
- Medical expenses
- Taxes
- Education
- Travel
- Legal fees
- Renovation
- Family support
- Loan payoff

Committed capital should not be counted as available for another investment.

## Required Reserves

Possible reserves include:

- Emergency reserve
- Medical reserve
- Tax reserve
- Property reserve
- Vehicle reserve
- Legal reserve
- Family-support reserve
- Currency reserve
- Insurance reserve

## Net Investable-Capital Formula

Net investable capital equals:

Cash and liquid investments
minus all required reserves
minus current liabilities
minus committed near-term spending

This amount may be substantially lower than total net worth.

## Net-Worth Categories

A useful classification is:

### Tier 1 — Immediate liquidity

Accessible within one day.

### Tier 2 — Near liquidity

Accessible within thirty to ninety days.

### Tier 3 — Marketable investments

Can normally be sold but may fluctuate in value.

### Tier 4 — Restricted assets

Pension or contractually restricted assets.

### Tier 5 — Illiquid property

Real estate and business interests.

## Liquidity Ratio

Liquidity ratio may be measured as:

Liquid assets
divided by monthly essential expenses

This estimates how many months of essential expenses are covered by liquid assets.

## Debt-to-Asset Ratio

Debt-to-asset ratio equals:

Total liabilities
divided by total assets

A lower ratio generally means less leverage, but the composition of assets and debt also matters.

## Debt-to-Income Ratio

Debt-to-income ratio compares required monthly debt payments with income.

Use both:

- Gross income
- Net income

Net-income comparison is often more useful for household affordability.

## Property Concentration

Property concentration equals:

Net property value
divided by total net worth

Also calculate:

Total cash committed to property
divided by total investable assets

A household can become overly concentrated even when the property appears profitable.

## Country Concentration

Calculate exposure by country.

Possible categories include:

- Colombia
- United States
- Other countries

Include:

- Property
- Bank accounts
- Investments
- Income
- Debt
- Currency
- Employment exposure

## Currency Concentration

Calculate asset and liability exposure to:

- COP
- USD
- Other currencies

Record separately:

- Assets
- Income
- Expenses
- Debt
- Planned purchases

A USD asset does not necessarily offset a COP liability over a short period.

## Institution Concentration

Calculate total exposure to each:

- Bank
- Broker
- Fund manager
- Property manager
- Custodian

Consider both:

- Cash
- Securities
- Debt

## Net-Worth Reconciliation

At each update:

Beginning net worth
plus savings
plus investment gains
plus property-value changes
minus spending from capital
minus investment losses
minus new debt
equals approximate ending net worth

Large unexplained differences should be investigated.

## Monthly Versus Annual Updates

### Monthly

Update:

- Cash
- Credit cards
- Loans
- Investment accounts
- Current liabilities

### Quarterly

Update:

- Portfolio allocation
- Currency exposure
- Institution concentration
- Investable capital

### Annually

Update:

- Property values
- Business interests
- estate inventory
- Long-term liabilities
- retirement accounts
- full net worth

## Conservative Valuation Rules

Use:

- Current account balance
- Current market value
- Realistic property value
- Current loan payoff
- Probable receivable value
- Estimated selling costs

Avoid:

- Highest historical value
- Unverified asking price
- Projected future appreciation
- Gross property value without debt
- Business revenue presented as company value
- Full value of disputed assets

## Double-Counting Risks

Common errors include:

- Counting a brokerage account and its holdings separately
- Counting property value and property equity
- Counting cash reserved for a purchase as free capital
- Counting pension income and pension-account value incorrectly
- Counting gross business assets and personal ownership value
- Counting a receivable and the asset purchased with it
- Counting foreign-currency balances twice after conversion

## Balance-Sheet Stress Tests

## Scenario 1 — Equity Markets Fall 30%

Reduce marketable equity investments by 30%.

Recalculate:

- Net worth
- Liquid net worth
- Investable capital
- Allocation
- Emergency coverage

## Scenario 2 — Property Values Fall 20%

Reduce property values by 20% while keeping mortgage balances unchanged.

Recalculate:

- Property equity
- Net worth
- Loan-to-value
- Concentration

## Scenario 3 — COP Strengthens

Revalue USD assets and income in COP.

Determine:

- Effect on COP net worth
- Effect on monthly cash flow
- Effect on property affordability

## Scenario 4 — Major Medical Expense

Subtract a large emergency expense.

Determine:

- Reserve remaining
- Investable capital remaining
- Assets requiring liquidation
- Need to delay purchases

## Scenario 5 — Employment Income Stops

Do not change net worth immediately.

Recalculate:

- Monthly deficit
- Reserve duration
- Debt affordability
- Planned investment amount

## Scenario 6 — Property Requires Major Repair

Create a liability or committed reserve for:

- Repair
- Legal cost
- Vacancy
- temporary accommodation
- financing

## Scenario 7 — Tax Obligation Is Higher Than Expected

Increase tax reserve and recalculate investable capital.

## Investment Capacity Decision

A new investment should be considered only after determining:

- Total net worth
- Liquid net worth
- Emergency reserves
- Committed capital
- Current liabilities
- Currency needs
- Concentration
- Worst-case scenario
- Net investable capital

## Capital Allocation Limits

Define maximum amounts for:

- One ETF
- One broker
- One bank
- One CDT issuer
- One property
- Distressed property
- Renovation
- Individual stocks
- Specialized investments

Use investable assets rather than total property-inclusive net worth when setting liquid-investment limits.

## Confidential Personal Balance Sheet

The completed personal statement should show:

- Assets
- Liabilities
- Net worth
- Liquid net worth
- Investable assets
- Required reserves
- Investable capital
- Concentration metrics
- Review date

Store the completed version privately.

## Warning Signs

Pause new investing when:

- Emergency reserves are incomplete
- Current liabilities are unknown
- Tax obligations are unreserved
- Near-term purchases are unfunded
- High-interest debt remains unresolved
- Property would dominate net worth
- Investable capital is being confused with net worth
- Income interruption would require immediate asset sales
- Currency exposure is unknown
- Asset values are based on optimistic estimates
- Completed financial records are stored in Git

## Beginner Rules

1. Use one valuation date.
2. Use one base reporting currency.
3. Value assets conservatively.
4. Record current liabilities accurately.
5. Separate net worth from investable capital.
6. Exclude emergency reserves from investment funds.
7. Avoid double counting.
8. Measure property and currency concentration.
9. Stress-test the balance sheet.
10. Store completed personal records securely outside Git.
