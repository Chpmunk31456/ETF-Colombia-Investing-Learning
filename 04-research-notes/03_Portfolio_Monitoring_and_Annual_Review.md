# Lesson 16 — Portfolio Monitoring, Rebalancing, and Annual Review

## Learning Objective

Learn how to monitor investments without reacting emotionally to daily market movements, rebalance systematically, update records, and conduct a structured annual portfolio review.

## Important Principle

Monitoring is not the same as constant trading.

The purpose of monitoring is to confirm that:

- Investments still serve their intended purpose
- Risk remains within acceptable limits
- Liquidity needs are covered
- Allocation remains near target
- Fees remain reasonable
- Tax and reporting obligations are current
- Legal or operational risks have not changed
- Personal circumstances still support the strategy

## What Should Be Monitored

Monitor:

- Total portfolio value
- Asset allocation
- Currency allocation
- Emergency reserve
- Income received
- Fees
- Tax withholding
- Investment performance
- Portfolio drawdown
- Property cash flow
- CDT maturity dates
- Broker and institution exposure
- Regulatory or legal changes
- Unresolved tax questions

## What Should Not Drive Decisions

Avoid changing the portfolio solely because of:

- Daily market headlines
- Social-media predictions
- Short-term price declines
- Fear of missing out
- One strong month
- One weak quarter
- Political commentary
- Market forecasts
- Unverified AI predictions
- Recent performance rankings

## Monitoring Frequency

### Daily

Usually unnecessary for long-term investors.

Daily review may increase:

- Anxiety
- Overtrading
- Reaction to noise
- Poor timing decisions

### Monthly

Appropriate for:

- Updating account balances
- Recording contributions
- Recording dividends and interest
- Reviewing cash flow
- Checking fraud or unauthorized activity

### Quarterly

Appropriate for:

- Allocation review
- ETF overlap review
- Broker concentration
- Property income and expenses
- CDT maturity planning
- Currency exposure review

### Annually

Appropriate for:

- Full investment-policy review
- Rebalancing
- Tax-document preparation
- Risk-capacity reassessment
- Beneficiary review
- Professional review
- Long-term goal assessment

## Rebalancing Methods

### New-contribution rebalancing

Direct new money toward underweight categories.

Potential advantages:

- Avoids selling
- May reduce tax consequences
- Lowers transaction costs
- Encourages disciplined contributions

### Cash-flow rebalancing

Use:

- Dividends
- Interest
- Rental income
- CDT maturities
- Property sale proceeds

to restore target allocations.

### Sale-and-purchase rebalancing

Sell overweight assets and buy underweight assets.

Before doing so, review:

- Capital gains
- Tax consequences
- Trading costs
- Bid-ask spreads
- Currency conversion
- Withdrawal needs
- Professional advice

## Rebalancing Bands

Possible educational rule:

- Review every six months
- Rebalance when an asset class differs from target by more than 5 percentage points
- Use contributions and cash flows first
- Sell only when necessary

Example:

- Target equity allocation: 40%
- Rebalancing band: 35% to 45%
- Action considered only if allocation moves outside the band

## Calendar Rebalancing

Calendar rebalancing occurs on a fixed schedule.

Examples:

- Every six months
- Annually
- On the investor’s birthday
- At year-end
- After tax preparation

Advantages:

- Simple
- Predictable
- Reduces emotional decisions

Disadvantages:

- May trigger unnecessary trades
- May ignore large allocation changes between review dates

## Threshold Rebalancing

Threshold rebalancing occurs when allocation moves outside a defined range.

Advantages:

- Responds to meaningful drift
- Avoids unnecessary calendar trades

Disadvantages:

- Requires regular monitoring
- May create more frequent transactions

## Hybrid Rebalancing

A hybrid rule may combine:

- Quarterly review
- Annual formal rebalance
- Immediate action only when allocation exceeds a defined limit

## Performance Measurement

Track performance using:

- Beginning value
- Contributions
- Withdrawals
- Income
- Ending value
- Fees
- Taxes
- Currency effects

Do not confuse portfolio return with the change in account balance.

A higher balance may result from new contributions rather than investment performance.

## Time-Weighted Return

Time-weighted return measures investment performance while reducing the effect of deposits and withdrawals.

It is useful for comparing:

- Fund managers
- ETFs
- Portfolio strategies
- Benchmarks

## Money-Weighted Return

Money-weighted return reflects:

- Timing of contributions
- Timing of withdrawals
- Investment performance

It is closer to the investor’s personal experience.

## Benchmarking

Each investment should have an appropriate benchmark.

Examples:

- Broad equity ETF compared with its official index
- Bond ETF compared with a similar-duration bond index
- CDT compared with similar-term deposits
- Rental property compared with realistic local rental returns
- Distressed property compared with total capital committed

Do not compare unlike investments solely by return.

## Portfolio Drawdown

Drawdown measures decline from a previous portfolio high.

Track:

- Current drawdown
- Maximum historical drawdown
- Time to recovery
- Which assets caused the decline

A drawdown should be compared with the risk limits in the Investment Policy Statement.

## Income Tracking

Track gross and net income separately.

Possible income sources:

- CDT interest
- Bond interest
- ETF dividends
- Stock dividends
- Rental income
- Property sale proceeds
- Savings interest

Record:

- Gross income
- Tax withheld
- Fees
- Net income
- Currency
- Payment date

## Fee Monitoring

Review:

- ETF expense ratios
- Broker commissions
- Bid-ask spreads
- Custody fees
- Currency-conversion spreads
- Withdrawal fees
- Fund-management fees
- Property-management fees
- Legal costs
- Tax-preparation costs

A small recurring fee can materially affect long-term results.

## CDT Monitoring

Track:

- Institution
- Principal
- Effective annual rate
- Opening date
- Maturity date
- Renewal instructions
- Withholding
- Expected maturity value
- Purpose of funds

Create alerts well before maturity.

## Property Monitoring

Track:

- Property value estimate
- Rent collected
- Vacancy
- Administration fees
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Repairs
- Legal expenses
- Mortgage balance
- Net operating income
- Cap rate
- Cash-on-cash return
- Occupancy status

For distressed property, also track:

- Case status
- Possession status
- Attorney updates
- Registration progress
- Additional legal costs

## Broker and Institution Monitoring

Review:

- Regulatory status
- Account access
- MFA settings
- Authorized devices
- Beneficiary information
- Cash protection
- Custody arrangements
- New fees
- Service changes
- Account restrictions
- Security incidents

## Tax and Reporting Calendar

Track deadlines for:

- Colombian income-tax filings
- Colombian foreign-assets reporting
- US federal income-tax filing
- FBAR
- Form 8938
- Form 8621
- Estimated tax payments
- Property taxes
- Tax certificates
- Broker statements
- Withholding certificates

Exact obligations should be confirmed annually with qualified professionals.

## Annual Review Questions

1. Have my income or expenses changed?
2. Has my emergency reserve changed?
3. Has my health situation changed?
4. Have family obligations changed?
5. Has my country of residence changed?
6. Has my tax residency changed?
7. Is my portfolio within allocation limits?
8. Is any institution overconcentrated?
9. Is any property overconcentrated?
10. Are all investments still understandable?
11. Have fees increased?
12. Have tax rules changed?
13. Have beneficiary designations been reviewed?
14. Is any investment no longer serving its purpose?
15. Should the Investment Policy Statement be updated?

## Sell or Exit Review

An investment may be reviewed for sale when:

- Original purpose no longer applies
- Strategy changed materially
- Fund closed or merged
- Fees became unreasonable
- Tax treatment became unfavorable
- Legal risk increased
- Concentration became excessive
- Better diversification is available
- Property no longer produces acceptable net income
- Required liquidity has changed

Do not sell solely because of temporary market declines unless the investment no longer fits the plan.

## Annual Portfolio Review Sequence

1. Update all balances
2. Record income and fees
3. Calculate allocation
4. Review emergency reserve
5. Review liquidity needs
6. Review currency exposure
7. Review tax documents
8. Review property performance
9. Review broker and institution risk
10. Compare with target allocation
11. Identify required rebalancing
12. Document decisions
13. Update the Investment Policy Statement
14. Schedule the next review

## Monitoring Rules

1. Review accounts monthly for fraud.
2. Review allocation quarterly.
3. Rebalance according to written rules.
4. Use contributions before selling where practical.
5. Track gross and net income separately.
6. Maintain tax records throughout the year.
7. Review fees annually.
8. Review beneficiary information annually.
9. Document every major change.
10. Avoid decisions driven by daily market noise.
