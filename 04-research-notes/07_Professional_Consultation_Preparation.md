# Lesson 28 — Preparing for Professional Financial, Tax, Legal, and Property Consultations

## Learning Objective

Learn how to prepare for consultations with financial, tax, legal, lending, brokerage, and real-estate professionals so advice is based on complete facts and documented clearly.

## Important Principle

Professional advice is only as reliable as the information supplied.

Incomplete facts can produce:

- Incorrect tax conclusions
- Unsuitable investment recommendations
- Missed legal risks
- Inaccurate mortgage estimates
- Incomplete property analysis
- Unexpected compliance costs
- Conflicting advice

Prepare a structured consultation packet before each meeting.

- US tax professional

- Colombian real-estate attorney

- Mortgage officer

## Professional Scope

Before the meeting, confirm:

- Professional name
- Firm
- Jurisdiction
- Credentials
- Experience
- Services provided
- Services excluded
- Fee structure
- Conflict-of-interest policy
- Whether advice will be provided in writing
- Whether follow-up questions are included

## Credential Verification

Verify credentials independently where practical.

Record:

- Credential
- Issuing authority
- License number
- Jurisdiction
- Status
- Expiration date
- Disciplinary history where available
- Official verification source
- Verification date

Do not rely solely on business cards, social-media profiles, or website claims.

## Engagement Letter

A written engagement should define:

- Client
- Professional
- Scope
- Deliverables
- Fees
- Billing method
- Deadlines
- Required records
- Confidentiality
- Conflicts
- Reliance limitations
- Termination
- Record retention
- Follow-up process

Do not assume a consultation creates a continuing advisory relationship.

## Consultation Objectives

Define the exact outcome needed.

Examples:

- Determine whether a foreign fund may create PFIC reporting
- Confirm Colombian tax residency
- Review FBAR and Form 8938 exposure
- Review foreign-assets reporting
- Review property title
- Review a promesa de compraventa
- Confirm auction participation requirements
- Evaluate mortgage terms
- Review estate-planning documents
- Review beneficiary structure
- Evaluate rental lease
- Review contractor agreement
- Confirm investment-account reporting

## One-Question Rule

Each major issue should be written as a clear question.

Weak question:

- What do you think about this investment?

Stronger questions:

- What annual US tax forms could this specific fund require?
- Does this legal entity create PFIC concerns?
- Which Colombian reporting obligations could apply to this account?
- What liens or restrictions remain on this matrícula?
- Which obligations survive this auction sale?
- Does this contract make the deposit refundable if financing fails?

## Facts Versus Questions

Separate:

### Facts supplied

Information believed to be accurate and supported by records.

### Assumptions

Information not yet verified.

### Questions

Issues requiring professional analysis.

### Decisions requested

Specific approval, rejection, or recommendation needed.

## Advice Classification

Classify every statement as:

### Confirmed professional advice

Advice clearly provided within the professional’s scope.

### Preliminary view

Initial opinion requiring more evidence.

### Administrative information

Process or filing information, not legal or tax advice.

### Personal decision

A choice made by the investor.

### Unresolved

Question not answered.

Do not treat informal comments as final written advice.

## Written Confirmation

For material matters, request written confirmation covering:

- Question
- Facts reviewed
- Assumptions
- Conclusion
- Limitations
- Required action
- Filing or transaction deadline
- Records to retain
- Need for future review

## Conflicting Advice

When professionals disagree:

1. Confirm that both received the same facts.
2. Identify different assumptions.
3. Confirm jurisdiction and scope.
4. Request authority or reasoning.
5. Ask whether the issue is uncertain.
6. Obtain a second specialist opinion where material.
7. Document the final decision.
8. Avoid choosing only the cheaper or preferred answer.

## Professional Independence

Identify how the professional is compensated.

Possible compensation includes:

- Hourly fee
- Flat fee
- Percentage of assets
- Commission
- Referral fee
- Product compensation
- Property commission
- Success fee

Compensation can create conflicts.

## Broker and Adviser Distinction

A broker may focus on:

- Trade execution
- Product access
- Account services

An investment adviser may provide:

- Portfolio advice
- Suitability analysis
- Ongoing management

The exact legal obligations depend on the jurisdiction and engagement.

Confirm the person’s actual role.

## Salesperson Versus Independent Reviewer

A person selling a product may provide useful information but is not automatically independent.

Examples include:

- Bank representative selling a CDT
- Broker promoting a fund
- Developer selling property
- Auction promoter
- Mortgage officer
- Insurance agent
- Contractor proposing work

Independent review may still be necessary.

- Filing fees

## Consultation Budget

Professional advice should be included in total investment cost.

Examples:

- PFIC analysis
- Form 8621 preparation
- Property title review
- Contract review
- Mortgage review
- Estate planning
- Engineering inspection
- Tax return amendment
- Legal possession procedure

A low-cost investment may become inefficient when compliance costs are high.

## Translation and Language

For cross-border matters, confirm:

- Required language
- Certified translation
- Bilingual document review
- Notarization
- Apostille
- Legalization
- Interpretation
- Which version controls

Do not rely on an informal translation for a material legal commitment.

## Secure Document Transfer

Use a professional-approved secure method.

Possible methods include:

- Encrypted client portal
- Encrypted file transfer
- Password-protected archive with separate password delivery
- Secure cloud folder
- Physical delivery

Avoid sending highly restricted documents through ordinary unencrypted email where safer options exist.

## Document Index

Create a list showing:

- Document name
- Date
- Source
- Pages
- Purpose
- Classification
- Provided to
- Delivery date
- Version

## Version Control

For each document, track:

- Draft
- Revised draft
- Reviewed version
- Signed version
- Filed version
- Registered version
- Superseded version

Do not confuse an unsigned draft with the final legal document.

## Decision Gate

A major transaction should not proceed until required professional conditions are met.

Possible gates include:

- Tax review complete
- Legal review complete
- Funding confirmed
- Title clear
- Contract approved
- Insurance confirmed
- Security controls complete
- Professional questions resolved
- Required permits confirmed
- Written opinion retained

## Green, Yellow, and Red Status

### Green

- Material facts verified
- Professional review complete
- Risks understood
- Required documents approved
- Transaction may proceed to the next internal step

### Yellow

- Some issues unresolved
- Additional evidence required
- No irreversible action permitted

### Red

- Material legal, tax, financial, title, identity, or fraud risk
- Transaction stopped

## Consultation Follow-Up

After the meeting:

1. Organize notes.
2. Separate advice from assumptions.
3. Save written confirmation.
4. Update decision log.
5. Update evidence register.
6. Assign actions.
7. Record deadlines.
8. Update transaction status.
9. Store confidential records securely.
10. Schedule follow-up if required.

For each question, record:

- Question

- Professional responsible

- Answer

- Deadline
- Status

## Stop Conditions

Do not proceed when:

- Professional credentials cannot be verified
- Scope is unclear
- Material conflicts are undisclosed
- Advice depends on missing facts
- Professional refuses written confirmation for a material issue
- Receiving account differs from engagement documents
- Pressure is applied to invest immediately
- Professional also receives undisclosed product compensation
- Legal or tax issue remains unresolved
- Final document differs from reviewed version
- Filing or payment deadline is not understood
- Confidential documents cannot be transferred securely

## Beginner Rules

1. Define the exact question before the meeting.
2. Supply complete and accurate facts.
3. Verify credentials independently.
4. Confirm professional scope.
5. Request written engagement terms.
6. Separate advice from assumptions.
7. Document conflicts of interest.
8. Include professional fees in total investment cost.
9. Use secure document transfer.
10. Do not proceed while material questions remain unresolved.
