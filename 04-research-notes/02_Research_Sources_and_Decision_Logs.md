# Lesson 12 — Research Sources, Evidence, and Decision Logs

## Learning Objective

Create a disciplined process for collecting evidence, checking source quality, separating facts from assumptions, and documenting investment decisions.

## Why Documentation Matters

Investment decisions can be distorted by:

- Recent performance
- Advertising
- Social media
- Sales pressure
- Incomplete data
- Confirmation bias
- AI-generated errors
- Memory gaps
- Changing market conditions

A written research process improves consistency and makes later review possible.

## Source Hierarchy

Use sources in this order whenever possible.

### Primary sources

Examples:

- Official prospectus
- Fund factsheet
- Issuer filings
- Audited financial statements
- Official regulator records
- Official tax guidance
- Court records
- Property title records
- Auction notices
- Broker fee schedules
- Official appraisals

### Secondary sources

Examples:

- Reputable financial publications
- Independent research firms
- Academic research
- Established broker research
- Professional legal or tax commentary

### Tertiary sources

Examples:

- Blogs
- Social media
- Video commentary
- Discussion forums
- AI-generated summaries

Tertiary sources may help identify questions, but they should not be the sole basis for a decision.

## Source Quality Questions

For every source, ask:

1. Who created it?
2. Is the author identifiable?
3. Is the source official or independent?
4. What date was it published?
5. Is the information current?
6. Does the source disclose conflicts of interest?
7. Does it cite evidence?
8. Is the information consistent with primary sources?
9. Is the source selling the product?
10. Can the claim be independently verified?

## Fact, Assumption, and Estimate

Each note should label information as one of the following.

### Fact

Supported directly by a reliable source.

Example:

- Expense ratio shown in the official prospectus

### Assumption

A condition used for analysis but not yet verified.

Example:

- Property possession will take six months

### Estimate

A calculated or professional judgment.

Example:

- Repair cost estimated at COP 35,000,000

Do not present assumptions or estimates as confirmed facts.

## Research Note Structure

Every research note should include:

- Product or property
- Date reviewed
- Objective
- Primary sources
- Secondary sources
- Key facts
- Assumptions
- Estimates
- Risks
- Missing information
- Tax questions
- Legal questions
- Decision status
- Review date

## Decision Status

Use one of these statuses:

- Research only
- Waiting for source
- Waiting for professional review
- Rejected
- Watchlist
- Approved for small test
- Approved
- Purchased
- Sold
- Closed

## Decision Log

For every purchase or rejection, record:

- Date
- Product
- Amount
- Purpose
- Alternatives considered
- Expected return
- Main risks
- Worst-case scenario
- Tax review
- Legal review
- Funding source
- Reason for decision
- Conditions for exit
- Next review date

## AI Research Rules

AI may be used to:

- Explain terminology
- Summarize official documents
- Generate comparison tables
- Identify missing questions
- Structure research notes
- Review logic
- Create checklists

AI should not be treated as the final authority for:

- Current prices
- Tax rules
- Legal status
- Regulatory registration
- Auction requirements
- Property title
- Investment suitability
- Guaranteed returns
- Trade execution

Always verify current facts through official or primary sources.

## Version Control

When a major research note changes, record:

- What changed
- Why it changed
- Which source caused the change
- Whether the investment decision changed

Git history can provide a useful audit trail.

## Review Frequency

Review research:

- Before purchase
- After major market changes
- After tax or legal changes
- After new financial statements
- After significant price movement
- Before increasing the position
- At least annually

## Beginner Rules

1. Prefer primary sources.
2. Date every source.
3. Label assumptions clearly.
4. Record missing information.
5. Document rejected investments.
6. Never rely on one source.
7. Recheck current prices and rules.
8. Use AI only as an assistant.
9. Save official documents.
10. Record why each decision was made.
