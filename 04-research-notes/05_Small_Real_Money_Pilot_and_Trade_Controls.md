# Lesson 18 — Small Real-Money Pilot and Trade Execution Controls

## Learning Objective

Learn how to conduct a small, controlled real-money investment test while limiting operational, execution, liquidity, cybersecurity, tax, and behavioral risk.

## Important Principle

The purpose of a first transaction is not to maximize profit.

The purpose is to verify that the complete process works:

- Account funding
- Currency conversion
- Order entry
- Trade execution
- Custody
- Statements
- Income reporting
- Tax records
- Withdrawal
- Security controls

A successful transaction is one that is correctly executed, documented, and reconciled.

## Preconditions

Do not begin a real-money pilot until:

- Emergency reserves are intact
- Essential expenses are covered
- High-cost debt has been reviewed
- Investment Policy Statement is complete
- Product purpose is documented
- Legal entity is verified
- Broker regulation is verified
- Custody structure is understood
- Fees are understood
- Tax questions are identified
- Currency exposure is intentional
- MFA is enabled
- Withdrawal process is understood
- The amount is financially insignificant if lost

## Pilot Amount Rule

The pilot amount should be small enough that:

- A total loss would not affect household finances
- A processing delay would not create hardship
- A reporting mistake could be corrected
- A withdrawal test could be completed
- Emotional reactions could be observed
- The process could be stopped without consequence

Do not increase the amount merely because the first transaction rises in value.

## One-Product Rule

The initial pilot should normally involve one understandable product.

Possible educational examples:

- One short-term CDT
- One broad, diversified ETF
- One regulated cash product

Avoid beginning with:

- Leveraged ETF
- Inverse ETF
- Options strategy
- Thematic fund
- Individual speculative stock
- Cryptocurrency
- Non-US pooled fund with unresolved PFIC risk
- Distressed property
- Automated trading bot
- Margin borrowing

## Account Verification

Confirm:

- Exact broker legal entity
- Account holder name
- Account number
- Account jurisdiction
- Custodian
- Base currency
- Funding method
- Withdrawal destination
- Beneficiary information
- Customer-support channels

## Security Controls

Before funding:

- Use a unique password
- Enable application-based MFA when available
- Store recovery codes securely
- Review authorized devices
- Enable login alerts
- Enable trade alerts
- Enable withdrawal alerts
- Disable unnecessary API access
- Disable API withdrawal permission
- Confirm official support contact information

## Funding Test

Begin with a small transfer.

Record:

- Transfer date
- Sending institution
- Receiving institution
- Amount sent
- Currency sent
- Exchange rate
- Transfer fee
- Amount received
- Processing time
- Reference number

Reconcile the amount received with the amount sent.

## Currency Conversion Test

When converting COP and USD, record:

- Market reference rate
- Broker conversion rate
- Conversion spread
- Fixed conversion fee
- Amount converted
- Net currency received

Calculate:

Currency conversion cost =
Expected amount at reference rate
minus actual amount received

## Order Types

## Market Order

A market order seeks immediate execution at the best available price.

Main risks:

- Price may move before execution
- Wide bid-ask spread
- Poor execution in a thin market
- Unexpected price during volatile periods

## Limit Order

A limit order specifies the maximum purchase price or minimum sale price.

Potential advantages:

- Greater price control
- Protection against unexpected execution

Potential disadvantages:

- Order may not execute
- Partial execution may occur
- Price may move away from the limit

A limit order controls price, not investment loss after purchase.

## Bid-Ask Spread

The bid is the highest current purchase offer.

The ask is the lowest current sale offer.

Approximate spread:

Bid-ask spread =
Ask price minus bid price

A wide spread increases transaction cost.

Record:

- Bid
- Ask
- Order price
- Execution price
- Spread
- Time of execution

## Order Review Before Submission

Verify:

- Correct account
- Correct ticker
- Correct exchange
- Buy versus sell
- Quantity
- Order type
- Limit price
- Estimated total
- Currency
- Estimated commission
- Estimated conversion cost
- Order duration

Read the complete confirmation screen before submitting.

## Trade Confirmation

After execution, retain:

- Execution date
- Settlement date
- Security name
- Ticker
- Quantity
- Execution price
- Commission
- Fees
- Total cost
- Currency
- Exchange
- Order number
- Trade confirmation

Do not rely only on the mobile-app display.

## Settlement

Settlement is the process by which cash and securities are formally exchanged.

Before withdrawing or transferring recently traded funds, confirm:

- Settlement date
- Availability of proceeds
- Withdrawal hold
- Transfer restriction
- Currency-conversion timing

A completed trade display does not always mean funds are immediately withdrawable.

## Cost-Basis Record

Record independently:

- Purchase date
- Settlement date
- Quantity
- Unit price
- Commission
- Currency-conversion cost
- Total original-currency cost
- USD tax basis
- COP tax basis
- Exchange-rate source

Do not assume the broker will produce the required basis for both countries.

## Position Monitoring

During the pilot, monitor:

- Account access
- Trade accuracy
- Market value
- Fees
- Statements
- Dividend or interest entries
- Tax withholding
- Currency effects
- Unauthorized activity

Do not monitor solely to react to normal short-term price movements.

## Withdrawal Test

After the account and transaction are operating correctly, conduct a small withdrawal test where practical.

Record:

- Withdrawal request date
- Amount
- Currency
- Fee
- Destination account
- Approval steps
- Processing time
- Amount received
- Exchange rate
- Problems encountered

A platform should not be trusted with a large amount until withdrawal procedures are understood.

## Reconciliation

Reconcile:

### Funding

Amount sent versus amount credited

### Trade

Expected cost versus executed cost

### Holdings

Quantity purchased versus quantity held

### Cash

Expected cash balance versus displayed balance

### Withdrawal

Amount requested versus amount received

### Documents

Transaction history versus official statement

## Stop Conditions

Stop the pilot immediately when:

- Account identity is inconsistent
- Receiving account differs from the verified entity
- Unexpected fees appear
- Withdrawal is blocked without a valid reason
- Password or MFA code is requested
- Remote computer access is requested
- Regulatory status cannot be confirmed
- Trade executed in the wrong security
- Account activity is unauthorized
- Tax classification differs from expectations
- The platform pressures additional deposits
- Customer support cannot explain material discrepancies

## No Automatic Scaling

Do not increase the investment merely because:

- The position rises
- A salesperson recommends more
- A promotional rate is expiring
- Friends report gains
- Social media sentiment is positive
- The first transaction completed successfully

Scaling requires a separate written decision.

## Scaling Review

Before increasing the amount, confirm:

- Pilot completed without material problems
- Withdrawal test completed
- Statements reconciled
- Fees matched expectations
- Tax records are usable
- Product remains suitable
- Portfolio allocation remains within limits
- Emergency reserve remains intact
- Currency exposure remains acceptable
- Decision log is complete

## CDT Pilot Variation

For a CDT pilot, verify:

- Issuing institution
- Effective annual rate
- Opening date
- Maturity date
- Renewal instructions
- Withholding treatment
- Early-withdrawal restrictions
- Deposit-insurance review
- Maturity-payment destination

The first CDT should not lock up money needed for household expenses.

## ETF Pilot Variation

For an ETF pilot, verify:

- Exact fund domicile
- Correct ticker
- Correct exchange
- Index or strategy
- Expense ratio
- Bid-ask spread
- Order type
- Distribution policy
- Tax classification
- Brokerage account reporting

## Property Exclusion

Distressed property should not be treated as a small operational pilot.

It requires a separate process involving:

- Legal review
- Title review
- Possession analysis
- Inspection
- Financing verification
- Maximum-bid calculation
- Large liquidity commitment

## Final Pilot Decision

Select one:

- Continue observing
- Complete another small test
- Approve limited increase
- Seek tax advice
- Seek legal advice
- Change broker
- Reject product
- Close account
- Take no further action

## Beginner Rules

1. Start with one understandable product.
2. Keep the first amount small.
3. Verify every account and ticker.
4. Use strong MFA.
5. Record all conversion and transaction costs.
6. Retain the official trade confirmation.
7. Maintain independent cost-basis records.
8. Test withdrawal procedures.
9. Do not scale automatically.
10. Stop when any material discrepancy appears.
