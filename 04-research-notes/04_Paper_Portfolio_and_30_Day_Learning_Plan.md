# Lesson 17 — Paper Portfolio and 30-Day Investment Learning Plan

## Learning Objective

Practice researching, selecting, monitoring, and reviewing investments without risking real money.

## What Is a Paper Portfolio?

A paper portfolio is a simulated portfolio.

It records hypothetical:

- Purchases
- Sale prices
- Contributions
- Dividends
- Interest
- Fees
- Currency conversions
- Property costs
- Portfolio returns

No real transaction is executed.

## Why Start With Simulation?

A paper portfolio allows the investor to practice:

- Reading ETF documents
- Comparing CDTs
- Calculating total costs
- Tracking currency exposure
- Monitoring allocation
- Applying rebalancing rules
- Testing emotional reactions
- Evaluating distressed property
- Maintaining tax records
- Documenting decisions

Simulation cannot reproduce every real-world factor, but it can reveal weaknesses in the decision process.

## Limits of Paper Investing

A simulation does not fully reproduce:

- Emotional pressure
- Real losses
- Bid-ask spreads
- Order execution
- Taxes
- Withdrawal restrictions
- Legal delays
- Property damage
- Broker problems
- Currency-conversion timing

Paper results should not be treated as proof that a strategy will succeed.

## Paper Portfolio Rules

1. Choose a fixed starting date.
2. Choose a fixed hypothetical amount.
3. Use actual market prices from that date.
4. Record all assumed fees.
5. Record currency-conversion costs.
6. Do not change historical entry prices.
7. Document every hypothetical purchase.
8. Apply the Investment Policy Statement.
9. Review according to a fixed schedule.
10. Do not rewrite assumptions after outcomes are known.

## Suggested Educational Portfolio Categories

A simulated portfolio may include:

- Emergency cash
- Short-term CDT
- Broad equity ETF
- Bond ETF
- Colombian investment
- Direct-property reserve
- Distressed-property opportunity
- Uninvested cash

This is an educational framework, not a recommended allocation.

## Starting Capital

Choose one hypothetical starting amount.

Examples:

- COP 10,000,000
- COP 50,000,000
- COP 100,000,000
- USD 10,000

Use only one base reporting currency for the main portfolio dashboard.

Record foreign-currency values separately.

## Hypothetical Purchase Record

For every simulated purchase, record:

- Date
- Product
- Ticker or identifier
- Asset category
- Quantity
- Unit price
- Currency
- Exchange rate
- Gross cost
- Commission
- Currency-conversion fee
- Total cost
- Investment purpose
- Expected holding period
- Primary source
- Main risk
- Exit condition

## Market Price Rule

Use the same pricing convention consistently.

Possible conventions include:

- Official closing price
- Published daily net asset value
- Broker quoted price at a fixed time
- Official CDT quotation
- Independent property estimate

Do not select whichever price makes the result look best.

## ETF Simulation

For each hypothetical ETF:

1. Confirm legal fund name.
2. Confirm fund domicile.
3. Read the official factsheet.
4. Read the prospectus summary.
5. Record expense ratio.
6. Record index or strategy.
7. Record number of holdings.
8. Record concentration.
9. Record distribution policy.
10. Record tax questions.

## CDT Simulation

For a hypothetical CDT, record:

- Institution
- Principal
- Term
- Effective annual rate
- Opening date
- Maturity date
- Expected gross interest
- Expected withholding
- Expected net value
- Early-withdrawal rules
- Deposit-protection review

At maturity, record whether the simulated funds would be:

- Reinvested
- Spent
- Moved to another product
- Added to the portfolio cash balance

## Distressed Property Simulation

Do not use only the advertised auction price.

Record:

- Appraised value
- Minimum bid
- Hypothetical winning bid
- Deposit
- Legal costs
- Registration costs
- Outstanding debts
- Repairs
- Contingency
- Possession delay
- Carrying costs
- Expected rent
- Expected resale value
- Worst-case outcome

The simulated purchase should be rejected if required documentation is missing.

## Dividends and Interest

Record income when it would actually be received.

For each payment, record:

- Gross amount
- Tax withheld
- Net amount
- Currency
- Exchange rate
- Payment date
- Reinvestment decision

Do not count projected dividends as received income.

## Fees

Include realistic estimates for:

- ETF expense ratio
- Broker commission
- Bid-ask spread
- Currency conversion
- Account fee
- Withdrawal fee
- Tax preparation
- Property legal fees
- Property administration
- Property maintenance
- CDT withholding

Ignoring costs makes the paper result unreliable.

## Currency Tracking

For each foreign-currency investment, record:

- Original currency
- Entry exchange rate
- Current exchange rate
- Value in original currency
- Value in COP
- Currency gain or loss
- Investment gain or loss

Separate asset performance from currency effects.

## Performance Tracking

Track:

- Starting value
- Contributions
- Withdrawals
- Investment income
- Fees
- Ending value
- Investment return
- Currency effect
- Total return

A higher ending balance may result from contributions rather than investment performance.

## Monthly Review

At the end of each month:

1. Update market values.
2. Record income.
3. Record fees.
4. Update exchange rates.
5. Review allocation.
6. Review unresolved tax questions.
7. Review risk limits.
8. Review investment thesis.
9. Record mistakes.
10. Take no corrective action without documenting the reason.

## Decision Review

For every paper investment, ask:

- Was the original purpose clear?
- Were primary sources used?
- Were risks correctly identified?
- Were fees underestimated?
- Was currency exposure understood?
- Did recent performance influence the decision?
- Would I still make the same decision?
- What evidence would change the decision?

## 30-Day Learning Plan

## Days 1–3 — Financial Foundation

Complete:

- Personal Investment Policy Statement
- Monthly essential-expense estimate
- Emergency-reserve target
- COP and USD obligations
- Maximum acceptable drawdown

Do not select investments yet.

## Days 4–6 — ETF Fundamentals

Review:

- ETF structure
- Expense ratios
- Bid-ask spreads
- Tracking difference
- Fund domicile
- Index construction
- Distributions

Complete one ETF research template.

## Days 7–9 — Colombia Investments

Review:

- Savings accounts
- CDTs
- Government bonds
- Collective investment funds
- Colombian stocks
- Mercado Global Colombiano

Create a hypothetical CDT ladder.

## Days 10–12 — Broker Safety

Review:

- Legal entities
- Regulation
- Custody
- Funding
- Withdrawals
- MFA
- Complete fee schedules

Compare at least two hypothetical brokerage options.

## Days 13–15 — Cross-Border Tax

Create an account and asset inventory.

Identify questions involving:

- FBAR
- Form 8938
- PFIC
- Form 8621
- Colombian foreign-assets reporting
- Foreign tax credits
- Cost basis
- Currency conversion

Do not make tax conclusions without professional review.

## Days 16–18 — ETF Comparison

Research four educational candidates:

- Broad US equity ETF
- Global equity ETF
- Bond ETF
- Dividend ETF

Complete:

- Detailed screening
- Weighted scoring
- Overlap review
- Domicile verification

## Days 19–21 — Property Analysis

Select one publicly documented hypothetical property opportunity.

Complete:

- Title checklist
- Occupancy assessment
- Repair assumptions
- Total project cost
- Best-case scenario
- Base-case scenario
- Worst-case scenario
- Maximum-bid estimate

Do not submit a real bid.

## Days 22–24 — Portfolio Construction

Build a paper portfolio using:

- Cash
- Fixed income
- Broad ETF exposure
- Colombian exposure
- Property reserve

Check:

- Liquidity
- Currency exposure
- Concentration
- Time horizon
- Risk capacity

## Days 25–27 — Stress Testing

Model:

- 30% equity decline
- COP weakening
- COP strengthening
- Lower CDT renewal rates
- Twelve-month property delay
- Major unexpected expense

Record whether the portfolio remains financially viable.

## Days 28–29 — Fraud and Safety Review

Complete:

- Broker verification checklist
- Scam-risk scorecard
- Account-security review
- Family protection rules
- Stop-transaction procedure

## Day 30 — Final Review

Complete:

- Portfolio dashboard
- Decision log
- Evidence register
- Annual-review template
- List of unresolved questions
- Professional-review requirements

Decide whether the next stage should be:

- More learning
- Professional tax consultation
- Broker verification
- Small real-money test
- No action

## Readiness Criteria

Before considering real money, confirm:

- Emergency reserve is defined
- No high-pressure debt problem is being ignored
- Investment Policy Statement is complete
- Product purpose is clear
- Legal entity is verified
- Fees are understood
- Tax questions are identified
- Currency exposure is intentional
- Worst-case scenario is affordable
- Decision is documented
- No guaranteed return is expected
- No emergency money is being invested

## Small-Test Principle

The first real transaction, when appropriate, should be small enough that:

- A mistake would not harm household finances
- Withdrawal procedures can be tested
- Reporting can be practiced
- Fees can be observed
- Emotional reactions can be evaluated
- The process can be corrected

A small test is not authorization to skip due diligence.

3. Which category remains unclear?
4. Which tax questions require professional advice?
5. Which investment risks would cause the most stress?
6. How did currency affect the paper portfolio?
7. Did I chase recent performance?
8. Did I underestimate fees?
9. Did I maintain enough liquidity?
10. Am I prepared to take no action when evidence is insufficient?

## Beginner Rules

1. Practice before investing.
2. Use real source data in the simulation.
3. Include all fees.
4. Separate investment returns from currency effects.
5. Do not rewrite assumptions after results are known.
6. Reject opportunities with missing evidence.
7. Follow the Investment Policy Statement.
8. Keep emergency funds outside the simulation.
9. Begin real investing only with a controlled amount.
10. Treat no-investment as a valid decision.
