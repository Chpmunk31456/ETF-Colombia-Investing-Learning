# Lesson 11 — Personal Investment Policy Statement

## Learning Objective

Create a written framework for making investment decisions consistently, rather than reacting to market headlines, sales pressure, fear, or recent performance.

## What Is an Investment Policy Statement?

An Investment Policy Statement, or IPS, is a written document that defines:

- Investment objectives
- Time horizon
- Liquidity needs
- Risk limits
- Permitted investments
- Prohibited investments
- Currency exposure
- Rebalancing rules
- Decision-making process
- Review schedule

An IPS does not guarantee investment success.

Its purpose is to improve discipline, consistency, and accountability.

## Time Horizons

Separate money by purpose.

### Immediate needs

Time horizon:

- Today through 12 months

Possible uses:

- Housing
- Food
- Utilities
- Medical care
- Transportation
- Insurance
- Emergency expenses

Priority:

- Liquidity
- Stability
- Accessibility

### Short-term needs

Time horizon:

- 1 to 3 years

Possible uses:

- Vehicle purchase
- Property down payment
- Major repairs
- Travel
- Education
- Legal or immigration costs

Priority:

- Capital preservation
- Known maturities
- Limited volatility

### Medium-term goals

Time horizon:

- 3 to 7 years

Possible uses:

- Property acquisition
- Business investment
- Family support
- Larger planned expenses

Priority:

- Balanced growth
- Controlled risk
- Reasonable liquidity

### Long-term goals

Time horizon:

- More than 7 years

Possible uses:

- Retirement support
- Legacy planning
- Long-term growth
- Inflation protection

Priority:

- Diversification
- Sustainable growth
- Risk control

## Emergency Reserve Policy

Define:

- Minimum reserve in months of essential expenses
- Currency of the reserve
- Institutions holding the reserve
- Maximum amount held with one institution
- Conditions under which the reserve may be used
- Conditions requiring replenishment

Emergency reserves should not depend on selling volatile investments.

## Liquidity Policy

Define the minimum amount that must remain accessible within:

- One day
- Seven days
- Thirty days
- Ninety days
- One year

Distressed property, long-term CDTs, and illiquid funds should not be counted as immediately available liquidity.

## Risk Capacity

Risk capacity depends on:

- Stable income
- Pension income
- Health expenses
- Dependents
- Debt
- Emergency reserve
- Investment horizon
- Need for withdrawals
- Ability to replace losses

Document the maximum temporary portfolio decline that would be financially tolerable.

## Risk Tolerance

Risk tolerance is the emotional ability to remain invested during losses.

Complete:

- A 10% decline would make me:
- A 20% decline would make me:
- A 30% decline would make me:
- A 40% decline would make me:

The allocation should reflect both financial capacity and emotional tolerance.

## Permitted Investments

Possible permitted categories:

- Insured or regulated savings accounts
- CDTs
- Colombian government fixed income
- Investment-grade bond funds
- Broad diversified ETFs
- Selected dividend ETFs
- Colombian equities
- Real-estate investment vehicles
- Direct residential property
- Distressed property after legal review
- Cash equivalents

Each permitted category should have a maximum allocation.

## Prohibited Investments

Possible prohibited categories:

- Leveraged ETFs
- Inverse ETFs
- Margin borrowing
- Uncovered options
- Binary options
- Unregistered investment platforms
- Guaranteed-return schemes
- Cryptocurrency transfers to unknown parties
- Automated trading bots with withdrawal access
- Investments not understood
- Non-US pooled funds without PFIC review
- Distressed property without title review
- Property purchased with emergency reserves

## Distressed Property Allocation Rule

Distressed property should have a separate maximum because it is:

- Illiquid
- Concentrated
- Legally complex
- Repair-dependent
- Time-intensive
- Difficult to value precisely

Possible rules:

- Maximum percentage of total investable assets
- Maximum cash committed to one property
- Minimum emergency reserve remaining after purchase
- Minimum legal review requirements
- Minimum repair contingency
- Maximum acceptable possession delay
- Required margin of safety
- Required second exit strategy

## Currency Policy

Define target exposure to:

- Colombian pesos
- US dollars
- Other currencies

Consider:

- Currency of income
- Currency of household expenses
- Future vehicle or property purchases
- Medical expenses
- Travel
- Tax payments
- Long-term purchasing power

Currency exposure should support real obligations rather than speculation.

## Institution Concentration Policy

Set maximum limits for:

- One bank
- One broker
- One fund manager
- One property
- One country
- One currency
- One ETF issuer

Institutional diversification can reduce operational and custody risk.

## ETF Selection Rules

Before purchasing an ETF:

- Legal domicile confirmed
- Index or strategy understood
- Expense ratio reviewed
- Bid-ask spread reviewed
- Assets under management reviewed
- Holdings and concentration reviewed
- Currency exposure reviewed
- Tax treatment reviewed
- PFIC risk reviewed when relevant
- Official prospectus retained
- Reason for purchase documented

## CDT Selection Rules

Before opening a CDT:

- Institution verified
- Effective annual rate confirmed
- Term confirmed
- Early-withdrawal rules confirmed
- Deposit protection reviewed
- Withholding tax considered
- Inflation-adjusted return estimated
- Maturity matches a planned need
- Concentration limit respected

## Property Selection Rules

Before purchasing property:

- Title reviewed
- Possession confirmed
- Independent appraisal obtained
- Property inspected where possible
- Repair estimate obtained
- Contingency included
- Taxes and administration debts reviewed
- Financing confirmed
- Worst-case scenario affordable
- Exit strategies documented
- Attorney approval obtained

## Rebalancing Policy

Rebalancing means restoring the portfolio toward target allocations.

Possible rules:

- Review every six months
- Rebalance annually
- Rebalance when an asset class deviates by more than a defined percentage
- Use new contributions before selling
- Consider tax and transaction costs
- Do not rebalance based solely on headlines

## Contribution Policy

Define:

- Monthly contribution amount
- Currency
- Funding source
- Priority order
- Conditions for pausing contributions
- Conditions for increasing contributions

Example priority:

1. Emergency reserve
2. High-interest debt
3. Short-term obligations
4. Diversified long-term investments
5. Specialized or illiquid investments

## Withdrawal Policy

Define when investment withdrawals are permitted.

Possible permitted reasons:

- Medical emergency
- Essential household expense
- Planned property purchase
- Required tax payment
- Major family emergency

Avoid selling long-term investments for discretionary spending during market declines.

## Decision Documentation

Every investment decision should record:

- Date
- Product
- Amount
- Purpose
- Expected holding period
- Risks
- Fees
- Tax questions
- Currency exposure
- Alternatives considered
- Reasons for purchase
- Conditions for selling
- Official sources

## Review Schedule

Review the IPS:

- Annually
- After a major income change
- After retirement or employment change
- After a major health event
- After moving countries
- After a large purchase
- After a major tax-law change
- After adding a new property
- After a significant change in household obligations

## Behavior Rules

1. Do not invest under time pressure.
2. Do not purchase based on fear of missing out.
3. Do not sell solely because of short-term market declines.
4. Do not chase recent performance.
5. Do not increase risk to recover prior losses.
6. Do not use emergency funds for speculative investments.
7. Do not make large decisions without written analysis.
8. Do not accept guaranteed-return claims.
9. Do not allow AI to make final investment decisions.
10. Review the IPS before every major purchase.
